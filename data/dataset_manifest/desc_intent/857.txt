Specify a TLA+ model of the classic prisoner lamp puzzle. There are N prisoners, each held in solitary confinement. A warden repeatedly selects any prisoner and places them alone in a special cell containing a single lamp that can be either on or off. After visiting, the prisoner is returned to their cell. Prisoners may communicate and agree on a strategy before the game begins but cannot communicate afterward.

At any point, a prisoner may announce to the warden that all prisoners have visited the cell at least once. If correct, all are freed; if wrong, they remain imprisoned forever.

The system should model two variants controlled by a configuration flag: one where the lamp's initial state is known to be off, and one where it is unknown. In the known-off variant one designated prisoner acts as a counter, turning the lamp off and incrementing a count whenever they find it on, declaring victory at count N. Non-counter prisoners turn the lamp on at most once when they find it off. In the unknown variant, non-counter prisoners may signal twice and the counter's threshold rises to 2N−1.

The warden must be fair, eventually selecting every prisoner so progress is possible. The specification must guarantee two properties: safety, meaning victory is never declared unless every prisoner has truly visited the cell, and liveness, meaning the prisoners eventually win.
