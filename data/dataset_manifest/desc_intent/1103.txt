Specify a TLA+ model of the Non-Blocking Atomic Commitment (NBAC) protocol for a distributed system of N processes that may crash, following Raynal's 1997 formulation. Each process begins with an initial vote of either YES or NO. Processes broadcast their vote and then decide to either COMMIT or ABORT based on what they receive and what a local failure detector reports. A process commits only if it has received YES votes from all processes and its failure detector reports no crashes; it aborts if it detects a crash or receives any NO vote. Processes may crash at any time, permanently. The failure detector at each process may nondeterministically report crashed or all-correct at any step. Message delivery is reliable but asynchronous: a process's received set grows monotonically as a subset of all sent messages.

The specification must enforce the following correctness properties: type safety across all variables, and validity — meaning that if any process commits, then all processes must have initially voted YES (equivalently, if any process voted NO, no process may commit). The system should be specified with weak fairness so that non-crashed processes that can vote or decide will eventually do so.
