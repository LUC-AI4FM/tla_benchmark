Specify the Dining Philosophers problem using the Chandy-Misra solution. There are N philosophers sitting in a circle, each needing two forks (shared with immediate neighbors) to eat. Unlike Dijkstra's original formulation, philosophers may directly hand forks to one another. Each fork has an owner and a cleanliness state: forks start dirty, and after eating a philosopher must clean a dirty fork before passing it to a requesting neighbor. Initially every fork is held dirty by the lower-numbered of its two adjacent philosophers. Each philosopher cycles between hungry and thinking states, passing dirty forks to neighbors when requested, and eating only when holding both adjacent forks in a clean state; after eating, both forks become dirty. All philosophers must be modeled as fair processes that never permanently stop. The specification must satisfy two safety invariants: type correctness of all state variables, and mutual exclusion meaning no two philosophers who share a fork may eat simultaneously. The key liveness property is that no philosopher ever starves — every philosopher must eat infinitely often, i.e., every hungry philosopher eventually gets to eat regardless of how other philosophers behave.
