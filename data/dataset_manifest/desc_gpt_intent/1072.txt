Specify a TLA+ specification for Dijkstra–Scholten/EWD998-style distributed termination detection in an asynchronous finite ring whose processes are identified by arbitrary identifiers, not natural-number indices. One distinguished but arbitrary process initiates detection. Processes may become active by receiving application messages, may send application messages only while active, and may later become passive. Application messages travel through per-process asynchronous inboxes and should include the sender identity and a logical/vector-clock timestamp for modeling causality, though clocks are not part of the termination-detection correctness argument.

Model a single circulating detection token over the identifier ring. Each process maintains enough local detection state to account for application messages it has sent and received and to record whether it has observed activity relevant to the current detection round. The token accumulates this information as it passes passive processes and can be restarted by the initiator when a round is inconsistent.

The specification must guarantee no false termination detection: detection implies all processes are passive and no application messages are in transit. It must also guarantee eventual detection once the computation has terminated, with detection within at most three full token rounds after termination.
