Specify the classic Dining Philosophers problem for a finite ring of N philosophers and N shared forks. Each philosopher repeatedly tries to acquire the two adjacent forks, eat, and then release both forks. Model the forks as exclusive resources, initially all available. To avoid circular deadlock, use an asymmetric acquisition discipline: one distinguished philosopher acquires the two neighboring forks in the opposite order from all the others.

The specification should allow concurrent interleavings of philosopher actions, with a philosopher able to take a fork only when it is available and obliged to release any forks held after eating. Include fairness assumptions strong enough that continuously or repeatedly enabled philosopher behavior is eventually scheduled.

The key safety property is mutual exclusion for adjacent philosophers: no two neighboring philosophers may be eating at the same time, including the wraparound pair in the ring. The intended liveness property is starvation freedom: every philosopher should eat infinitely often under the fairness assumptions.
