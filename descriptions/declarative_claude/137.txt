The module specifies a TCP-like three-way handshake protocol for connection establishment between an initiator and a receiver. It models the exchange of SYN, SYN-ACK, and ACK messages over a shared, ever-growing message set.

The state is captured by five variables: `msgs`, a set of records with boolean `syn`/`ack` flags and integer `seqno`/`ackno` fields representing all messages ever sent; `iseqno` and `rseqno`, the sequence numbers of the initiator and receiver respectively; and `istate` and `rstate`, the control states of each party. Initially the message set is empty, both sequence numbers are zero, the initiator is in `"INIT"`, and the receiver is in `"LISTEN"`.

The four actions follow the standard handshake phases. `SendSyn` transitions the initiator from `"INIT"` to `"SYN-SENT"` by adding a SYN message with a nondeterministically chosen sequence number. `SendSynAck` fires when the receiver is in `"LISTEN"` and a SYN message exists; it adds a SYN-ACK reply and moves to `"SYN-RECEIVED"`. `SendAck` fires when the initiator is in `"SYN-SENT"` and a matching SYN-ACK is present; it sends a final ACK and advances to `"ESTABLISHED"`. `RcvAck` fires when the receiver is in `"SYN-RECEIVED"` and a matching ACK message exists, advancing it to `"ESTABLISHED"`. The module checks the safety invariant `Inv`, which asserts that the receiver cannot reach `"ESTABLISHED"` unless the initiator has also reached `"ESTABLISHED"`, enforcing the expected ordering of connection establishment.
