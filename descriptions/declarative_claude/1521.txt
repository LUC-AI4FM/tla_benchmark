The module specifies the Coffee Can problem, originally attributed to Dijkstra and Scholten and popularized by David Gries. A coffee can holds some number of black and white beans, bounded by the constant `MaxBeanCount` (set to 100 in the model-checking configuration). The single variable `can` is a record tracking the current counts of black and white beans.

The system is initialized with any combination of black and white beans summing to between 1 and `MaxBeanCount`. Each step applies one of three bean-removal rules: picking two black beans removes both and adds one black bean back (net loss of one black bean); picking two white beans removes both and adds one black bean (net loss of two white, gain of one black); picking one of each color discards the black bean and returns the white one (net loss of one black bean). A `Termination` stuttering action fires when exactly one bean remains. The temporal specification `Spec` combines the initial condition, the next-state relation allowing any of these actions or stuttering, and weak fairness over `Next` to ensure the process does not stall indefinitely.

The module checks four properties: `TypeInvariant` confirms the can state stays within bounds; `MonotonicDecrease` asserts each non-stuttering step strictly reduces total bean count; `LoopInvariant` asserts the parity of the white bean count is preserved across every step; and `TerminationHypothesis` asserts that the process inevitably ends with a single black bean if the initial white bean count is even, or a single white bean if it is odd.
