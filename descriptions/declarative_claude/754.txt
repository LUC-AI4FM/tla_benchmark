This module specifies the Coffee Can problem, a classic puzzle attributed to Scholten via Dijkstra and Gries. A can contains black and white beans. Repeatedly, two beans are selected: if both are the same color, they are discarded and a black bean is added; if different colors, the white bean returns and the black is discarded. This continues until one bean remains.

The constant MaxBeanCount bounds the initial bean counts for finite model checking. The variable can is a record tracking the current number of black and white beans.

The initial state allows any combination of beans totaling between one and MaxBeanCount. Three actions model the selection process: picking two black beans removes one black, picking two white beans removes two white and adds one black, and picking different colors removes one black. A termination action allows stuttering when exactly one bean remains, preventing false deadlock detection.

The specification includes weak fairness on the next-state relation, ensuring progress cannot stall indefinitely.

Several properties are checked: a type invariant ensures valid bean counts; monotonic decrease confirms each non-stuttering step reduces total beans; eventual termination guarantees reaching one bean; a loop invariant preserves white bean count parity; and the termination hypothesis states the final bean is black if white beans started even, otherwise white.
