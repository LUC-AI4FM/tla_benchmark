This TLA+ module provides a mathematical specification for solving ordinary differential equations through integration. It defines foundational concepts from real analysis rather than modeling a distributed system or algorithm.

The module introduces local definitions for positive reals, open intervals, and epsilon-neighborhoods. The core mathematical machinery consists of two predicates: IsFirstDeriv defines when one function is the first derivative of another using the epsilon-delta limit definition—for every point in the domain and every positive epsilon, there exists a positive delta such that the difference quotient lies within epsilon of the derivative value. IsDeriv generalizes this recursively to characterize nth-order derivatives.

The main operator, Integrate, solves an implicit differential equation given a domain interval from a to b and a sequence of initial values. It constructs a family of functions where each successive function is the derivative of the previous one, the initial conditions are satisfied at point a, and the differential equation D (expressed implicitly as equal to zero) holds throughout an extended neighborhood of the interval. The operator returns the values of these functions evaluated at endpoint b.

This specification serves as a declarative mathematical definition of integration for differential equations rather than an executable algorithm, expressing the existence of solutions satisfying classical continuity and differentiability conditions from analysis.
