The module models the Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) process at an abstract level, tracking the cycling of temperature and the transformation of biological molecules across the three thermal stages of PCR: denaturation, annealing, and extension.

The model has two constants, `DNA` and `PRIMER`, representing the initial quantities of double-stranded DNA and primer molecules. Five variables are tracked: `tee` (the current temperature, one of `"Hot"`, `"TooHot"`, or `"Warm"`), and integer counts of `primer`, `dna` (double-stranded), `template` (single-stranded), and `hybrid` (template-primer hybrids). The system begins with all DNA double-stranded, no templates or hybrids, and the full primer stock available.

Four actions drive the system. `heat` transitions from `"Hot"` to `"TooHot"`, denaturing all double-stranded DNA and hybrids into single-stranded templates and recovering primers from hybrids. `cool` returns temperature from `"TooHot"` to `"Hot"` with no molecular change. `anneal` transitions from `"Hot"` to `"Warm"`, consuming some nondeterministically chosen number of primers and templates to form new hybrids. `extend` transitions from `"Warm"` to `"Hot"`, converting all hybrids into new double-stranded DNA.

The specification checks a `TypeOK` type invariant, a redundant non-negativity invariant on primers, and a conservation invariant asserting that `template + primer + 2*(dna + hybrid)` always equals `PRIMER + 2*DNA`. This conservation law is also expressed as a stuttering-tolerant safety property. A liveness property asserting eventual primer depletion is included but noted as not holding.
