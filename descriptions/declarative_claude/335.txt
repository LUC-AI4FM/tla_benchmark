The module models a simplified registration system that tracks a person's birth year and whether they have been issued a driver's license, illustrating the Y2K (Year 2000) problem caused by two-digit year storage.

Two constants parameterize the model: `BIRTH_YEAR`, the person's birth year stored as a two-digit integer (0–99), and `LICENSE_AGE`, the minimum age required to obtain a license (1–99). The two variables are `year`, representing the current year as a two-digit integer, and `hasLicense`, a Boolean indicating whether a license has been issued. Initially, `year` equals `BIRTH_YEAR` and no license has been issued. The `NewYear` action advances the year by incrementing it modulo 100, faithfully reproducing the two-digit overflow bug. The `IssueLicense` action grants a license whenever the computed age (`year - BIRTH_YEAR`) is at least `LICENSE_AGE`. Because the year wraps around modulo 100, `Age` can become negative or artificially small after the wraparound, allowing the system to issue a license prematurely or causing a previously valid license to appear invalid.

The module defines two invariants. `Inv` is a type-correctness invariant asserting that `year` stays within 0–99 and `hasLicense` is a Boolean. `Safety` expresses the intended property that a license holder must currently satisfy the minimum age requirement; this invariant is explicitly noted as being violated due to the year wraparound bug.
