This TLA+ module provides a formal TLAPS proof that the type-correctness invariant holds for a specification called "stages" (defined in a separate module that this one extends).

The module proves the theorem that the specification implies the type-correctness invariant always holds (Spec implies always TypeOK). This is a safety property establishing that all reachable states satisfy the type invariant.

The proof assumes that two constants, DNA and PRIMER, are natural numbers. It also includes a helper lemma establishing that the natMin function (minimum of two naturals) returns a natural number.

The main theorem proceeds by induction: first showing that the initial state satisfies TypeOK, then showing that TypeOK is preserved by every possible transition. The inductive step considers five cases corresponding to the system's actions: heat, cool, anneal, extend, and stuttering steps (unchanged variables). The anneal case requires the most detailed reasoning, involving arithmetic properties to show that subtracting some quantity k from primer and template while adding k to hybrid preserves the natural number type for all three variables.

The proof uses temporal logic (PTL) to lift the initialization and induction results into the final temporal property. The specification models some kind of staged process with primer, template, and hybrid quantities—likely a DNA replication or PCR-like biochemical process.
