Specify a formal model of a binary predicate checker over two finite sets that can be instantiated with different predicates and sets. The model should describe a system that, given two finite nonempty sets X and Y and a binary relation R ⊆ X × Y (the predicate may be equality, inequality, or any other decidable relation), determines whether there exists at least one pair (x ∈ X, y ∈ Y) that satisfies R. The specification must support multiple independent instantiations with different choices of X, Y, and R, and must allow composing assertions about whether a satisfying pair exists for each instantiation.

The model must capture the following requirements:
- For any instantiation, the system computes a boolean outcome GOOD_PAIR_EXISTS that is true iff ∃x ∈ X, ∃y ∈ Y: R(x,y).
- It must be possible to assert that some instantiations produce true and others produce false, reflecting different set/predicate combinations.
- The model must treat X, Y, and R as immutable constants for a given instantiation (i.e., the check is purely declarative, not dynamic).
- The specification must enable checking logical combinations of outcomes across instantiations (e.g., require that outcome A is true, outcome B is false, etc.).
- The correctness property: for every instantiation, GOOD_PAIR_EXISTS must exactly characterize existence of an R-satisfying pair; no false positives or negatives are allowed.

Do not prescribe a particular implementation strategy; focus on the abstract declarative behavior and the correctness conditions listed above.
