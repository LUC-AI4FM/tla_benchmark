Specify a formal model of a distributed termination-detection algorithm for a unidirectional ring of N > 0 processes. The system must represent processes that can be either active (doing work or sending messages) or inactive, and each process carries a local color which can be “white” or “black.” A single circulating token travels deterministically around the ring in one direction. The algorithm must allow active processes to send activation messages to other processes (which may be ahead or behind in the ring ordering); sending an activation to a process with a higher index should mark the sender black. Processes may independently become inactive at any time. A process that holds the token may pass it to its predecessor under rules that depend only on the holder’s active flag, the holder’s color, and the token color; passing a token over a black process must ensure the token becomes black and the process becomes white.

The specification should capture safety that when the token is white and located at the master process (index 0) while that master is white and passive (and its immediate successor is white), then in fact every process in the ring is inactive (meaning termination has been detected correctly). It should also capture liveness: whenever all processes have become inactive, eventually the master will hold a white token and detect termination. Include an assumption that N is fixed and known, and express fairness constraints sufficient to guarantee progress of token-passing and prevent starvation of the termination-detection protocol. Do not prescribe an implementation; describe only the states, allowed transitions, and the correctness properties above.
