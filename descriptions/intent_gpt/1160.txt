Model the Two-Phase Commit coordination protocol executed by a set of independent resource managers (participants) coordinating a single distributed transaction. The model must represent each participant's local decision state and the legal state transitions that reflect the protocol's allowed behaviors (preparing, voting to commit, and aborting). The system starts with all participants in an initial "working" state. Participants may independently move to a "prepared" state to indicate readiness. Once enough information exists for a global decision, participants may transition to either a permanent "committed" or "aborted" decision. The specification should capture both the optimistic commit path (all participants prepared leading to commit) and the abort path (any participant aborting or lack of unanimous preparedness causing abort). The model must allow nondeterministic interleaving of participant actions.

Specify the following correctness requirements as invariants and liveness-style properties:
- Safety: No two participants may end up in conflicting final decisions (one committed and another aborted) in any reachable state.
- Type and state-safety: each participant's state is always one of the allowed states (working, prepared, committed, aborted).
- Progress constraints: from a state where all participants are prepared, it must be possible for any participant to decide commit; if any participant has not voted to commit, it must still be possible for participants to decide abort.
- Specify the initial condition, the legal state transitions, and the overall system temporal behavior (always transitions are among the specified actions). Do not prescribe internal implementation details such as message formats or a coordinator process; the model should express intent at the level of participants' local states and allowed transitions.
