Specify a simple state-transition system with two integer state components. The system starts in an initial state where the first component equals 1 and the second equals 2. Each transition of the system must leave both components unchanged — that is, no action may modify either integer. Model the state space, the initial condition, and the transition relation so that it captures the requirement that every step is identity on the pair of components.

State the properties that must hold for all reachable states: safety properties asserting that the first component remains equal to 1 and the second remains equal to 2, and a stuttering-insensitive invariant stating that the pair of components always equals its initial value. Also assert that the system admits only stuttering steps (steps that do not change any component), and that no transitions exist that would alter one component while leaving the other unchanged.

Finally, include a temporal liveness assertion that the system does not deadlock (i.e., at every reachable state some transition is allowed), even though allowed transitions are only stuttering steps. Ensure the specification is explicit about treating the pair of components as a fixed tuple when referring to unmodified state, so equality and unchangedness are interpreted on the combined pair rather than on individual projection semantics.
