Specify a TLA+ model of a non‑blocking atomic commitment protocol for one coordinator and a set of participants that communicate by unreliable asynchronous point‑to‑point messages and by a simple broadcast primitive (a broadcast is modeled as sending the same message individually to each participant, any of which may be lost if the sender or a recipient fails). The model should represent crashes (stop failures) of the coordinator and participants; a crashed process stays crashed. Failures may be detected by other processes (modeled abstractly, not by real timeouts), and message loss may occur due to crashes during send.

The protocol to be modeled is the classic two‑phase style atomic commitment where:
- The coordinator first requests votes from all participants.
- Each live participant that receives a request replies with a vote (yes or no).
- The coordinator collects votes (unless some participant crashes or its vote is not delivered) and decides: commit iff all collected votes are yes, otherwise abort.
- The coordinator broadcasts its decision to participants using the simple broadcast primitive; participants that receive the decision adopt it.
- Participants may also decide locally to abort if they observe failures that prevent safe commit (e.g., coordinator crashed before sending request or a participant voted no).

Specify initial conditions and legal state space (who is alive, votes, undecided/commit/abort decisions). State the allowed transitions abstractly (sending requests, sending votes, coordinator making decision, broadcasting decision, process crashes) but do not prescribe implementation sequencing.

Require and formalize safety properties:
- Agreement: no two non‑faulty participants decide differently; once a participant decides, its decision does not change.
- Validity: commit can occur only if every participant’s vote was yes.
- Abort justification: an abort decision must be explainable by at least one no vote or by some crash (participant or coordinator).

Require liveness properties under appropriate fairness and fault assumptions:
- If no failures occur thereafter, all live participants eventually decide.
- If the coordinator and all participants remain non‑faulty and messages eventually get through, the protocol terminates with every participant deciding (commit or abort) consistent with validity.

Also include auxiliary invariants to aid reasoning (e.g., stability of faults and votes) but do not tie the specification to a particular implementation or variable naming.
