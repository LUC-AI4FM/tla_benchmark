Specify a formal model of a numeric system that augments the integers with a separate type of real-number-like values, supporting exact integer arithmetic, a distinct real value set, and mixed integer–real operations. The model should treat integer values and real values as disjoint domains and provide a well-defined binary division operation that accepts either two reals or two integers and returns a compatible numeric value; division by zero must be handled explicitly (e.g., by producing a distinguished Infinity value or by preventing the operation). Include a representation of an explicit Infinity element for the real side and rules for comparisons and arithmetic involving Infinity. The model must expose a notion of type discipline so that integer operations do not implicitly coerce integers into reals, yet mixed operations behave predictably according to specified promotion rules. Specify invariants ensuring preservation of integer arithmetic properties (associativity, commutativity for addition and multiplication, and identity elements) and correctness of mixed operations (no silent loss of integer identity, and deterministic promotion to real when required). State safety properties that forbid invalid divisions and operations across disjoint domains, and liveness properties guaranteeing that arithmetic computations progress when operands are valid. Include clear definitions of allowed values, division semantics, Infinity behavior, and required correctness invariants.
