Specify a formal model of the Two-Phase Commit (2PC) protocol for a distributed transaction involving a single transaction coordinator (transaction manager) and an arbitrary finite set of resource managers. The model should describe the allowed states and asynchronous message-driven interactions, not an implementation. It must include: initial conditions where every resource manager is in a local "working" state and the coordinator is in an initial state; actions by a resource manager to decide to prepare (move to prepared) or to locally choose to abort (move to aborted) without sending messages; the ability of a resource manager to receive and act upon a global Commit or Abort instruction (transition to committed or aborted respectively); the coordinator receiving prepare notifications from resource managers, collecting them, and when all resource managers have prepared, issuing a Commit; and the coordinator being able to spontaneously decide to Abort. Model messages abstractly as delivery predicates (i.e., whether a Commit, Abort, or a given RM’s Prepared notification has been received), allowing asynchronous delivery and independent ordering. Specify safety properties: (1) consistency — no two resource managers may end up with conflicting final decisions (one committed while another aborted), and (2) validity — if every resource manager prepared and the coordinator issues Commit, then all resource managers that later receive the Commit must move to committed; and (3) that once the coordinator decides Commit (or Abort), it stays in that terminal decision. Also require that state transitions respect the causal flow (e.g., an RM cannot move from working directly to committed unless a Commit is delivered). Do not constrain internal encodings; keep the model at the level of states and message delivery predicates so it can be used to prove these safety invariants under asynchronous message delivery and nondeterministic scheduling.
