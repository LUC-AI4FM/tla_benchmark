Specify a hierarchical directory-management system that maintains a tree of named directories and files. The model should capture operations to create, delete, rename, and move directories and files subject to filesystem constraints: names within the same directory must be unique, directories cannot be moved into their own descendants, and deletions of non-empty directories must either be disallowed or require an explicit recursive delete operation. Support atomic file creation and update operations that preserve content consistency.

Ensure the model supports concurrent clients performing operations, possibly conflicting, and express correctness under concurrency: the filesystem must remain a connected acyclic tree of directories with files attached to directories; invariants must prevent cycles and duplicate sibling names. Specify atomicity and isolation guarantees for each high-level operation (e.g., move, rename, recursive delete), and define what constitutes a successful operation versus a conflicting failure. Include eventual consistency requirements for concurrent non-conflicting operations: if two operations commute, they should both take effect.

Formalize safety properties (no cycles, name uniqueness, content preservation for non-overwritten writes) and liveness requirements: operations that are enabled and non-conflicting should eventually complete. Provide assertions or temporal formulas that capture these invariants and progress goals so the system can be model-checked for correctness.
