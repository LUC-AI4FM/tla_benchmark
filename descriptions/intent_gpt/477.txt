Specify a formal TLA+ model of a non‑blocking atomic commitment protocol for N distributed processes that may crash and that use unreliable, asynchronous message delivery and a local failure detector. The system should model processes that initially vote either YES or NO, send their vote to others, receive subsets of votes, and then decide to COMMIT or ABORT. The specification must capture that crashes can occur at any time, messages may be delayed or lost (only sent messages may be received later), and each process has a possibly inaccurate failure detector output that can change over time.

Require the protocol to guarantee the standard atomic‑commit safety properties:
- Agreement: no two non‑faulty processes decide differently (no COMMIT and ABORT concurrently).
- Validity: if every process initially votes YES and no relevant failures occur, then some process may decide COMMIT; if any process initially votes NO then no process decides COMMIT.
- Termination (non‑blocking liveness): if enough processes run and the failure detector eventually reports no suspected failures, then all correct processes that continue executing eventually decide (either COMMIT or ABORT); crashes do not permanently block decisions by correct processes.

Also require invariants for type correctness (message and state domains), and expose the intended weak fairness assumption that each enabled local receive/send/decision step for a live process will eventually occur. Do not fix an implementation strategy; allow nondeterministic receipt of any subset of sent messages and nondeterministic failure‑detector outputs.
