Model the Alternating Bit reliable-data-transfer protocol between a sender, a receiver, and an environment that injects new payloads. The model should treat payloads as abstract elements from a fixed set and messages as pairs of a payload and a single bit. The system continually accepts new data items from the environment and must deliver, in order, each submitted payload to the receiver exactly once despite unreliable communication of the control bit between sender and receiver.

Specify the roles and their observable behaviors abstractly: the sender may take the next queued payload when it decides to transmit and flips its control bit; the receiver accepts a transmitted message when it sees a bit different from its own and updates its delivered state; the environment non-deterministically appends new payloads to the pending-input queue but only when sender and receiver agree on the bit. Do not prescribe specific message loss, duplication, or timing mechanisms; allow nondeterministic interleaving and unreliable delivery by permitting that messages may or may not be acted on immediately.

State the key correctness properties to verify:
- Safety: every payload delivered to the receiver must be equal to some payload that was previously submitted by the environment (no spurious deliveries).
- No duplication: a payload must not be delivered more than once.
- Order preservation: delivered payloads must appear in the same order as submitted.
- Liveness: if the environment stops adding new payloads, then eventually every pending payload will be delivered (fairness of sender/receiver progress).
