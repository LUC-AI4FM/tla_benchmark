Specify a formal TLA+ model of a distributed mutual‑exclusion protocol that assigns logical tickets to processes to coordinate access to a single critical section (the classic bakery algorithm). The model should represent an arbitrary fixed number of processes and allow each process to repeatedly try to enter, execute, and exit a critical section. The protocol must let processes choose ticket numbers in a way that yields a total ordering (by ticket then process id) used to decide entry order, and it must allow concurrent, asynchronous steps and nonblocking waiting on other processes' state.

Require the model to guarantee the following correctness properties:

- Mutual exclusion: no two distinct processes are ever simultaneously in the critical section.
- Safety of ticket assignment: when a process holds a nonzero ticket, other processes that are competing must either respect the lexicographic ordering of tickets or eventually obtain tickets larger than that process's ticket.
- Type and state invariants: all state components remain within their declared domains through all transitions.
- Liveness goals (optional but supported): either deadlock‑freedom (someone trying eventually either enters) and/or starvation‑freedom (every process that persistently tries eventually enters), stated as temporal properties.

Describe the variables abstractly (e.g., per‑process ticket values, per‑process flags, per‑process bookkeeping sets, program control state) and the allowed atomic transitions (local steps for choosing a ticket, waiting on others, entering and leaving the critical section). Do not prescribe a particular implementation pattern; instead require that the model capture the intended ordering, waiting, and assignment semantics so the above invariants and temporal properties can be proved or model‑checked.
