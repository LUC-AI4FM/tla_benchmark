Specify a formal TLA+ model of the classical "prisoners and a light bulb" coordination puzzle with a single shared binary signal (lamp) and a central announcement mechanism. Model N distinct prisoners who are initially isolated and cannot communicate once the protocol begins. Repeatedly and nondeterministically one prisoner is chosen and placed alone in a special room that contains a lamp that can be either on or off. Each visit may change the lamp. At any time any prisoner may make a single final announcement claiming that every prisoner has visited the room at least once; if the announcement is correct all prisoners are released, otherwise the game ends with failure.

Capture both variants where the initial lamp state is known (off) and where it is unknown. Allow the prisoners to agree on an arbitrary strategy beforehand but do not model any post-start communication. The model should express the standard reliable strategy idea: one distinguished prisoner will be the only one to increment a shared count when seeing a certain lamp state; other prisoners will use the lamp at most a specified bounded number of times as a private signal. The model must enforce that non-counter prisoners only signal up to the allowed limit, and the counter only increments when legitimately observing the signal. Include fairness that ensures each prisoner is eventually chosen infinitely often.

Specify safety properties:
- No announcement can be made unless every prisoner has indeed visited the room (no false victory).
Specify liveness properties:
- Under the fairness assumption, the protocol eventually leads to a correct announcement (eventual termination/victory).

Also specify type and boundedness invariants (counts and per-prisoner signal budgets), and make the victory threshold depend on whether the initial lamp state is unknown.
