Specify a formal model of a simple assertion/invariant-checking scenario that distinguishes between two ways of expressing membership and existential quantification over sets of functions and maps. The system should represent a finite domain and a mapping value, and allow expressing two candidate invariants: one that asserts that a particular map value belongs to the union of a family of singleton sets of maps, and another that asserts the existence of a family member such that that map value belongs to it. The model should permit checking whether each invariant is well-typed and whether membership and existential quantification are equivalent in this context. The specification must capture the exact set constructions: a singleton family containing a single set whose element is a specific map, and the union over that family. It must ensure that the invariants are decidable (true or false) under standard set-theoretic semantics, and must expose any subtle differences between explicitly forming a union of a comprehension versus using an existential over the comprehension domain. The correctness properties to guarantee are (1) soundness of membership judgments about maps in unions and (2) equivalence (or documented non-equivalence) between the union-membership form and the existential form when the family is a singleton. The model should be minimal and avoid implementing any operational transition system beyond initial state and invariant checks.
