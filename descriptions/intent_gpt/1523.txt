Specify a formal model of the classic “prisoners and switches” coordination problem. Model a finite set of prisoners who are repeatedly and nondeterministically chosen, one at a time, to enter a room containing two binary switches. Each visit a prisoner must change exactly one switch. The schedule guarantees that every prisoner is selected infinitely often, but the order is otherwise arbitrary and unpredictable. Before visits begin the prisoners may agree on a protocol; afterwards they cannot communicate except via the room’s switches.

The model should capture the intended cooperative protocol goal: eventually some designated prisoner can safely declare that every prisoner has visited the room at least once, and doing so must be guaranteed correct. The design must ensure two key correctness properties: safety — the declaration is only ever made when every prisoner has actually visited the room at least once (no false positives); and liveness — under the fairness assumption that every prisoner is scheduled infinitely often, the protocol eventually enables a correct declaration (the prisoners eventually succeed).

The specification must express system invariants needed to prove safety (for example, bookkeeping that prevents over-counting), the nondeterministic scheduling of visits, the constraint that every visit flips exactly one switch, and fairness assumptions ensuring infinite visitation. Do not prescribe a particular implementation mechanism; instead state the behavioral requirements, the roles (one dedicated counter vs. other prisoners, if used), and the safety and liveness properties the protocol must satisfy.
