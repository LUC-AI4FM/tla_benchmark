Specify a concurrent state machine controlling a simple two-component system: a nonnegative integer counter and a boolean flag. The system repeatedly performs one of two kinds of steps, chosen nondeterministically, while the counter stays within a bounded range. The model should allow initial states where the counter is within a small specified range and the flag is either true or false.

The first kind of step is enabled only when the counter is strictly less than a given threshold and the flag is true; it must flip the flag and leave the counter unchanged. The second kind of step is enabled only when the flag is false; it must flip the flag and increment the counter by one. Steps must always update at least the flag or the counter as required, and the system must be closed under stuttering of the two variables.

Specify liveness so that the system cannot permanently avoid making enabled transitions (fairness for the overall step relation). Capture safety properties: the counter never decreases, never exceeds the threshold plus allowance implied by the initial range, and the flag is always a boolean. Also capture the intended eventual progress property: under weak fairness, the counter should eventually reach the threshold when the environment allows repeated transitions. Finally, include a predicate to express a meaningful state reduction (for example, that a particular counter value may be reduced to another under the transition relation) so that refinement or invariant checking can be exercised.
