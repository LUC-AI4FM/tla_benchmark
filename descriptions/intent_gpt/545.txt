Specify a concurrent system of N processes that coordinate to enter a critical section while ensuring mutual exclusion and progress. Model the processes as identical finite-state threads that repeatedly alternate between noncritical work, protocol phases for requesting access, and a critical section. The system must guarantee that at most one process is in the critical section at any time (safety). It must also guarantee liveness properties: if a process continually attempts to enter the critical section, it will eventually succeed (no starvation) under fair scheduling; and if a process is in the protocol requesting access and other processes do not block indefinitely, the requesting protocol makes progress toward the critical section (absence of deadlock). Include the ability to vary N as a constant parameter. The model should abstract away timing but include local flags or stages that allow processes to express intent and coordinate (e.g., reads/writes of simple shared variables). Specify an initial state where all processes are in their noncritical state and shared variables are in a neutral state. Formally state invariants expressing mutual exclusion and any required properties about shared variables, and temporal properties expressing eventual entry and deadlock freedom under fairness assumptions.
