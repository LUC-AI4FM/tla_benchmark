Specify a model of a system that maintains and reasons about a fixed numeric interval [t_min, t_max] that is established as an external/environmental constant. The specification should treat the interval endpoints as immutable throughout the execution and capture the assumption that the lower bound is at most the upper bound. The model must allow arbitrary system behavior otherwise (no internal state changes required), but must always preserve the invariant that the declared bounds satisfy the assumed constraint t_min <= t_max.

The goal is to verify that any analysis which permits independent initialization of constants and then composes that with runtime invariant checking will respect the externally provided constraints on those constants. The specification should therefore:

- Declare two numeric parameters representing the lower and upper endpoints of an interval.
- Require as a top-level assumption that the lower endpoint is less than or equal to the upper endpoint.
- Leave the dynamic behavior of the system unconstrained so that only the relationship between the endpoints is relevant.
- State as the principal safety property that the assumed ordering of endpoints holds in every reachable state.

The correctness property to prove is that, under the given assumption on the constants, the invariant t_min <= t_max is always true; the model should expose any mismatch between assumed constant constraints and the invariant-checking mechanism.
