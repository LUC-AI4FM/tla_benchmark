Specify a formal model of the translation pipeline that takes a parsed abstract syntax tree (AST) of a small imperative, labeled algorithm language and produces a target-state specification suitable for model checking. The model should treat the AST as input (a tree of records for constructs such as algorithms, procedures, processes, labeled statements, loops, conditionals, assignments, calls, returns, gotos, variable declarations, and expression tokens) and must capture the legal shapes and well-formedness constraints of that AST independently of any particular translation strategy.

The specification must require detecting and rejecting ill-formed inputs: invalid identifiers, illegal labels (including reserved names), malformed statement nesting, duplicate or unreachable labels, misuse of call/return/goto, incorrect variable declaration forms, and bad expression tokens. It must also define the semantics of label scoping so that control-transfer constructs reference only labels valid in their surrounding context.

Additionally, specify the correctness requirements of the translation: if the AST is well-formed then the translation must produce a syntactically valid target specification (represented abstractly as a structured sequence or tree of strings) that preserves control flow and data-flow semantics of the source program, maps procedures/processes and local/global variables consistently, and encodes termination and error states explicitly. The model must expose and check error-reporting behavior (translation fails with informative error when input is illegal) and must support a switchable fairness constraint option that the resulting specification must reflect (no fairness, weak/strong fairness of actions, or weak fairness of the whole next-state relation).
