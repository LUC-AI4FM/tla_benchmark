Specify a formal model of a light-client block verification protocol used by a client that periodically fetches and verifies headers from a set of validators. The model should represent multiple validators with a bounded fraction of Byzantine faults, a trusted initial verification point, and a target chain height the client wants to verify. The system must capture that the client uses time-bounded trust (a trusting period) and must account for clock drift between the client’s local clock and a reference/global time; the model must allow the actual clock drift to differ from the assumed worst-case drift. The client should probe validators to obtain signed headers, maintain a set of fetched headers and their verification status, and move its latest verified height forward only when sufficient evidence (e.g., signatures from a quorum of non-faulty validators or equivalent) establishes correct commitment to a higher height within the trusting period. The model should permit some validators to be faulty or unresponsive and should capture detection of equivocation or conflicting headers. Key correctness properties to guarantee: safety (the client never finalizes a header that conflicts with a header that would be produced by a correct majority of validators), liveness under bounded faults and bounded network delays (the client eventually verifies the target height when enough honest validators respond and time bounds hold), and time-safety with respect to trusting periods and clock drift (the client refuses to extend trust beyond allowable time windows, and clock-drift assumptions are sufficient to prevent acceptance of stale or replayed headers). Include invariants asserting monotonicity of verified height, preservation of trust assumptions despite drift, and that verdicts about chain validity are consistent with evidence collected.
