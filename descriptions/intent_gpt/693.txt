Specify a formal model of finite directed and undirected graphs and common graph-theoretic predicates and queries. The model should treat a graph as a pair consisting of a finite set of nodes and a set of edges that are ordered pairs of nodes; support taking subgraphs, unions, and the transpose of a directed graph; and characterize when a graph is directed, undirected, empty, or drawn from the universe of all graphs over a given node set.

Model operations and derived relations that must be available and precisely defined:
- Paths and simple paths (finite sequences of nodes connected by successive edges) and a predicate for reachability between two nodes via a simple path.
- A transitive closure / connectivity relation that can be computed more efficiently than existential path search (so it may be used as a cached or algorithmic definition).
- Tests for strong connectivity and acyclicity (DAG), including detection of self-loops and mutual reachability indicating cycles.
- Roots and leaves, predecessors and successors of a node, and functions that map a set of nodes to their immediate predecessors or successors.
- Ancestors and descendants via transitive closure, and in-degree and out-degree of nodes.
- Tests for tree structure with a designated root (each node has exactly one parent except the root, and all nodes reach the root).
- Bipartiteness with given partitions, and a predicate indicating existence of a cycle.

State the correctness properties to verify:
- Definitions are consistent (e.g., transpose preserves node set; undirectedness implies edge symmetry).
- Reachability as defined by the transitive closure is equivalent to existence of a simple path.
- Cycle detection predicates correctly identify exactly those graphs that are not DAGs.
- Tree predicate implies acyclicity, unique parent property, and connectivity to the root.
- Successor/predecessor and ancestor/descendant operators correctly relate via transitive closure and edge relation.

Do not prescribe an implementation technique; allow both declarative (relation-based) and algorithmic (matrix or Warshall-style recursion) formulations provided they are provably equivalent to the high-level definitions.
