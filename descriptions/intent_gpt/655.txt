Model a light-client blockchain verification protocol used by a client that polls a set of validator nodes to obtain and verify block headers (light blocks) up to a target height. The model should describe how a client chooses validators to query, sends probes, receives potentially delayed or faulty responses, and decides whether it has enough evidence to advance its verified height. The system must capture that some subset of validators may be Byzantine (faulty) and may equivocate, withhold responses, or send incorrect signatures; network communication may introduce delays and message loss; and local clocks may drift relative to an external reference and affect time-based timeouts and trust windows.

Specify the client’s verification workflow: how it uses recent trusted headers, trusting period and clock drift bounds to validate newly fetched headers, when it requires conflicting or matching votes from multiple validators, and how it handles timeouts and insufficient evidence. The specification must guarantee safety properties (the client never accepts an invalid header as verified, and never reverts to a lower verified height) and liveness properties under reasonable conditions (if a sufficient fraction of honest validators respond within bounds and the client’s clock drift remains within assumptions, the client eventually verifies the target height). Also include monitoring properties: detect and report suspicious validator behavior and maintain audit traces of previous verification decisions for postmortem analysis.
