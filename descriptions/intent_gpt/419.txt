Specify a simple, always-stable system that exercises integer arithmetic and ranges so a model checker can validate the language’s integer semantics. The model should present a single-state system that does not change over time but is required to satisfy a collection of arithmetic and relational facts about integers. The specification must assert equalities, inequalities, range membership, division and modulus identities, exponentiation, and evaluation of mixed arithmetic expressions, including negative operands. It should require that integer division and remainder satisfy the identity dividend = divisor * (dividend div divisor) + (dividend % divisor) for representative positive and negative dividends and divisors, and that division by 1 behaves as identity and modulus by 1 yields zero. It must check behaviors of modulus with negative dividends (e.g. resulting remainder in the standard nonnegative range) and confirm properties of integer ranges, including empty ranges when lower bound exceeds upper bound and correct enumeration of descending ranges. Also include tests for operator precedence in mixed expressions, basic comparisons of negative numbers, and exponentiation identities. The specification’s correctness properties are that every asserted arithmetic fact holds in the initial state; any failing assertion constitutes a counterexample.
