Model a simple sequential counter system that starts at zero and repeatedly increments by one until reaching a fixed nonnegative bound, then remains constant. The system should accept the bound as a parameter (assumed to be a natural number). It must initialize the counter to zero and permit only actions that either increase the counter by exactly one when it is strictly less than the bound, or leave the counter unchanged once the bound is reached or exceeded.

Specify safety properties: (1) the counter always remains a natural number; (2) the counter never decreases; (3) the counter never increases by more than one in a single step; and (4) the counter never exceeds the given bound. Specify a liveness requirement that the counter will eventually reach the bound (under a fair scheduler that does not forever prevent progress while increments are enabled).

State an invariant that captures the allowed range and monotonicity of the counter, and a property characterizing permitted transitions. Also require that the bound parameter is nonnegative. Do not prescribe an implementation strategy; focus on the abstract state, the transition relation, and the correctness properties (safety and liveness) to be proven.
