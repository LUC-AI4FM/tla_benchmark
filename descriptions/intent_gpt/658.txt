Specify a formal model of a light-client blockchain verifier that fetches and verifies headers (light blocks) from a set of validators, some of which may be Byzantine. The model should describe the protocol by which the client incrementally advances its trusted chain tip from an initially trusted height to a target height by fetching headers from validators, validating signatures/quorums, and applying cryptographic and time-based safety checks. The client must tolerate up to a specified fraction of faulty validators and must detect and respond to conflicting headers that could indicate misbehavior.

The model must include: an abstract notion of validators and their responses (including correct, faulty, and offline behaviors); the process of selecting validators to probe and rules for when to accept a fetched header as verified; clock skew and trusting-period constraints that affect the acceptance of headers; and the verification of validator sets across heights (changes in validator membership or voting power). It should capture retries, timeouts, and how the client escalates probe patterns when verification fails.

Key correctness properties to guarantee: safety (the client never accepts two conflicting headers for the same height as both verified), liveness under modelled network and fault assumptions (if a sufficient quorum of honest validators exists and messages eventually arrive, the client eventually verifies the target height), and validity of acceptance (every verified header has sufficient signatures/quorum from the assumed honest set and satisfies time/trust constraints). Also specify detection and handling of equivocation or long-range attacks.
