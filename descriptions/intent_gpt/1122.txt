Specify a formal model of a two-phase commit protocol with a backup decision maker and crash-failure behavior for both resource managers and the primary transaction coordinator. The system coordinates a fixed set of distributed resource participants that initially do local work, then independently prepare, and must eventually decide to commit or abort a global transaction. A primary coordinator should observe whether all participants are prepared to decide commit or whether any participant has aborted/failed, and then issue a global commit or abort decision. The coordinator may crash after making its decision but before communicating it; a backup coordinator must be able to take over when the primary becomes unavailable and reliably complete the decision based on the observed participant states and any partially visible coordinator state. Participants may crash once (fail-stop) and later be observed as failed; they should make local irrevocable transitions to prepared, committed, aborted, or failed only according to protocol rules.

Model requirements and correctness properties:
- Safety: no two participants may end up in conflicting final outcomes (i.e., one committed while another aborted). The global decision must be consistent across all non-failed participants.
- Validity: if every participant reached prepared and the coordinator (or backup) decides commit, then no participant can end up aborted; if any participant aborted or failed before commit, the system may decide abort but must not produce a commit that violates observed aborts.
- Liveness/termination: under fairness assumptions that allow nonfailed processes to make progress, the protocol must eventually reach a state where all processes have reached a terminal local state (commit, abort, or failed) and the coordinator role (primary or backup) completes the global decision.
- Failover correctness: the backup must only decide commit when it is safe to do so (equivalent knowledge to the primary), and must be able to decide abort if any participant indicates abort/failed state, even if the primary is hidden/crashed.

Do not prescribe implementation details such as message encodings or exact action sequences; instead specify abstract state transitions for participants, the primary coordinator, and the backup coordinator, their enabling conditions, and the fairness assumptions needed to prove the safety and liveness properties above.
