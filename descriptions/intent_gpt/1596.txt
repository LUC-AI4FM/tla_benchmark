Specify a formal model of a fast mutual-exclusion protocol for N asynchronous processes that repeatedly attempt to enter a single critical section. The model should describe the abstract behavior of each process: a nonblocking attempt to acquire the lock, a bounded (but possibly multi-step) waiting protocol that involves announcing intent and probing others’ announcements, a possible short backoff/abort and retry if contention is detected, and a final successful entry followed by explicit release. The model must allow arbitrary asynchrony and interleaving of processes, and must not assume atomic multi-process updates beyond single-variable assignments.

Requirements:
- Model N numbered processes that loop forever, each repeatedly performing the entry protocol, executing the critical section, and then leaving.
- Represent the shared state needed for coordination (e.g., flags/announcements, one or two shared coordination variables) and any per-process local state needed for the algorithm’s waiting and retry logic.
- Allow processes to non-deterministically observe shared variables and to wait until certain conditions hold (representing busy-wait/spin or conditional blocking).
- Allow a process to detect contention and abort its current attempt, resetting any announced state before retrying.

Correctness properties to guarantee:
1. Safety (mutual exclusion): at most one process may be inside the critical section at any time.
2. Progress/liveness: if processes continually attempt to enter, then infinitely often some process enters the critical section (global eventual entry fairness).
3. No permanent starvation under fairness assumptions for each individual process’s entry attempt (weak fairness for actions of each process should ensure eventual success if others do not indefinitely prevent it).

Specify these behaviors and properties at the abstract state-machine level so they can be checked with a model checker, leaving implementation choices (exact number of coordination variables, structure of waiting loops) open as long as the above guarantees are expressible and verifiable.
