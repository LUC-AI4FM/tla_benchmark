Specify a formal model of a proof-verification environment for a dependently scoped, label-aware theorem-proving language (similar to TLA+ proofs). The model should capture the structure of proof obligations, nested assumptions, and labeled subproofs without committing to a particular implementation of proof checking. The system must represent introductions of new identifiers under scope (including constants, variables, state/action/temporal declarations), the creation of named proof obligations, and the ability to refer to subexpressions and subproofs by hierarchical labels. It must allow assumptions that assert simple boolean facts or introduce witnesses, picks, and quantifier instantiations, and it must allow combining facts with logical connectives (including equivalence) and reasoning rules such as SUFFICES, HAVE, TAKE, WITNESS, CASE, and QED.

Key correctness properties:
- Soundness: any statement marked proved must be logically entailed by the available assumptions and previously proven facts within the visible scope.
- Scope discipline: names introduced NEW or by quantifiers are visible only where allowed; shadowing and nested NEW/ASSUME must not leak identifiers outside their scopes.
- Label integrity: references to labeled subexpressions or subproofs resolve unambiguously to an existing label in the current or enclosing scopes; malformed or illegal label references are detected.
- Consistency of proof status: a proof step may be marked QED only when its subgoals are discharged; SUFFICES/HAVE/WITNESS steps update obligations consistently.
- Error detection: malformed constructs (illegal label usage, invalid subexpression references, wrong-arity instances, or misuse of proof primitives) are reported as proof errors.

Model the proof-verifier as a transition system that manipulates obligation stacks and scopes; specify invariants ensuring the correctness properties above and liveness conditions that legitimate proof strategies can eventually discharge obligations.
