Model a mutual‑exclusion protocol for N concurrent processes that generalizes Peterson’s algorithm to N participants. The model should describe processes that repeatedly alternate between noncritical work, a structured entry protocol to acquire exclusive access, a single critical section, and an exit protocol that relinquishes access. The entry protocol must use a bounded number (O(N)) of ordered "levels" or stages and a mechanism for indicating which process most recently attempted to enter at each level, so that progress is coordinated without shared locks or atomic multi‑writer updates beyond single shared variables. Processes may read and write shared level indicators and last‑entrant indicators and may busy‑wait (spin) on those values.

Specify the safety and liveness requirements to be verified:

- Mutual exclusion: At most one process may be inside the critical section at any time.
- Absence of deadlock: From any reachable state, some process that is attempting to enter or is in a nonterminal state of the entry protocol must be able to eventually make progress (system does not get stuck with all contending processes blocked forever).
- Starvation‑freedom (or bounded waiting): If a process attempts to enter the critical section and other processes stop trying after a finite time, then the attempting process eventually enters the critical section. Equivalently, a process that repeatedly attempts to enter should not be permanently overtaken by others.
- Fairness assumptions: State any fairness constraints needed (e.g., weak/strong fairness for each process’s enabled steps) to guarantee liveness properties.

Do not prescribe the exact program structure; allow choices for representing per‑process level counters, last‑entrant indicators per level, and waiting behavior. Specify initial states, the possible atomic steps processes may take, and the invariants and temporal properties that capture the correctness criteria above.
