Specify a formal model of the classic Missionaries and Cannibals river‑crossing problem. The system should represent two river banks and a boat that can shuttle between them, carrying at most two people. There are two kinds of agents: missionaries and cannibals. Initially all agents are on one bank. A move is an atomic boat crossing that transfers one or two agents from the bank where the boat is located to the other bank, and then the boat ends up on the opposite bank. Model only legal moves that respect the boat capacity and only allow people who are present on the departure bank to board.

Require safety: at all times on each bank the number of missionaries present must be greater than or equal to the number of cannibals, unless there are zero missionaries on that bank (i.e., cannibals must never strictly outnumber missionaries on a bank where missionaries are present). Require progress/termination: there exists a sequence of legal moves that transfers all agents to the opposite bank (goal state). The model should permit nondeterministic choices of which legal crossing to take. Include explicit state invariants for valid bank and boat configuration types and for the safety property above. Do not commit to a particular search/control strategy; specify the transition relation and the invariants to be checked.
