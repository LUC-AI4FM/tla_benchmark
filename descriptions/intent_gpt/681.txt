Specify a formal model of solving an initial-value problem for a system of ordinary differential equations (ODEs) and reasoning about existence and evaluation of a solution. The model should represent an n-dimensional autonomous ODE specified by a differential operator that, given a time and a vector of values (function value and its first n derivatives up to order n), returns zero exactly on solution trajectories. The model must allow an interval of interest [a,b] (real numbers) and a vector of initial values for the function and its derivatives at the left endpoint a. It must assert the existence of a family of real-valued functions defined on an open interval containing [a,b] that are sufficiently differentiable: the 0th component is the unknown function, each higher component is its successive derivative, and all derivatives up to the required order exist and match the usual limit definition. The family must satisfy the differential operator pointwise on the interval and the initial-value constraints at a. The specification should produce the vector of values of the 0th function and its derivatives at b (the solution at the right endpoint) and guarantee that any reported result corresponds to a genuine differentiable solution meeting the ODE and initial conditions. Key correctness properties: well-formedness of the functions and derivatives; satisfaction of the ODE everywhere on the interval; matching initial values; and the result correctly equals the endpoint evaluations of that solution.
