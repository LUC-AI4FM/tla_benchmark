Model the classic sliding-block puzzle on a finite rectangular grid (for example Klotski). The model should represent a board as a partition of grid cells into pieces; each piece occupies one or more adjacent orthogonally-connected cells and pieces do not overlap and remain within the grid. The only legal state changes are sliding a single piece by one cell in a cardinal direction into an adjacent empty cell or sequence of empty cells such that the piece’s moved cells remain within the board and do not collide with other pieces.

Specify an initial configuration that places pieces in a given starting arrangement and a goal condition identifying a target set of cells (occupied by some piece or pieces) that constitutes success. The model must allow nondeterministic choices of which legal slide to perform next.

State and verify the following correctness properties:
- Safety: Invariantly, pieces remain disjoint and contained within the grid; no move produces overlapping pieces or cells outside the grid.
- Progress/Liveness: From the initial configuration, it must be possible (there exists a sequence of legal slides) to reach a state satisfying the goal condition (reachability of the goal).
- Optional symmetry/irrelevance: Moves that do not change the set of occupied cells (no-op) are forbidden.

Do not constrain the implementation strategy; describe only the state representation, legal moves, initial and goal configurations, and the invariants and reachability property to be proven.
