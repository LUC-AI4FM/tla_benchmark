Model a system that determines whether a person is legally allowed to operate a vehicle based on their year-of-birth and the jurisdiction's licensing age. The model should accept as inputs a person's two-digit birth-year encoding (0–99) and a fixed minimum age for licensure, and it must account for the century ambiguity of two-digit years so that the computed current age is unambiguous given a fixed current year. The system must compute: the person's age in whole years, whether the person meets the minimum licensing age, and whether they therefore are legally permitted to drive.

Specify the legal decision as a deterministic function from the inputs (birth-year encoding, current year, licensing age) to the outputs (computed age, eligible? boolean). Capture boundary cases precisely: when the person’s age equals the licensing threshold, that must be considered eligible. Include validation that the input birth-year encoding is within 0..99 and the licensing age is a nonnegative integer. State correctness properties: (1) eligibility iff computed age ≥ licensing age, (2) computed age equals current year minus resolved four-digit birth year, and (3) for any valid inputs, the decision is stable (idempotent) when the current year and inputs do not change. Also assert that no ambiguous two-digit input can lead to two different computed ages under the same fixed current year.
