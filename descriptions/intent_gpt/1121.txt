Specify a formal model of the generalized Tower of Hanoi puzzle with D disks and N pegs. The system state should represent which disks are located on which peg (no implementation detail of encoding required). Initially all disks are stacked on a single designated source peg in size order (largest at bottom, smallest at top). A step of the system moves exactly one disk from one peg to another under the standard legal-move constraints: a disk can be moved only if it is the topmost disk on its current peg, and it may be placed only on an empty peg or on top of a larger disk. Moves are atomic and preserve the total set of disks and their identities.

Model all possible sequences of legal moves (including sequences that do not make progress toward a goal). Specify the correctness properties to check:

- Invariance: the multiset of disks across all pegs is constant (no duplication or loss).
- Safety: every move respects the “topmost” and “no smaller-on-larger” constraints.
- Liveness / eventual reachability: there exists a sequence of moves that reaches the goal configuration in which all disks are stacked in order on a designated target peg, and provide a bound on the minimum number of moves needed (2^D - 1) as an expected optimal length.
- Optionally, state and check that when N < 3 the goal is unreachable for D > 1, and for N ≥ 3 the goal is reachable.

Do not prescribe a particular encoding of pegs or disks; focus on the abstract state, legal move relation, initial and goal states, and the properties above.
