Specify a model of a simple program that nondeterministically chooses one of two independent update sequences and then performs the chosen updates, ensuring the final state satisfies a deterministic safety condition.

Model an execution that starts with two integer counters initialized to zero. At a single choice point, the program must pick either the left branch or the right branch. If the left branch is chosen then the left counter is set to 1 and subsequently incremented by 1. If the right branch is chosen then the right counter is set to 1 and subsequently incremented by 1. The two branches must be mutually exclusive: only one branch’s sequence executes in any run. After the chosen branch completes, the execution reaches termination.

Specify the observable behavior (the sequences of states) and require the following correctness properties:
- Safety: upon termination, the sum of the two counters equals 2.
- Mutual exclusion of branches: in every execution exactly one branch’s two-step sequence executes and the other counter remains unchanged by that sequence.
- Liveness: the system must eventually terminate (the chosen branch completes).
- Fairness: the nondeterministic choice must permit both branches to be possible choices (no permanent starvation of a branch across enabled choice points).

Do not prescribe implementation details; model only the initial conditions, the nondeterministic choice between branches, the two-step update sequence of the chosen branch, and the termination condition. Include assertions or invariants formally expressible to check the safety and mutual-exclusion properties, and a liveness condition stating eventual termination.
