Specify a formal model of a light-client protocol for verifying blockchain headers using a small set of validators. The model should capture a client that incrementally advances a trusted verification height up to a target height by fetching and validating light blocks from validator nodes under partial fault assumptions. The client should maintain a trusted header, a verification threshold for voting power, and retry or recovery behavior when responses are missing, inconsistent, or appear to be equivocated.

Model the network as asynchronous and potentially adversarial: some validators may be faulty (Byzantine) up to a known fraction, messages may be delayed or lost, and clocks on client and nodes can drift within known bounds. The client may probe validators to obtain headers, commit signatures, and validator sets. The protocol must require that the client only advances trust to a header when it has sufficient evidence (e.g., signatures or voting power) consistent with safety policy and validator set continuity. The model should include timeouts, probe scheduling, and a trusting period after which previously trusted headers expire.

State the correctness properties to verify: safety (the client never accepts two conflicting headers for the same height or accepts a header not justified by enough honest voting power), liveness under assumed non-faulty conditions (if a majority of validators remain honest and responsive, the client can eventually reach the target height), and expiry handling (the client refuses to use headers beyond the trusting period unless re-verified). Also specify monotonicity of trust (trusted height never decreases except when explicitly expired) and tolerance to clock drift (protocol decisions remain safe within the drift bounds). Include invariants ensuring proper handling of equivocation and that recovery probes do not cause unsafe trust transitions.
