Specify a formal model of the Alternating Bit reliable data‑transfer protocol between a sender and a receiver over an unreliable channel. The model should treat data items drawn from an arbitrary finite set of messages and should capture the sender repeatedly offering new data values and the receiver repeatedly accepting and delivering them. The channel may drop, duplicate, or reorder transmissions, but may not corrupt message contents. The protocol must use a single alternating bit to associate acknowledgments with data transmissions and must ensure that each delivered data item at the receiver corresponds to some value sent by the sender.

Require safety and liveness properties: safety — the receiver never delivers the same data item twice in succession without the sender having issued an intervening new send (no spurious duplicate deliveries that the protocol fails to distinguish); and integrity — every delivered data value was previously sent. Liveness — every data value that the sender intends to transmit infinitely often (or that the sender attempts to send and continues to retransmit until acknowledged) is eventually accepted and delivered by the receiver, assuming the channel eventually delivers some retransmission (i.e., there is no permanent loss of all copies). Also require fairness conditions that prevent either process from being permanently starved.

Do not prescribe a particular implementation structure; instead state the roles (sender, receiver, channel), the observable actions (send, receive, deliver, acknowledge), and the required invariants and eventualities the model must prove.
