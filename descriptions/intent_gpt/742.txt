Model a stone-weighing system: a farmer has a single stone of integer total weight N and may break it into P integer-weight pieces. The system must capture using a two-pan balance scale and those pieces to measure out any integral target weight from 1 through N inclusive. The model should treat pieces as indistinguishable by label except for their weights (i.e., different orderings of the same multiset of piece weights are equivalent). Allowed weighing operations place any subset of pieces on the same pan as the corn and any disjoint subset on the opposite pan; a target weight w is measurable if there exists a choice of disjoint subsets whose pan-weight difference equals w.

Specify the problem parametrically in N and P, and require that a valid break is a multiset of P positive integers summing to N. The model should identify all breaks that make every w in 1..N measurable. Correctness properties to guarantee include:
- Completeness: for every target weight w in 1..N, there exists a placement of pieces (possibly moving pieces to either pan) that balances exactly w.
- Conservativity: pieces are not subdivided or combined beyond the initial break, and total mass equals N.
- Uniqueness or classification: compute or characterize all distinct multisets of piece weights (up to permutation) that satisfy the completeness property.

Also enable generation of witness assignments for each w (which pieces go on each pan) for each valid break.
