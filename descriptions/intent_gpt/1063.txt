Specify a model of an asynchronous distributed system in which a fixed set of processes repeatedly send and receive messages to accomplish a simple task. The system must include unreliable, unordered message delivery (messages may be delayed, duplicated, or lost) and nondeterministic scheduling of process steps. Each process maintains local state and may send messages containing its state or commands to others. The model should capture initialization, local computation steps, sending a message, receiving a message (if one is available), and termination conditions for processes.

The specification must state the safety properties that must always hold: (1) a global invariant expressing consistency between processes’ local states and their sent/received messages (for example, no process can claim to have completed a phase unless sufficient acknowledgements were received); (2) absence of invalid state transitions that violate application-level constraints (for example, a process cannot regress to an earlier committed phase). It must also state liveness goals: (3) eventual progress under fair scheduling and assuming a bounded number of message losses (for example, if a correct majority of processes keep taking steps and messages are eventually delivered, the protocol eventually reaches a stable decision); and (4) termination: processes that are not permanently crashed eventually stop in a decided state.

Include assumptions about faults: processes may crash (stop taking steps) but do not behave Byzantinely; message loss and duplication are allowed but channels do not corrupt payloads. Allow expressing optional stronger delivery guarantees (e.g., reliable FIFO channels) for alternative scenarios. Specify which properties should be checked as invariants versus those to be verified under fairness or liveness conditions.
