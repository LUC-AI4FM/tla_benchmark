Model a proof-management mechanism for a theorem-proving environment that supports nested assumptions, local declarations, and scope rules for introducing new names. The model should capture how proofs are structured into hierarchical proof steps, including ordinary assumptions, "NEW" declarations that create fresh local identifiers, and "SUFFICES" steps that change visibility rules. The system must enforce that NEW declarations are scoped to the immediate proof statement in which they are introduced (they cannot leak to sibling or parent proof steps), except that identifiers introduced by a SUFFICES step must be visible throughout the remainder of the current surrounding proof. The model should represent the process of proving a theorem by discharging subgoals, importing locally defined abbreviations, and checking that DEFINE'd names inside a subproof do not clash with or override outer definitions except according to the intended scoping rules. It must also handle nested proofs and nested SUFFICES steps, ensuring that QED closes exactly the block whose assumptions and local names it introduced. Key correctness properties: (1) Freshness — NEW introduces names that are distinct from any names visible outside its scope. (2) Locality — names introduced inside a proof step are not usable outside that step unless propagated by SUFFICES semantics. (3) Visibility of SUFFICES — names introduced by SUFFICES remain visible to the rest of the containing proof but not beyond it. (4) Definition isolation — local DEFINEs shadow outer names only within their intended scope and are restored after the scope ends. Provide invariants and temporal properties that assert these scoping and freshness rules hold for all proof structures and transitions.
