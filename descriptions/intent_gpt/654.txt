Model a light-client verification protocol that fetches and validates succinct proofs (light blocks) of blockchain headers from a set of known full nodes, where up to two-thirds of those nodes may be Byzantine (arbitrary/faulty). The model must express the normal sequence of progressive header verification from a trusted starting height to a target height, the client's clock and network timing uncertainties, and the possibility of faulty peers returning incorrect or inconsistent headers. The client should be able to probe multiple peers, request headers for specific heights, and apply verification rules that may require multiple agreeing responses to accept a header as verified.

Specify the following requirements and correctness properties:

- Safety: The client never accepts a header that contradicts the canonical chain established by at least one correct honest node; in particular, two headers accepted for the same height must be consistent (no conflicting finalized headers).
- Liveness under bounded faults: If a sufficient number of honest peers exist and communication eventually succeeds, the client eventually reaches and verifies the target height starting from the trusted height.
- Fault tolerance: The client must tolerate up to two-thirds of peers behaving arbitrarily (including equivocating, withholding, or sending malformed responses) without violating safety.
- Time and clock assumptions: Model local clock drift and a trusting period after which previously trusted state expires; verification must check time-based freshness constraints and reject stale proofs.
- Probing strategy: The client can adaptively choose peers to probe and retry; specify the minimal acceptance quorum behavior required to advance a height.
- Observability and verdict: The model should record whether the client decides the chain is safe, stalls due to insufficient honest responses, or detects equivocation attempts.

Do not prescribe internal implementation details; focus on the abstract interactions, assumptions about peers and clocks, and the above correctness properties to be verified.
