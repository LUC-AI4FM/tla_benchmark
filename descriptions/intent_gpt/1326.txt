Specify a model of a concurrent probe propagation protocol for a ring of N+1 nodes that repeatedly initiates and forwards a special probe token to detect and collect local information, subject to fairness and safety constraints.

Model the system as N+1 processes arranged in a logical ring. Each node can be in a passive or active state, can hold or not hold a probe token, and may have a local color/marking that can change when it initiates or receives a probe. The system should support periodic initiation of a new probe from any node and forwarding of an existing probe from a node to its clockwise neighbor. Probes must carry the identifier of their origin and may flip or record node colors as they traverse the ring.

The model must guarantee:
- Safety: At most one probe with a given origin identifier exists in the ring at any time; no two distinct active tokens with the same origin coexist.
- Token conservation: A probe is neither duplicated nor lost during forwarding — initiation increases the number of probes by one, forwarding preserves the probe count, and delivery to its origin completes the probe's round-trip.
- Termination/Progress: If a probe is initiated, under weak fairness of forwarding and initiation actions it eventually completes a full circulation back to its origin.
- Mutual exclusion property for critical sections: when a node holds the probe and is in its critical action (e.g., collecting data), no other node may be in that critical action simultaneously for the same probe.
- Liveness under bounded scheduling: the system cannot deadlock with active initiations enabled; continuously enabled forwarding/actions must eventually occur.

Allow nondeterministic choices for which node initiates, for timing of initiation vs forwarding, and for local color changes, but enforce the above invariants and liveness requirements. Provide parameters for N and for fairness assumptions so properties can be checked for different ring sizes.
