Specify a formal model of numeric computation that includes both integer and real-valued quantities and the operations on them, intended for reasoning about correctness of algorithms that manipulate real numbers. The model should treat reals as a distinct numeric domain with addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, ordering, and an abstract notion of infinity. It must permit expression of arithmetic identities and comparisons between integers and reals, and it must allow quantification over real values.

The specification must state what operations are available, their expected algebraic properties (for example closure where applicable, commutativity and associativity of addition and multiplication, distributivity, existence and uniqueness of multiplicative inverses for nonzero reals, behavior of division, and total order properties), and how integers embed into reals. It must also specify a representation of an element that is larger than every real (an abstract infinity) and its required monotonic relations with real values.

Key correctness properties to guarantee: arithmetic laws hold for real values as axioms used by proofs; comparisons between integers and reals are consistent with the embedding; division by zero is invalid or explicitly undefined; the abstract infinity compares greater than any real but does not behave like a real under arithmetic operations that would contradict ordering axioms. The model should be usable to state and verify higher-level algorithms that rely on real arithmetic without committing to a concrete numeric implementation.
