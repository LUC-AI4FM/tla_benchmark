Specify a real-time system that enforces bounded-response timing for an action: model a system with a global continuously increasing real-valued clock and one or more discrete actions that may be enabled and eventually taken. The model should capture the passage of real time between discrete steps and allow nondeterministic scheduling of the actions subject to timing constraints.

Requirements:
- Time is represented by a real-valued clock that can always advance to any larger real value; time does not decrease and does not stagnate forever.
- Whenever the specified action becomes enabled, the system must ensure the action executes within a given upper bound E on real-time delay from the moment it first becomes enabled.
- While the action remains enabled, the elapsed real time since it became enabled must never be less than a given lower bound D before the action occurs (i.e., the action cannot occur sooner than D after becoming enabled).
- The model must express these bounds existentially via a logical "timer" value: there exists a nonnegative timer that resets to zero when the action occurs or is no longer enabled, increases with real time while the action remains enabled, and is always bounded above by E and below by D when the action fires.
- Liveness should guarantee that time can always progress and that weak/strong fairness conditions ensure the action will eventually occur respecting the timing bounds when enabled.

State the properties to be verified: time monotonicity, nonZeno progress (time can always advance), the upper-bound response property (action occurs within E), the lower-bound delay property (action does not occur before D), and the existence and correct update behavior of the timer that enforces those bounds.
