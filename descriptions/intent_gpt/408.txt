Specify a simple concurrent-state transition system with two integer state components that start at 1. The model should allow stuttering steps as well as explicit state-changing steps, and must expose and check a variety of temporal observations about whether particular local or global actions occur. The specification must:

- Describe initial states where both components equal 1.
- Permit a transition that leaves both components unchanged (a stuttering step).
- Permit a transition that explicitly assigns both components to 1 (an explicit action that does not change values).
- Provide at least one transition that is syntactically a step on one component only, and at least one transition that cannot occur (an impossible action).
- Enable assertions or checks that distinguish between the existence of enabled actions, the possibility of performing an action as a single-component step, and the availability of steps that are observationally equivalent to stuttering.
- Express and check temporal properties asserting that certain actions are never possible on a trace, that certain impossible actions remain impossible, and that particular expected prints/notifications (or their abstract equivalents) must occur when conditions hold.
- Maintain a trivial invariant that always holds, so the focus is on temporal/observational properties rather than safety violations.

State the correctness requirements in temporal logic: which action occurrences must be impossible, which must be detectable when enabled vs. executed as stuttering, and that the specified notifications are reached on any fair execution that exercises the relevant branches.
