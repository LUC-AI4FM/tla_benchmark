Specify a formal model of a unidirectional ring leader election algorithm where a fixed set of processes, each with a unique numeric identifier, communicate only by sending messages to their clockwise neighbor. The model should treat processes as asynchronous: each process may act independently, messages may be delayed but not duplicated or lost, and communication channels preserve FIFO order. The system must allow any subset of processes to initiate an election simultaneously.

The model should capture messages that carry process identifiers and any local state needed to run the protocol. It must ensure that eventually exactly one non-faulty process decides it is the leader, and that every non-faulty process either eventually knows the elected leader or repeatedly participates in further elections consistent with liveness assumptions. Safety properties: no two processes may ever simultaneously decide different leaders; the elected leader must be the process with the maximum identifier among those participating in the election. Liveness properties: if message delivery is fair (every sent message is eventually delivered) and processes take steps fairly, then the protocol must eventually terminate with a leader elected and known to all non-faulty processes. Also specify correctness under multiple concurrent initiations and show that redundant messages do not create multiple leaders. Include invariants for message integrity (identifiers are preserved) and termination conditions.
