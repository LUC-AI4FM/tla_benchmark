Specify a formal TLA+ model of a single-element FIFO buffer implemented with two asynchronous rendezvous-style channels: an input channel that receives messages from a producer, and an output channel that delivers messages to a consumer. The model should treat messages as elements drawn from an abstract set and should not prescribe a particular encoding.

The system must allow the producer to place messages onto the input channel at arbitrary times, and allow the consumer to accept messages from the output channel at arbitrary times. The buffer must decouple producer and consumer: when the producer delivers a message to the input channel, the buffer may later accept it into its internal queue; when the buffer has an element available, it may place the element onto the output channel for the consumer to take. The channels should behave like single-slot handshake channels with separate ready and acknowledge signals, so sends and receives are synchronized by matching ready/ack states rather than atomic transfers.

Specify the initial states, the legal atomic transitions for the producer send, channel receive and send handshakes, buffer enqueue and dequeue operations, and the interleaving semantics. Require preservation of typing invariants (channels' fields and the queue contents), absence of message loss and creation (every message in the buffer or delivered on the output must have been previously sent by the producer), and FIFO order (messages are delivered to the consumer in the same order sent by the producer). Also require that the channels’ handshake protocol preserves mutual consistency (ready and ack alternate correctly) and that the model guarantees safety properties: no unauthorized state updates, and the queue length is always a finite sequence of messages.
