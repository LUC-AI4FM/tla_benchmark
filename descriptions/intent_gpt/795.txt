Model a finite-state discrete-time probabilistic process that starts in a single designated initial state and moves along transitions that carry rational probabilities. The system should record (1) the current control state and (2) the cumulative probability mass remaining for the single execution branch being tracked. Each step selects one outgoing destination from the current state according to that state's transition distribution and multiplies the tracked probability mass by the probability of the chosen transition. The model should stop exploring transitions out of a state once the state is in a specified set of absorbing or "accepting" states. The transition graph may contain cycles and branching, and transition probabilities from each state form a proper probability distribution (sum to 1). 

Specify correctness properties ensuring:
- The process begins in the initial state with full unit probability mass.
- At each non-accepting state with nonzero remaining mass, exactly one outgoing transition is taken and the mass is updated by multiplication with the transition probability.
- If the remaining mass becomes zero the process may continue but no further probability is transferred.
- Liveness: from the initial configuration, eventually either an accepting state is reached or the tracked probability mass becomes zero.
- Safety: probability mass never becomes negative and is always representable as a rational number derived by multiplying transition probabilities.
