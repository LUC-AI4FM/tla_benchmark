Specify a formal model of a system that manipulates and reasons about functions, function domains, product domains, and finite sequences (tuples) over integer and natural-number ranges, and that can perform assertions and enumerations over those sets. The model should capture the following capabilities and correctness obligations:

- Representation of total functions whose domains are specified by ranges, finite explicit sets, Cartesian products, or comprehensions; representation of function-value spaces built from expressions over integers and small finite sets.
- Correct computation of function domains for various function constructions, including lambda-like mappings, indexed function definitions, and nested multiple-index (Cartesian-product) domains.
- Accurate evaluation of membership tests for functions and tuples in function type sets and sequence-type sets, including boundary conditions (elements outside the specified range, wrong domain shape, or values outside value-type constraints).
- Precise counting (cardinality) of finite function spaces and domains, including function spaces over finite domains and product domains, and verification that cardinalities match expected combinatorial values.
- Support for filtering/comprehension over function spaces (selecting functions by predicate on values) and reasoning about the cardinality and domain of resulting sets.
- Ability to enumerate small finite function spaces and sequences and to assert or print elements for diagnostics.
- Safety properties: all membership, domain, and cardinality checks must hold as specified; any violation must be flagged as an assertion failure. Also require invariance of any unrelated state across transitions (i.e., no unintended state changes).

Do not commit to a particular implementation style—focus on modeling the mathematical sets, operations, and the correctness properties to be checked (domain equality/subset relations, membership/non-membership, cardinalities, comprehensions and product-domain behavior).
