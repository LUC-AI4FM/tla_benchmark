Specify a model of a single-lane bridge crossing system with cars approaching from two opposite directions. The bridge is narrow so vehicles can travel on it only in one direction at a time, and cars can occupy discrete positions (approach lanes, bridge positions, and exits). The model should support multiple cars on the approach and on the bridge, with a queue for cars waiting to enter. It must permit cars to move forward along their path, enter the bridge when allowed, and leave the bridge to the far side.

Requirements:
- Safety: Never allow two cars that travel in opposite directions to be on the bridge simultaneously. Never allow two cars to occupy the same bridge position at the same time. The number of cars on the bridge must never exceed its capacity.
- Liveness: Every car that reaches the bridge approaches should eventually be able to enter the bridge and eventually exit the bridge (no permanent starvation).
- Fairness: Progress of cars must not be indefinitely blocked by unfair scheduling; specify fairness constraints so that each enabled move of a car cannot be deferred forever.
- External behavior: Cars follow deterministic step-forward movement rules along positions; when a car would step onto the bridge while another car(s) are already on it, entry must be blocked unless they all move in the same direction.
- Environment assumptions: Cars are partitioned into two direction groups (left-bound and right-bound), approach and bridge have ordered position sets, and there is at least one car and at least one bridge position.

State what constitutes a legal initial state (cars start off the bridge at ends) and state invariants and temporal properties to check (safety invariants and eventual entry/exit liveness).
