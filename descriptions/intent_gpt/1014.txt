Specify a formal TLA+ model of a voucher lifecycle management system used by an issuer and multiple holders. The model should describe vouchers as identifiable tokens that may be created, transferred among accounts, redeemed for value, or cancelled. It must allow concurrent operations by different principals and model eventual termination of a voucher’s active lifecycle (either redeemed or cancelled). The system must enforce type and structural invariants: every voucher has a unique identifier, a valid owner (an authorized account or a special null owner when not assigned), a nonnegative remaining value, and a well-defined state drawn from a fixed set of lifecycle states (e.g., issued, active, redeemed, cancelled). Operations must only apply when preconditions hold (for example, only the issuer may create vouchers; only current owner may transfer; redemption requires sufficient remaining value and appropriate state). Safety properties to guarantee: uniqueness of identifiers, preservation of total supply accounting (no creation or destruction of value except by authorized issuance and redemption), absence of double redemption (a voucher cannot be redeemed twice), and ownership-consistency (the recorded owner matches transfers). Liveness properties to guarantee: issued vouchers that remain active can eventually be redeemed or cancelled under fair scheduling assumptions, and transfers do not deadlock progress of other independent vouchers. Specify these as invariants and temporal properties (safety as invariants; liveness as eventualities) suitable for model checking and theorem proving.
