Specify a formal model of a distributed termination-detection algorithm based on weighted acknowledgements (Huang’s algorithm). The system consists of a fixed set of processes with one designated coordinator (leader). Initially the leader is active and holds total weight 1; all other processes are idle and hold weight 0. Processes may become active and perform work, may send weight-carrying messages to other processes, and may receive such messages, adding their carried weight to their own local weight. An active process may also become idle: when it becomes idle it transfers its current local weight back toward the leader by sending that weight as a message and setting its own weight to zero. When the leader becomes idle it does not immediately declare termination; instead the leader must accumulate weight returned by messages. The algorithm must ensure conservation of total weight: the sum of all local weights plus the weights in transit always equals 1.

Model the asynchronous, reliable message-passing setting with arbitrary message reordering and arbitrary interleaving of process actions. Do not assume FIFO channels. Specify liveness and safety requirements: safety — once the leader detects termination (becomes idle and has accumulated total weight 1) then thereafter all processes must be idle and no messages may be in transit; liveness — whenever the system globally quiesces (all processes idle and no messages in transit), eventually the leader will detect termination. Additionally, include a practical state constraint preventing infinitely small weight fractions to avoid Zeno behaviors (e.g., bound the denominator). The specification should make no implementation commitments beyond these behaviors.
