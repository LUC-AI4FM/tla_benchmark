Model the classic Dining Philosophers system with N philosophers seated around a circular table who alternate between thinking and eating and who must acquire two shared forks (one on each side) to eat. The specification should treat philosophers and forks abstractly, with N a positive integer parameter and each fork being a shared binary resource that can be either free or held by a single philosopher.

Require that philosophers repeatedly attempt to eat: to eat a philosopher must acquire both adjacent forks, hold them while eating, and then release them. The model must allow concurrent attempts and interleaving of actions, and it must include fairness so each philosopher continually gets opportunities to progress.

Verify these correctness properties:
- Safety (mutual exclusion of neighbors): no two adjacent philosophers may be in the eating state at the same time (adjacent forks cannot be simultaneously held by different philosophers so that both eat).
- Absence of deadlock: the system should not reach a state where a set of philosophers are permanently blocked from making progress because of circular wait over forks.
- Starvation-freedom under fair scheduling: for each philosopher, it is always the case that eventually the philosopher will enter the eating state infinitely often (every philosopher makes progress repeatedly).
- Resource integrity: each fork is held by at most one philosopher at any time, and forks are returned to the free state after eating.

Do not encode a particular locking order; instead, specify the abstract acquire/release requirements and state the safety and liveness properties to be proven for all allowed behaviors.
