Specify a randomized algorithm that generates an outcome from a fixed discrete distribution on six labeled outcomes {1,2,3,4,5,6} by repeatedly tossing unbiased coins and following a finite-state decision process. The model should represent an initial "undecided" state and a finite directed transition graph whose edges are labeled by coin results; some nodes are terminal and correspond to one of the six outcomes. The process repeatedly samples a fair coin and moves along the edge determined by the toss until a terminal node is reached. At each coin toss the remaining probability mass assigned to the current undecided branch is halved. 

The specification must require termination with probability 1: eventually a terminal outcome is selected. It must also require correctness of output probabilities: the probability of terminating at each outcome equals the intended target distribution (given as rational dyadic numbers summing to 1). Probability reasoning should be explicit: track the residual probability mass carried by the current undecided path and show that when a terminal node is reached the accumulated mass yields the correct output probability. In addition, require a quantitative convergence property: for any positive epsilon, with probability 1 the algorithm reaches a terminal node before the residual mass falls below epsilon (equivalently, the residual mass converges to 0 only along nonterminating traces, which have probability 0). Do not prescribe a particular encoding of states or coin flips; focus on the behavior, termination, and distributional correctness properties.
