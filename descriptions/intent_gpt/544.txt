Specify a concurrent system of N identical processes that repeatedly perform a guarded increment of a shared counter under mutual exclusion, and capture its safety and liveness requirements.

Model N > 0 processes that share a single integer counter initialized to 0 and use a binary semaphore (or lock) initialized to the unlocked state to ensure mutual exclusion. Each process must, exactly once, acquire the lock, read the counter into a local temporary, compute the temporary plus one, write that value back to the shared counter, and then release the lock. Processes should not proceed past the release step. The model should permit any interleaving of different processes subject to the lock semantics (only one holder at a time).

Specify the following correctness properties:

- Safety: The semaphore must never be 0 for more than one process concurrently; at most one process may be in the critical section that reads and writes the counter. The shared counter must always equal the total number of completed critical-section increments performed so far (i.e., increments are not lost or duplicated).
- Liveness: Every process that begins its attempt to enter the critical section eventually completes its sequence (acquire, read, increment, release) — no process is permanently blocked by others (no starvation), and all processes eventually reach the terminated state.
- Termination: If all processes stop attempting further operations, the system may stutter but remains stable.

Do not prescribe an implementation; allow any lock-acquire/release protocol that enforces mutual exclusion, and model per-process local temporaries abstractly.
