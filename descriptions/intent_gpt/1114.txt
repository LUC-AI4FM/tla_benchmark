Specify a formal TLA+ model of a two-party TCP three-way handshake connection establishment between exactly two endpoints. Model the protocol behavior at the level of connection states, message transmission and delivery, and retransmission suppression, without committing to a particular implementation. The model should allow one endpoint to perform an active open and the other to do a passive open; the active endpoint initially sends a SYN and the passive endpoint initially listens and replies. The system must represent messages in transit and support non-deterministic message delivery and processing order, but must forbid duplicate identical adjacent messages in any single channel to model that retransmissions do not produce immediately repeated identical copies on the same link.

The model must ensure that after a finite sequence of message deliveries and appropriate local actions both endpoints reach the established connection state, provided the network does not permanently drop all required messages (i.e., liveness under fair delivery assumptions). It must guarantee safety properties: no endpoint transitions to the established state without the correct preceding handshake semantics (SYN → SYN-ACK → ACK), and no unexpected simultaneous multiple active open events occur beyond the initial scenario. Also require symmetry of the two peers so the specification is invariant under swapping their identities. Include invariants asserting valid per-endpoint TCP state progression, consistency of connection existence between peers, and absence of immediate duplicate messages on a channel.
