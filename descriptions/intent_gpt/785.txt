Specify a distributed termination-detection protocol based on weight distribution (Huang’s algorithm). Model a fixed set of processes with one distinguished leader. The system must track each process’s local activity state (active or idle), local fractional weight, and messages in transit carrying fractional weights. Initially only the leader is active and holds the entire weight (total weight 1); no messages exist. Processes may send part of their current weight to any other process (model a fully connected network), receive weight-bearing messages which make them active and increase their local weight, or become idle by sending their entire local weight back toward the leader and setting themselves inactive. The leader can also become idle and must absorb incoming weight-bearing messages to increase its weight.

Requirements:
- Conservation of total weight: the sum of all local weights plus weights carried by messages in transit must always equal 1.
- No assumptions about message ordering: messages may be delivered in any order; the network may buffer multiple messages per destination.
- Liveness: if all processes eventually become idle and all in-transit weights return to the leader, the leader should detect termination by becoming inactive and holding total weight 1.
- Safety: once the leader detects termination, the system must remain in a globally terminated state (all processes idle and no messages).
- Fairness: model fair delivery of messages and fairness of leader’s receive actions sufficient to ensure progress toward detection.
- Prevent pathological behaviors like infinite weight halving by bounding representation precision or denominators.

Express these properties as invariants and temporal properties (safety and liveness) to verify that the protocol correctly detects global termination without false positives and that detection eventually occurs when the system has terminated.
