Specify a concurrent mutual-exclusion algorithm for N asynchronous processes that use Lamport-style logical clocks and totally ordered timestamps to coordinate entry to a critical section. The model should represent processes that can repeatedly request, enter, and release the critical section, communicate by sending and receiving timestamped requests and acknowledgments, and maintain local logical clocks that advance on local events and on receipt of messages according to Lamport clock rules. The system must ensure that at most one process is in the critical section at any time (safety), and that any process that continually attempts to enter the critical section eventually succeeds (liveness/fairness under reliable FIFO-less message delivery and no process crashes). The specification should capture ordering of requests using timestamps and process identifiers to break ties, and ensure processes honor requests with smaller timestamps when deciding to enter. Model also the boundedness assumption for timestamps (e.g., clocks limited to a finite range) while preserving correctness. Key properties to assert: mutual exclusion, absence of deadlock (system does not get stuck with pending entries forever), and starvation-freedom (every requesting process eventually enters). Include invariants about clock monotonicity and message delivery semantics needed to prove the properties.
