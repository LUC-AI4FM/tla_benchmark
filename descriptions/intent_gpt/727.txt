Specify a TLA+ specification of a statistical test utility that evaluates goodness-of-fit between observed categorical counts and expected categorical counts using the Pearson chi-square test. The model should accept as inputs two finite nonempty sequences (or multisets) of nonnegative integer counts of equal length: observed counts and expected counts (or expected probabilities plus a sample size). The system must compute the chi-square statistic, derive the appropriate degrees of freedom, and compare the statistic to critical values for given significance levels to determine whether to reject the null hypothesis that observed counts come from the expected distribution.

Require the model to support both exact expected counts and expected probability vectors converted to expected counts by a sample size parameter. Ensure handling of edge cases: zero expected counts, small sample sizes, and inputs whose sums differ (with clear validation rules). Specify correctness properties: the computed statistic equals the sum over categories of (observed-expected)^2/expected; the decision to reject must be consistent with standard chi-square cutoff comparisons; degrees of freedom equals number of categories minus one (adjustable for estimated parameters); and comparison outcomes must be monotonic in significance level (if a distribution is rejected at alpha, it must be rejected at any larger alpha). Include invariants that inputs are valid and the algorithm returns a deterministic decision for any given inputs and alpha.
