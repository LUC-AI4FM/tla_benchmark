Specify a formal TLA+ model of a round-based Byzantine fault-tolerant consensus protocol in the style of Tendermint (leader-based rounds with PROPOSAL, PREVOTE, and PRECOMMIT phases). The model should treat processes as either correct or faulty, allow asynchronous message delivery (messages persist in per-round message sets), and represent proposers per round. It must capture the lifecycle of each correct process through rounds and phases, including sending and receiving proposals, prevotes, and precommits, updating local locks and valid values/rounds, and making a final decision on a value.

The specification must require that correct processes never send messages for future rounds beyond their local round, that correct processes do not equivocate (send two different votes in the same phase/round), and that proposers send at most one proposal per round. It must express quorum thresholds (two‑thirds and above‑two‑thirds) and require that any precommit by a correct process is justified by sufficient prevotes. It should record evidence of messages used to justify decisions.

State the main safety properties to be proved: (1) Agreement — no two correct processes decide different non-nil values when the number of faulty processes is less than one‑third of the system; (2) Inductive invariant existence — exhibit a global invariant combining type, no‑future‑message, non‑equivocation, locking, and quorum lemmas that is inductive under the protocol steps (unless a large faulty quorum exists); and (3) Accountability-style guarantee — when faulty processes are fewer than two‑thirds, either Agreement holds or any fork can be attributed to faulty behavior (equivocation or omission). Do not prescribe a concrete algorithm encoding; leave the model parameterized by N, T (fault bound), and quorum thresholds.
