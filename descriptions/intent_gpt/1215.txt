Specify a formal model of a temporal logic checker for a state-based system that enforces legal use of temporal operators and primed (next-state) expressions in specifications. The model should represent states and expressions, distinguish between state expressions (which refer only to current-state variables) and next-state expressions (which refer to primed variables), and capture typing rules that forbid mixing levels of temporal operators (like always, eventually, leads-to) with raw next-state references in contexts where they are not allowed. The checker must accept compositions where temporal operators are applied to state expressions or to properly actionified expressions, and must reject cases where a temporal operator directly scopes a next-state expression or where quantification ranges include primed expressions improperly. It must also validate allowed constructions: boxed temporal formulas under state variables, temporal modalities applied to legal predicates, and quantifiers over empty or state-only domains. Key correctness properties: (1) soundness — any accepted formula conforms to the allowed stratification of temporal and state/next-state levels; (2) completeness relative to the described rule set — all formulas that satisfy the stratification rules are accepted; (3) safety — the checker never accepts a formula that would require evaluating raw next-state expressions under pure temporal operators; and (4) determinism — the checker produces a unique accept/reject result for every input formula. Provide the formal model of these rules and proofs of the stated properties.
