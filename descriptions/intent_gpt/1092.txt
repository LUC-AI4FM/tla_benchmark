Specify a formal model of a cyclic molecular amplification process (similar to PCR) that manages pools of single-stranded templates, primer molecules, intermediate hybrids, various double-stranded forms, and a distinct small final product. The model should represent repeated temperature-controlled phases: a high-temperature denaturation that separates double strands into single templates and recovers primers from hybrids, a cooling/ready phase, an annealing phase where primers bind to templates forming hybrids (consuming primers and chosen counts of available templates), and an extension phase that converts hybrids into corresponding double-stranded products (consuming hybrids, creating longer double strands and the small final product), while advancing a cycle counter on each complete extend step.

Require these invariants and correctness properties:
- All counts are natural numbers and total mass is conserved across denaturation/anneal/extend transitions (no spontaneous creation/destruction beyond specified conversions).
- Primers cannot be overconsumed: annealing may only consume up to the currently available primer count and available single-stranded templates.
- Denaturation must release primers from hybrids and fully convert doubles back into appropriate numbers of single-stranded templates.
- The stage and temperature sequencing must enforce the intended order of operations (denature → cool → anneal → extend → denature …) so that anneal occurs only when ready and extend only after anneal.
- The cycle counter increments only during extension and accurately counts completed cycles.
- Safety property: before a specified cycle threshold (e.g., three cycles), the small final product count must remain zero (no premature product).
- Liveness: annealing, denaturation, cooling, and extension steps are weakly fair so the system cannot indefinitely avoid progress through phases.

Do not prescribe implementation details; allow nondeterministic choices for how many and which templates pair with primers during annealing, subject to available resources and mass conservation.
