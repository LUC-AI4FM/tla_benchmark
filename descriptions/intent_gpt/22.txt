Specify a system of a sender and a receiver communicating over an unreliable network using sequence numbers. The sender repeatedly produces messages, assigning each a strictly increasing sequence number. The network may deliver any subset of the sent messages to the receiver at any time and may reorder them; it may also drop messages indefinitely. The receiver buffers any messages it obtains from the network but must present (deliver) messages to its client strictly in sequence-number order: it may deliver message n only after all messages 0..n-1 have been delivered. The receiver may only deliver messages that were actually sent.

Model the concurrent behaviors of the sender, the network (non-deterministically moving arbitrary sent messages to the receiver), and the receiver’s delivery mechanism. Specify safety properties: (1) the receiver never delivers a sequence number that was never sent; (2) the receiver never delivers sequence numbers out of order and never skips a number when delivering (deliveries form a prefix 0..k of sent sequence numbers); and (3) the sender’s sequence numbers are monotonically increasing and unique per message. Specify liveness goals: every message that is sent should eventually either be delivered or persistently lost by the network (i.e., avoid claiming both "all sent are eventually delivered" and allowing permanent loss), and optionally specify a variant that requires eventual delivery of every sent message (reliable delivery). Also require invariants to relate sent, received, and delivered sets and sequence counters so model checking can validate safety properties.
