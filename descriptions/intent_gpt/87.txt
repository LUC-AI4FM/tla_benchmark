Specify a TLA+ model of the Chang–Roberts leader election algorithm for an asynchronous unidirectional ring of N processes. The model should treat process identities as distinct nonnegative integers and allow an arbitrary subset of processes to start the election (initiators). Communication is by point-to-point messages sent only to the clockwise neighbor; message delivery may be asynchronous but reliable (messages are neither duplicated nor corrupted and are eventually delivered unless the sender never sent them). Processes have simple local states (candidate, lost, won) and may buffer multiple incoming messages.

The model must capture the protocol behavior at a high level: initiators send their own identity into the ring; when a process receives an identity value it either forwards it, discards it, or adopts it according to the Chang–Roberts decision rule so that smaller identities suppress larger ones; when a process receives its own identity it declares itself leader. Do not fix particular scheduling fairness beyond requiring weak fairness for each process’s message-handling step so that enabled actions are not permanently ignored.

Formalize and verify these key properties:
- Safety: at most one process can ever be in the leader (won) state, any leader must be an initiator, and any other process must be in the lost state; if any non-leader was an initiator then its identity must be larger than the elected leader’s identity.
- Liveness: if at least one initiator exists, eventually some process enters the leader (won) state.
- Type and invariant checks: message buffers only contain valid process identities; local states and process identifiers remain within their declared domains.

Parameterize the model by N and by the mapping of process indices to unique identities.
