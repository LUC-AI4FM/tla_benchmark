Specify a formal model of arithmetic on dyadic rationals (fractions whose denominators are powers of two) and basic operations to construct, reduce, compare, and combine them. The model should represent dyadic values as rational numbers with integer numerators and positive integer denominators, but constrain the set of allowed rationals to exactly those whose denominator is a power of two. Provide operations to create the canonical zero and one values, to halve a dyadic value, and to add two dyadic values producing a reduced dyadic result. Ensure the model always maintains canonical reduced form (numerator and denominator with no common factor > 1, and denominators positive powers of two). Include a mechanism to compute least common denominators needed for addition while preserving the dyadic domain. Specify equality and ordering predicates for dyadic rationals and require that arithmetic operations respect these relations (e.g., addition is commutative and associative, halving yields a value exactly equal to dividing by two). State invariants that rule out non-dyadic or unreduced representations and that prevent zero denominators. Specify a set of properties for verification: closure under the operations, correctness of reduction, preservation of dyadic-ness, arithmetic laws (associativity, commutativity, identity elements), and absence of runtime errors such as division by zero or invalid intermediate denominators.
