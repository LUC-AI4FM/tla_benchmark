Specify a formal model of a parameterized module-instantiation and theorem-application framework. The system should represent a collection of modules that can be instantiated with specific parameter bindings, nested and reused many times, and that export definitions, operators, constants, and labeled theorems. It must allow referring to exported items of an instance (including labels inside theorems and nested instances) and applying those items in assumptions or hypotheses elsewhere. The model must permit multiple simultaneous instantiations of the same module with different actual parameters, local instantiations inside definitions, and references to labeled subparts of instantiated theorems or operators.

The model must ensure sound name resolution and scoping: references to exported operators, theorem labels, and nested instance members must resolve unambiguously according to lexical/module instantiation structure and parameter substitution. It must preserve the semantics of parameter substitution (actuals replacing formals) and of labels inside theorems when instantiated. It must detect and forbid invalid or ambiguous references. Correctness properties: (1) well-formedness — every referenced exported symbol or theorem label in an assumption or expression must resolve to a defined entity in some accessible instance; (2) substitution soundness — instantiating a module with given parameters yields expressions equivalent to textual substitution of parameters into the original definitions and labeled theorem bodies; (3) label preservation — labels inside theorems remain addressable after instantiation and refer to the corresponding subformula; (4) isolation — different instantiations do not interfere except via explicit exported names. Include support for numeric and tuple/list values as example data and allow asserting equalities about instantiated expressions.
