Specify a formal model of the classic "prisoners and switches" coordination problem with one designated counter and several non-counter prisoners. The model should describe an asynchronous sequence of prisoner visits to a central room containing two binary switches. Each visit, exactly one prisoner is chosen nondeterministically to act. Prisoners may read the two switch states and may toggle at most one of the switches according to their private protocol. The protocol must allow anonymous prisoners (all identical except the designated counter) to eventually enable the counter to deduce that every prisoner has visited the room at least once.

Requirements:
- There is one distinguished counter role; all other prisoners are identical and memory-limited (they may record a small finite state across visits).
- Non-counter prisoners may perform a bounded number of token-like contributions toward a global count by changing switches; they must not contribute more often than their allocated budget.
- The counter must only use the observable switch states and its local state to increment its internal tally when it detects valid contributions from others; the counter must not rely on external clocks or visit-order assumptions.
- Liveness: eventually the counter reaches a value proving that every prisoner has visited at least once, at which point a global "done" condition holds.
- Safety: no operation or sequence of actions may cause the counter to over-count (i.e., count the same prisoner more than its allowed contributions) or corrupt the protocol so that correctness cannot be determined.
- The model should support verification of both safety (no false positives) and eventual termination (the counter eventually recognizes all prisoners).
