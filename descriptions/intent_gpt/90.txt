Specify a concurrent coordination protocol for the classic "cigarette smokers" problem. The system has one dealer and several smokers. Each smoker possesses an unbounded supply of exactly one distinct ingredient type (for example: tobacco, paper, matches). The dealer repeatedly places on the table a nonempty subset of the ingredient types that is exactly all but one of the available ingredients—i.e., the dealer offers every ingredient except one. A smoker whose personal ingredient is the missing one may then take the offered items, assemble a cigarette, and smoke; while smoking the smoker should be considered "busy" and unavailable to respond to further offers. After a smoker finishes smoking, the table becomes empty and the dealer may make another offer.

Model the system at an abstract level (no implementation details like message buffers or step interleavings). The model must represent each smoker’s state (idle/ smoking), the current subset of ingredients on the table (possibly empty), and the dealer’s ability to choose any allowed offer. Specify the initial condition (no smoker is smoking and the table is empty or ready for an offer, per design choice). The correctness properties to guarantee are:
- Safety: at most one smoker is smoking at any time.
- Liveness: whenever the dealer places an offer that enables some smoker, some (enabled) smoker eventually starts smoking (progress/fairness).
- Persistence: a smoker who starts smoking must eventually finish (no permanent starvation while smoking).
Also assert that the dealer always offers only allowed subsets (exactly all-but-one) and that offers lead to table-emptying transitions only via a smoking action.
