Specify a model of the classical "coffee can" process as an abstract, nondeterministic state machine. The state should record only the counts of two kinds of indistinguishable items (called white and black beans). The system repeatedly chooses two items uniformly at random (or nondeterministically) and updates the counts according to these rules: if the two chosen items have the same color, remove both and add a single black item; if they are of different colors, remove both and return the white item to the container. The process continues until fewer than two items remain.

The specification must require (assume any reasonable finite upper bound on initial total count for model checking) and guarantee the following properties:

- Safety: every transition preserves that both counts are nonnegative integers and that the total count decreases by exactly one whenever an update is performed, until termination.
- Termination: repeated application of the rules must eventually reach a state with exactly one item remaining.
- Invariant: parity of the number of white items is preserved by every transition (i.e., white-count mod 2 is an invariant).
- Final-state characterization: the color of the single remaining item is determined solely by the initial parity of white items — even parity yields a black final item, odd parity yields a white final item.

Model the system abstractly enough to enable model checking finite instances and to support formal proof of the invariant and final-state claim for all natural initial sizes.
