Specify a formal TLA+ model of a distributed mutual-exclusion algorithm in which an arbitrary finite set of concurrent processes repeatedly attempt to enter a single critical section. The model should capture the protocol’s coordination state and any per-process flags or tokens used to indicate intent to enter or release the critical section, plus a shared selector or pointer that one process may change to influence which process is favored next. Processes repeatedly perform a noncritical phase, a protocol to acquire permission, a critical section, and a release phase that restores their flags and the selector appropriately. Model nondeterministic scheduling and nondeterministic choices of the order in which a process inspects other processes’ flags.

Require the following correctness properties:
- Safety (mutual exclusion): at most one process may be in the critical section at any time.
- Deadlock-freedom under weak fairness: if some process is trying to enter the critical section (i.e., it is in the entry protocol), then eventually some process (not necessarily the same one) will enter the critical section, assuming processes that can take steps are not permanently disabled.
- Distinguish starvation: do not require starvation-freedom (it is acceptable that a particular waiting process might never enter), but allow the model to exhibit starvation counterexamples.
- Liveness nuance for nonblocking remainder: allow processes to remain forever in the noncritical section; when modeling fairness, ensure fairness obligations do not force progress for processes that choose to stay in their noncritical section.

Do not prescribe particular variable names or implementation structure; focus on the abstract actions and the temporal properties the model must satisfy.
