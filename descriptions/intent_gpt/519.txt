Model a simple two-party message-exchange protocol in which one party (sender) repeatedly transmits data messages to the other party (receiver) and the receiver acknowledges receipt with a one-bit acknowledgment. The system must allow messages and acknowledgments to be buffered in FIFO channels of bounded capacity. Specify the abstract states of the sender and receiver (each holding a current data value and a one-bit tag) and the two channels (sender→receiver carrying data messages, receiver→sender carrying one-bit acknowledgments). Allow nondeterministic delivery and buffering subject to the capacity bounds.

The model must capture the following behaviors and requirements:
- The sender may send a message containing a data value paired with a bit; the receiver, upon receiving a data message, may accept it when its bit differs from the last-accepted bit and then update its stored data and bit.
- The receiver may send one-bit acknowledgments back to the sender; the sender updates its state upon receiving an acknowledgment whose bit indicates successful receipt.
- Messages and acknowledgments can be lost or delayed but cannot be corrupted; order within each channel is preserved (FIFO).
- Channel lengths must never exceed their specified finite capacities.

Specify correctness properties:
- Safety: the receiver never delivers the same data twice as a new delivery unless the sender has retransmitted it (no spurious duplicates), and the sender and receiver bits agree exactly when the latest data has been acknowledged.
- Liveness: every message sent infinitely often is eventually either acknowledged or superseded by a later message (no permanent starvation).
- Invariance: channel length constraints are maintained at all times.

Also include a refinement relation showing that a lower-level representation of messages (e.g., as tuples) implements the abstract message records, and assert that the concrete implementation satisfies the abstract system's specification.
