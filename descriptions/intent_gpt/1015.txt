Specify a formal model of a voucher lifecycle system in which each voucher progresses through a well-defined sequence of states from creation to termination. The model should treat the set of vouchers as given and, for each voucher, represent (1) its business state (e.g., not-yet-issued, issued/usable, consumed, or invalidated) and (2) the lifecycle-machine state that tracks where the voucher is in its issuance/use/closure process (e.g., not-started, active, completed). The system must allow these operations: issue a not-yet-issued voucher (moving it to the issued/active phase), allow transfers or other no-op operations while it is issued/active, allow redemption which consumes the voucher and completes its lifecycle, and allow cancellation which invalidates the voucher and completes its lifecycle. Once a voucher is completed, it must remain in a terminal consumed or cancelled state and its lifecycle machine must be in the completed state; once issued, it must not revert to the not-yet-issued state. Specify initial conditions (all vouchers start unissued and lifecycle machines not-started), the permitted state transitions, and that only one of the terminal states (consumed or cancelled) may hold when the lifecycle machine is completed. Require invariants asserting (a) that every voucher’s business state and lifecycle-machine state always belong to the allowed sets, and (b) that the pair of states for each voucher are consistent (e.g., issued must correspond to active lifecycle, not-yet-issued to not-started, and consumed/cancelled only when lifecycle is completed). Also state liveness expectations: issuing must be possible only from not-yet-issued, and redemption or cancellation must only occur from issued/active.
