Specify a model of the classic "coffee can" process: a finite container initially holding at least one bean, each bean being either black or white. The system repeatedly performs an operation that removes two beans chosen uniformly at random (or nondeterministically) and then updates the container according to the rule: if the two removed beans are the same color, both are discarded and a single black bean is placed into the container; if the two removed beans are different colors, the white bean is returned and the black bean is discarded. The process continues until fewer than two beans remain.

Model the state space (counts of black and white beans) and the transition relation induced by the two-bean selection and the deterministic update rule. Include an initial condition allowing any nonzero finite total number of beans.

Specify and verify the following correctness requirements:
- Safety: every transition strictly decreases the total number of beans by exactly one, until termination.
- Termination: the process inevitably reaches a state with exactly one bean remaining (no further two-bean selections possible).
- Invariant: the parity (evenness/oddness) of the number of white beans is preserved by every transition.
- Final-state characterization: prove that the color of the single remaining bean is determined solely by the initial parity of white beans — if the initial white count is even, the final bean is black; if odd, the final bean is white.

State that fairness assumptions may be used to rule out pathological infinite deferral of enabled transitions, and restrict to finite initial totals so the liveness/termination claim is meaningful.
