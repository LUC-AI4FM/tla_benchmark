Specify a distributed mutual-exclusion algorithm for N processes that coordinate access to a single critical section using message passing. The model should represent asynchronous processes that may delay, reorder, or drop messages subject to fairness assumptions. Each process repeatedly alternates between noncritical work and requesting the critical section; when granted access it enters the critical section, performs an atomic section of work, and then releases its hold. The system must ensure safety: at most one process is in the critical section at any time. It must ensure liveness under reasonable progress assumptions: if a process repeatedly requests the critical section and messages are eventually delivered, then that process eventually enters the critical section (no starvation). The model must capture the protocol’s use of request and grant-type messages (or equivalent tokens), show how processes decide to enter and leave the critical section, and represent possible transient failures of message delivery. Specify fairness conditions required to guarantee liveness. Include invariants that express safety, assertions that message buffers remain well-formed, and temporal properties asserting the absence of deadlock and eventual entry for requesting processes. Do not commit to a particular implementation technique; allow the protocol to be instantiated by a token, permission-by-quorum, or request‑acknowledgment scheme.
