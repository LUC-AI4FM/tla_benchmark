Model the one-step Byzantine-tolerant consensus subprotocol in which processes make a single broadcast of their proposal and then decide based on the collection of received votes. The model should treat processes as either correct or Byzantine and allow up to F faulty processes, with system size N and resilience parameter T (with N > 3T and T ≥ F). The protocol must allow every correct process to (1) propose an initial binary value (0 or 1), (2) send exactly one vote corresponding to its proposal, (3) receive votes from others (including possibly forged votes from Byzantine processes), and (4) decide a value or move to a non-decisive outcome if insufficient votes arrive.

Specify the state abstractly in terms of counts of sent votes by correct and faulty processes and of votes received by each process; do not model lower-level message delivery mechanics beyond these counts. The decision rule must require a process to have received at least N−T total votes, and to decide 0 (resp. 1) if it has at least N−T votes for 0 (resp. 1); if neither vote reaches N−T but total received ≥ N−T, the process should transition to a designated “undecided” state preserving its proposal.

Require safety properties: if all non-faulty processes propose 0, no correct process may decide 1 (and symmetrically for all proposing 1). Require liveness properties under weak fairness: correct processes that keep making progress in receiving messages and whose decision action remains enabled must eventually decide or reach a terminal state consistent with the vote thresholds. Also assert bounds: no more than F processes may become Byzantine and counts remain within 0..N.
