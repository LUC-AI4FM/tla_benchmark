Specify a formal model of a proof-tree coloring service used by an interactive theorem prover frontend. The system manages hierarchical proof trees whose leaves correspond to proof obligations; some leaves may have a single dummy obligation representing a missing or deliberately omitted proof. Each (ordinary) obligation records, for each available backend prover, a status drawn from that prover’s allowed status values. The model must allow an arbitrary number of provers with arbitrary finite status sets.

Model how logical colors are defined by predicates over obligation states. Each color predicate is one of two quantifier modes: “every” (true when every obligation in a subtree matches) or “some” (true when some obligation matches). Each predicate is defined by a finite union of state-sets; a state-set is either a dummy-state (missing or omitted) or a Cartesian product constraint on per-prover statuses (each coordinate is a specified subset or its complement, with empty meaning “all”). A predicate may optionally be restricted to apply only to leaf subtrees. A subtree satisfies a predicate precisely according to these semantics.

Specify how a proof tree is assigned a logical color: it is the lowest-index color whose predicate holds for that subtree, with uncolored as the default. Key correctness properties to guarantee: predicates are well-defined and decidable; every subtree deterministically receives at most one logical color according to the priority rule; dummy obligations behave as atomic states; quantifier semantics over sets of obligations are consistent with tree structure; and the representation supports enumeration and bijective indexing of all possible obligation states for use in compact predicate evaluation.
