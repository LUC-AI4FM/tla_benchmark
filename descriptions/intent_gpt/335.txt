Model a simple registration-and-licensing system that tracks calendar time and issues licenses based on age, where the stored year representation is limited to two decimal digits and therefore wraps every 100 years. The model should represent a population (at least one record) with fixed birth year values stored in two-digit form and an advancing global year counter that increments annually and wraps from 99 back to 0. The system must allow issuance of a license to a person once they satisfy a minimum-age requirement computed from the stored birth year and the current two‑digit year value.

Specify the system behaviors (initialization and state transitions) without prescribing an implementation: the year advances by one each step (with modulo-100 wrap), and license issuance is an enabled transition whenever the computed age meets or exceeds the license threshold. The specification should allow demonstrating the Y2K-type anomaly that can arise from two-digit years (for example, incorrectly computing age after a wrap).

State the key correctness properties to verify:
- Safety: No license is issued unless the computed age (under the two-digit-year arithmetic) meets the required threshold.
- Type and range invariants: year values remain in 0..99 and records maintain valid types.
- A liveness/possibility property: if a person reaches the threshold age according to an unambiguous absolute timeline that does not wrap, they should eventually be granted a license (or state clearly when this cannot be guaranteed because of wrap-around). 

Also request checking and reporting counterexamples that show how wrap-around can violate the intended safety or liveness expectations.
