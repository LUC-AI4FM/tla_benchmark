Specify a mutual-exclusion protocol for N concurrent processes that implements a fast mutex with spinning and back-off, and formally verify its safety and liveness properties.

Model a system of N identical processes that repeatedly try to enter a single critical section. Each process may execute a finite sequence of steps to attempt entry, including setting a local intent flag, writing and reading two shared "tour" variables that coordinate entry order, withdrawing intent and waiting (spinning) on other processes’ flags, and conditionally retrying the whole attempt. After successfully entering the critical section, a process must reset its shared indicators and restart the protocol loop.

The model must include:
- Shared state representing two coordination registers and per-process boolean intent flags; processes have local state encoding where they are in the entry protocol, waiting loops, or in the critical section.
- N as a parameter, allowing arbitrary positive number of processes.

Requirements:
- Safety (mutual exclusion): At no reachable state may two distinct processes be simultaneously in their critical section.
- Progress/liveness: The protocol must guarantee fairness so that if processes continuously attempt entry, then infinitely often some process will enter the critical section (system-wide starvation freedom). Optionally assert per-process eventual entry under weak fairness of process steps.
- Nontriviality: The model must permit arbitrary interleavings and must capture waits that depend on other processes’ flags and on shared register values, including the possibility to abort and retry entry.

Do not constrain implementation details such as specific variable names or exact control locations; specify behavior in terms of actions (setting intent, writing/reading shared registers, waiting until conditions hold, entering CS, and cleanup) and the above correctness properties.
