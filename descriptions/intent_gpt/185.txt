Model a concurrent system of two independent cyclic processes that each pick and add new integer tokens into disjoint sets, maintaining a global record of all tokens seen. The system should start with one specific token present in the first process’ set and a different specific token present in the second process’ set, and a simple finite-state control variable indicating which step each process is executing. Each process must be able to progress through a fixed small cycle of token-producing steps: at each step the process may produce a new token (a fresh integer) and add it to its own local set, advancing its control state according to its cycle; a process may also leave its control state unchanged. The two processes must operate concurrently and independently, with the global record always reflecting the union of their local sets.

Specify the allowed initial states, the per-process step transitions (token generation and control-state updates), and the update semantics for the global record. Require that the two local sets remain disjoint at all times (no token ever belongs to both local sets). Require liveness that each process can continue to make progress around its cycle indefinitely (no permanent deadlock of a process’ cycle). Also require safety: the global record equals the union of the two local sets after every step. Finally, require that token values are never removed (sets grow monotonically) and that new tokens are distinct from previously produced tokens.
