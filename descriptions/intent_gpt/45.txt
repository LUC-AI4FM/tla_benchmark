Specify a simple discrete-time counter system that starts at zero and, on each step, increases by either one or two. Model the state space, initial condition, and nondeterministic transition relation abstractly (do not prescribe an implementation). Require that every reachable state satisfies a nonnegativity safety property: the counter value is always >= 0. Require that the step relation always strictly increases the counter (every transition results in a larger numeric value). Also state two properties that must be shown false: one that claims the counter is always strictly positive (counter > 0) and one that claims the counter can remain unchanged on a step (next value equals current value). Specify liveness-irrelevant safety and action-level invariants separately: which assertions quantify only over states and which constrain allowed transitions. Require proofs (or checks) that the true invariants hold for all reachable states and transitions, and that the false assertions are violated (counterexamples exist). Finally, request that the specification be amenable to model checking and invariant-based proof: make explicit initial condition, transition relation, and the invariants to be verified, so that reachable-state analysis and counterexample generation can be performed.
