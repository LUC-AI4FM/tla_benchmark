Specify a TLA+ model of a blockchain light client protocol that synchronizes headers from a set of validators with possible faulty and drifting clocks. The model should express a network of multiple validators, some of which may be Byzantine and may produce incorrect headers or withhold data. Validators maintain heights; the client must attempt to fetch and verify headers up to a target height while relying on a trust root at a trusted height.

The specification should model time and clock drift: each validator and the client have local clocks that may drift relative to a reference clock; the model must allow the assumed maximum clock drift used by the client to differ from the actual drift experienced by some nodes. The client must enforce a trusting period for previously verified headers and must not accept headers that appear expired under the trusted time assumptions.

Capture the light client’s behavior for advancing the verified height: it should fetch candidate headers, verify signatures according to an honest-majority threshold, and perform time-based checks (trusting period and clock consistency) before updating its verified state. The model must allow retries, probing multiple validators, and handling missing or inconsistent responses.

Formalize correctness properties: safety (the client never accepts a header not justified by sufficient validator agreement or that violates trusting-period/time constraints), liveness (if a sufficient fraction of honest validators produce valid headers and clocks remain within modeled bounds, the client eventually reaches the target height), and resilience bounds relating to the maximum faulty fraction and clock drift tolerances. Include invariants expressing that accepted headers are monotonically increasing in trusted height and that the client refuses headers beyond trusting-period expiration.
