Specify a formal model of a simple reliable data transfer protocol between a single sender and a single receiver that uses an alternating single-bit sequence number and acknowledgments to tolerate lossy, unordered channels. The model should describe the sender and receiver behaviors and the two unidirectional unreliable channels (data channel carrying (bit,message) pairs and acknowledgment channel carrying bits). The sender repeatedly accepts new application data items, transmits a packet that tags the data with the next sequence bit when it believes the previous packet was acknowledged, and retransmits the last packet when it suspects loss. The receiver accepts incoming packets, delivers data to the application only when the packet’s sequence bit matches the expected bit (thus avoiding duplicates), toggles its expected bit on successful delivery, and sends an acknowledgment message with the received bit. Both channels may nondeterministically drop individual messages but do not duplicate, reorder or corrupt message contents beyond loss. Specify fairness assumptions sufficient to ensure progress when messages and acknowledgments are eventually delivered often enough (e.g., weak fairness for retransmission and acknowledgment-sending actions and strong fairness for packet and ack receptions). Key correctness properties: safety—no delivered application data is ever corrupted and the receiver delivers each application datum at most once; and liveness—every datum that the sender attempts to send infinitely often (or is repeatedly retransmitted under fairness) is eventually delivered by the receiver and eventually acknowledged, assuming fair delivery conditions. Also assert an invariant capturing legal system states (types/domains of queues, bits, and data) and show it is preserved.
