Specify a model of a distributed breadth-first spanning-tree construction algorithm that runs on an undirected graph whose nodes are fixed and whose set of edges is initially unknown and may be any subset of possible links between nodes. The model should represent asynchronous local actions in which a node may adopt a neighbor as its parent and update its distance-to-root estimate based only on neighbors’ current distance values. The root node must advertise distance 0 and never change parent; unreachable nodes must remain with a special “infinite” distance and consider themselves as having no parent. The system must permit arbitrary initial undirected topologies and must allow nondeterministic asynchronous choices of which node acts next.

State the correctness requirements to prove:
- Safety: at all times when no further actions are enabled, every node either is the root, or is unreachable (distance infinite and no neighbor with finite distance), or has a parent that is one of its graph neighbors and whose distance equals the node’s distance minus one; in particular parents must lie on a shortest-path tree rooted at the root.
- Liveness: eventually the system reaches a stable configuration satisfying the postcondition above (every reachable node has a parent and correct distance), assuming fair scheduling of enabled actions.

Also require type and structural invariants (distances are natural numbers with a designated “infinite” value large enough, parents are nodes, and edges are undirected pairs of distinct nodes).
