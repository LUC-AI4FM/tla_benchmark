Specify a formal model of a small, static mathematics-checking environment that can validate basic logical and set-theoretic identities over a finite domain of values. The model should expose a finite collection of named, distinct constant values and allow expressions built from Boolean values, natural numbers, and those constants. It must permit checking of propositional identities (e.g., implications, disjunction, conjunction, negation) over the two-valued Boolean domain, verifying tautologies and non-tautologies. It must allow construction and comparison of finite sets and multisets composed of the constants and numbers, supporting equality, subset, union, intersection, set difference, membership, and powerset. The model should be able to assert universally quantified properties over bounded domains (finite sets of booleans or explicitly enumerated subsets) and report whether each asserted formula holds. Key correctness properties: every asserted tautology over the Boolean domain must be provably true in the model; assertions that are not logical tautologies must be detectable as false for some assignment in the bounded domain; set operations must obey standard set-theoretic laws (e.g., idempotence and equality of sets ignoring multiplicity, subset relationships, and that the powerset operator yields exactly all subsets of the base set). The specification should avoid modeling unbounded quantification or choice and limit its universe to finite, explicitly enumerated values.
