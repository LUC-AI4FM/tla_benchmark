Specify a TLA+ model of a biochemical sample-processing pipeline that repeatedly prepares reaction mixtures by combining templates, primers, and DNA, then optionally removes or resets intermediate state between cycles. The model should represent a finite set of reagents and a single processing station that alternately assembles a reaction and performs a cleanup operation. Each cycle must nondeterministically select available template, primer, and DNA items to form a hybrid complex, record which reagent items were consumed or bound, and produce a reaction outcome. The cleanup step must be able to restore reagents to an available state or permanently consume items according to chosen policies. The system must allow concurrent logical steps only in the sense of atomic assembly and atomic cleanup; no two assemblies or two cleanups may overlap.

Formalize invariants that always hold: reagents counts remain nonnegative, no reagent is simultaneously marked both available and consumed, and any hybrid complex references only reagents that were available at the time of its creation. Specify liveness properties: from any reachable state where a template, primer, and DNA are available, eventually either a hybrid is formed or the reagents become unavailable; and the system does not deadlock (it can always progress by assembling or cleaning until reagents are exhausted). Also specify termination conditions when all reagents are consumed. Do not prescribe implementation details of selection or storage.
