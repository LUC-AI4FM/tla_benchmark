Specify a simple state machine that models a single integer-valued variable evolving over discrete steps under nonchanging (stuttering) behavior, and permit assertions that reference properties and subproperties of Boolean formulas. The model should include an initial condition that sets the integer to a concrete value and a transition relation that allows the variable either to stay the same or to change according to a specified deterministic rule. The specification must expose several derived Boolean predicates composed of smaller conjuncts (for example, a predicate that is the conjunction of two comparisons of the variable to constants, and a parameterized predicate that is the conjunction of two comparisons of a parameter to constants). The correctness requirements are:

- Safety: the system must maintain a global invariant that is always true in every reachable state (for example, the variable stays within some intended integer range or satisfies a designated conjunctive predicate).
- Preservation of predicate structure: it must be possible to assert and check both whole derived predicates and their individual conjuncts as separate invariants; the model should allow verifying that both the combined predicate and each of its constituent comparisons hold where intended.
- Well-formedness of assertions: the specification must make clear which assertions are syntactically valid properties of the state and which are ill-formed uses of subformula selection, so that a model checker can detect illegal references to subformulas while still checking legal invariants.

Do not constrain the exact implementation of transitions or the naming of derived predicates; focus on specifying the variable, initial condition, transition behavior, the derived conjunctive predicates, and the invariants to be checked (both whole predicates and individual conjuncts).
