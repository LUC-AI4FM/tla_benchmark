Specify a formal model of a distributed probe-based termination-detection protocol for N processes arranged in a logical ring (indexed 0..N-1). The model should represent processes that can be active or inactive and that may send asynchronous activation messages to other processes; processes also maintain a local color (white or black). A single circulating probe (token) travels counterclockwise around the ring; the probe has a position and a color. The protocol must allow processes to initiate a probe from a distinguished initiator (process 0) and to pass the probe along, with rules that the probe’s color is updated to reflect whether any process has performed a “work-producing” action (e.g., sent a message extending activity) since the probe last passed with a white color. Processes may spontaneously deactivate when they finish, and active processes may send messages to reactivate other processes. Messages may cause a target process to become active; sending a message should also potentially change the sender’s color to black if the message could generate future work.

Specify the following correctness requirements:
- Safety (soundness): if the initiator detects termination (the probe returns to the initiator white and certain local conditions hold), then all processes are actually inactive and no messages are in flight.
- Liveness (completeness): if all processes eventually become passive and no further messages are generated, then the protocol will eventually detect termination; also, if the system reaches global quiescence, the detection predicate will eventually hold.
- Fairness: model weak fairness for actions that move the probe and ensure progress of the probe circulation under enabled conditions so that detection is not prevented by unfair scheduling.
- Invariants: state and verify a ring-based invariant that ties probe position and colors to the absence of active processes beyond the probe or to the probe’s color indicating pending work.

Do not prescribe an implementation; instead specify the allowed system events (process activation/deactivation, message send, probe initiation, probe pass) abstractly, and prove the above safety, liveness, and invariant properties for all N > 0.
