Specify a model of the classic “coffee can” / bean-removal process. The system contains a multiset of indistinguishable black and white beans inside a container. Repeatedly, two beans are nondeterministically drawn at random (or chosen nondeterministically) and then replaced according to these rules: if both drawn beans are the same color, one white bean is removed and one black bean is added (so the total count decreases by one, with white count reduced by two and black count increased by one when the same color is white; when both are black, black count decreases by one); if the two beans are different colors, a single white bean is removed (equivalently, one black is removed and the white is returned), so the total count decreases by one. The process continues until termination when fewer than two beans remain. The model must enforce that total bean count never becomes negative and remains within a fixed upper bound provided as a parameter.

Specify the initial state, allowing arbitrary nonnegative counts of black and white beans whose sum is at most the given bound. Require fairness/termination semantics that allow the removal actions until the process reaches fewer than two beans.

State and prove (as safety properties) that:
- The total bean count strictly decreases by one on every removal step and therefore is always in 0..Max (type/arithmetical invariant).
- The system never produces negative counts for either color and the container contents always satisfy the declared type/finite-bound constraints.

Also state the termination property: eventually the process reaches a state with at most one bean (no further removal actions are enabled). Optionally assert the classical parity/result: the parity of the number of white beans is invariant (or the color of the last bean is determined by initial white-bean parity).
