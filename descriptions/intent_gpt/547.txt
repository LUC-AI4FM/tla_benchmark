Specify a parameterized mutual‑exclusion algorithm for N concurrent processes that use only a single Boolean flag per process and local indices to coordinate access to a single critical section. The model should allow arbitrary N ≥ 1 and must represent each process repeatedly cycling through: noncritical work, a protocol to acquire the lock, the critical section, and release. The acquisition protocol may read other processes’ flags and advance a local pointer to wait for other processes to clear their flags, and it may momentarily clear and reassert the process’s own flag to avoid livelock.

Require that the specification capture interleaving concurrency (asynchronous steps of individual processes) and the following correctness properties:

- Safety (mutual exclusion): at most one process may be in the critical section at any reachable state.
- Progress/liveness: if a process repeatedly attempts to enter the critical section and other processes eventually stop trying, then the requesting process eventually enters (no permanent starvation under weak fairness of others).
- Absence of deadlock: from any reachable state there is always some enabled step (the system does not get stuck with all processes blocked).
- Invariant conditions: every process’s flag is Boolean, local indices remain within 0..N, and processes only enter the critical section after completing the prescribed entry protocol.

Do not prescribe a particular program structure; instead describe the allowed observable operations (setting/clearing a per‑process flag, reading others’ flags, advancing a local index, waiting for a flag to become false, performing a critical action, and clearing state on exit) and require the specification to prove the safety and stated liveness/invariant properties for all N.
