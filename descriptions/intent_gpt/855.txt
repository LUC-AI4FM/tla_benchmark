Specify a distributed protocol for solving the “prisoners and switches” coordination puzzle with one designated counter and N > 1 prisoners. Model an asynchronous system in which at each discrete step a single prisoner is chosen (fairly, so each prisoner is chosen infinitely often) and taken into a room containing two independent binary switches. When a prisoner is chosen, she must perform exactly one atomic change to the switches (flip one switch or set a switch to a specified position). Prisoners may agree on a deterministic strategy ahead of time but cannot communicate or observe others except via the switch positions; they do not know who will be chosen next.

The specification should express allowed behaviors of all prisoners under the chosen strategy and require that:
- Safety: Any declaration that “every prisoner has visited the room at least once” may be made only when it is guaranteed true; a mistaken declaration must be impossible. In other words, whenever the protocol reaches the terminal signaling condition, it must imply that every prisoner (including the counter) has visited the room at least once.
- Liveness: Eventually the protocol reaches the terminal signaling condition (i.e., the counter can eventually conclude all prisoners have visited), assuming the fairness condition that no prisoner is starved.

Capture the key correctness invariants used to prove safety (such as an accounting relation between contributions made by non-counter prisoners and the counter’s internal tally) and state the liveness requirement separately. Do not prescribe implementation details like exact variable names or step-by-step actions; instead describe the allowed kinds of state updates (single switch flips or setting a switch) and the global guarantees that must hold.
