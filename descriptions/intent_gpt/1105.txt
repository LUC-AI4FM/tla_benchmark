Model an asynchronous, reliable-message-passing atomic commitment protocol for N processes that may crash (stop) and be suspected by a failure detector with the following properties: every crashed process is eventually and permanently suspected, and no noncrashed process is ever suspected. Each process initially has a binary vote (YES or NO). Processes send their vote to all, may crash before sending, and when nonfaulty they eventually send exactly one broadcast of their vote. Message delivery is asynchronous but reliable: messages sent by noncrashed processes are eventually delivered to noncrashed recipients, although crashed senders may have sent at most one unpredictable message. Processes decide either COMMIT or ABORT (or may crash and stop). The model should be parameterized by N and by the initial distribution of votes.

Specify the protocol’s allowed behaviors abstractly (no specific code): sending a vote, delivering individual vote messages to recipients, processes crashing (which immediately makes them permanently suspected), and local transitions to decide COMMIT or ABORT based only on local knowledge of received votes and suspicions.

Require and formally check these correctness properties:
- Agreement: no two nonfaulty processes decide different outcomes (no commit vs abort conflict among correct processes).
- Abort validity: if any process initially votes NO, then no process may decide COMMIT.
- Commit validity: if all processes initially vote YES and no process is suspected at that time, then no process decides ABORT (and no crashes occur thereafter).
- Termination under stability: if no further crashes occur and failure detector stops suspecting any noncrashed process, then every noncrashed process eventually decides (COMMIT or ABORT).
Also state appropriate type invariants (counts within bounds, process states drawn from allowed values) and include weak fairness for liveness of enabled actions (sending, delivery, and decision) so as to permit verification of the liveness properties.
