Specify a TLA+ model of Dijkstra’s distributed termination-detection algorithm for a unidirectional ring of N processes. The model should abstract the computation and the termination-detection protocol separately: processes perform local work and may send activation messages to other processes; independently, a circulating token is used by a monitoring protocol to detect global quiescence. The system must allow processes to be active or passive, to send an activation message that can turn another process active, and to become passive at any time. Activation messages may be sent in either ring direction; sending an activation in the direction opposite the token’s travel must mark the sender so it can stain the token. The token circulates around the ring in a fixed direction; a process that holds the token may pass it to its predecessor, possibly changing the token’s color according to the process’s mark. One distinguished process may initiate a probing round by sending a white token when appropriate. The model must capture interleaving of message sending, local computation (becoming passive), and token-passing, and include weak fairness for the token-passing mechanism.

State the correctness properties to verify: (1) Safety — if the token arrives white at the distinguished process with that process passive, then all processes are passive (termination has been correctly detected); (2) Liveness — if every process eventually becomes and stays passive, then eventually the protocol will detect termination by producing the white token at the distinguished process while it is passive; (3) an inductive invariant that characterizes the relation among token position, token color, process marks, and activity status sufficient to prove the safety property. Do not prescribe a particular encoding of variables or procedural steps.
