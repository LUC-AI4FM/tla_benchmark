Specify a formal model of the polymerase chain reaction (PCR) process that captures its cyclical thermocycling and mass-balance of nucleotide-containing species, without prescribing a particular implementation. The model should represent three temperature phases (high denaturation, lower annealing, intermediate extension) and allow repeated cycles that move the system through those phases. It must track counts of: intact double-stranded target molecules, single-stranded templates, primer molecules of the two complementary types (or, if simplified, a single pooled primer count that is consumed in annealing), and primer–template hybrids. The model should allow annealing to consume primers and templates to form hybrids (bounded by availability), extension to convert hybrids into new double strands, and denaturation to break double strands and hybrids into single strands and release any unbound primers. It must enforce conservation of molecular material (a linear invariant equating the weighted sum of species to the initial total), prohibit negative counts, and restrict temperature to the specified phases. Key correctness properties: (1) safety — the conservation invariant and nonnegativity always hold; (2) eventual behavior — the system can perform infinite cycles (liveness of cycling) and, under sufficient initial primers and templates, eventual production of additional double-stranded product occurs; (3) resource depletion — if primers are limited, the model should be able to exhibit primer depletion and the consequences for further amplification. Do not fix implementation details such as step ordering or action encoding; allow nondeterminism for how many annealments occur per cycle as long as they respect availability constraints.
