Specify a formal model of a round-based Byzantine-fault-tolerant consensus protocol in an asynchronous setting inspired by Tendermint-style voting. The model should represent a fixed set of processes, partitioned into correct and faulty ones, that proceed through numbered rounds. In each round, processes may broadcast three kinds of messages: proposals, prevotes, and precommits. Correct processes follow the protocol rules to decide on a single value, while faulty processes may send arbitrary messages subject only to being from the allowed message types. The system must allow injection of a bounded number of faulty messages at startup or during execution to model adversarial behavior.

The specification must capture the protocol’s liveness intent: eventually correct processes should progress through protocol steps and reach decisions when enough timely messages arrive; it must also capture safety: no two correct processes may decide different non-nil values. Model the rules that govern when a correct process advances rounds, locks a value, sets a valid value, and issues prevotes and precommits based on quorum thresholds (supermajorities). Include the notion of evidence for faulty behavior and how it affects future behavior.

Formal properties to verify: agreement (no two correct processes decide distinct values), validity (decided value must have been proposed by some process), and eventual decision (under reasonable network assumptions or absence of further faults, all correct processes eventually decide). Also provide reachability checks that detect stalled progress (e.g., no prevote or no precommit occurring) and permit experimenting with varying numbers of injected faulty messages.
