Specify a formal TLA+ model of a replicated-state-machine consensus protocol operating among a small set of nodes with at most one Byzantine (arbitrary) faulty node. The model should abstract rounds and leader rotation, quorums and blocking sets, and a notion of proposal logs at each correct replica. The system must allow a Byzantine node to behave arbitrarily while ensuring that the correct nodes follow the protocol rules.

The model must express the progression of rounds, leader selection by round number, and the communication assumptions captured by quorums and blocking sets (quorums must intersect pairwise in at least one correct node; every blocking set must intersect all quorums and contain a correct node). The protocol should support proposal creation, voting/acknowledgement, and log appends; it must allow replicas to advance rounds and commit proposals when they have sufficient support from a quorum.

State invariants to guarantee:
- Safety (consistency): no two correct replicas commit conflicting proposals for the same log position/round.
- Liveness under eventual synchrony: if leaders remain live after GST, the protocol eventually commits new proposals proposed by correct leaders.
- Fault-tolerance: one Byzantine node cannot cause the intersection properties to be violated; quorums of correct nodes suffice to make progress and to prevent equivocation from causing inconsistent commits.
- Termination bound: under the model’s finite round horizon, correct replicas will eventually stop advancing when rounds max out.

Include properties to check both that progress can be made (some correct replica appends multiple entries) and that particular leaders can cause commits, while enabling exploration of Byzantine behaviors that should not violate safety.
