Model a simple two-party, asynchronous message-exchange protocol in which each participant must send exactly one initial greeting to the other and then terminate. The system should represent distinct participants and directed communication channels between every ordered pair of distinct participants. Initially, all channels are empty and every participant is in an initial state. In each step, any participant that has not yet completed its action may non-deterministically choose the other participant as a destination and place a single one-message “hello” into the corresponding outgoing channel, then move to a completed state. Once a participant is completed it does not perform further actions. The model must allow interleaving of actions and not assume synchronous or atomic multi-party steps. Specify fairness so that if a participant remains enabled to send its message forever, it is eventually chosen to act. Formalize the safety requirement that channels only ever contain messages explicitly sent (no spurious messages), and that participants do not send more than one greeting. Formalize the liveness requirement that eventually all participants reach the completed state (the system terminates). Provide invariants and temporal properties sufficient to prove both safety and eventual termination under the stated fairness assumption.
