Specify a model of the dining philosophers problem for N concurrent philosophers seated around a round table with one shared fork between each adjacent pair. Each philosopher alternates between thinking and being hungry (attempting to eat). A philosopher may eat only when they possess both adjacent forks and both forks are in a state that permits eating. Forks may be held by one philosopher at a time and can be passed directly between neighboring philosophers; passing a fork may change its state (for example, marking it clean or dirty) according to the protocol rules. The model should allow philosophers to: request or receive forks from neighbors, hand forks to neighbors, change fork state when eating or when giving up a fork, and transition between thinking and hungry/eating states.

Require the following correctness properties to be specified as temporal formulas:
- Safety: No two neighboring philosophers may be in the eating state simultaneously (mutual exclusion on shared forks).
- Invariants: Forks are always held by exactly one of the two adjacent philosophers; all variables respect their intended types/ ranges.
- Liveness: Under fairness assumptions for philosopher processes, every philosopher who becomes hungry will eventually eat (no starvation).
- Progress: The system must always be able to make a next step (no global deadlock), assuming the protocol rules and fairness.

Do not prescribe a particular implementation; leave the protocol choices abstract enough to allow modeling the Chandy–Misra style token/dirty-fork approach or equivalent fork-passing protocols that satisfy these properties.
