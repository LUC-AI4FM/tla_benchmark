Specify a model of the Chang–Roberts leader election algorithm running on a unidirectional ring of N deterministic processes with unique numeric identities. The model should allow any subset of processes to initiate the election concurrently by sending their own identity as a candidate message to their clockwise neighbor. Processes forward candidate messages, compare incoming candidate IDs with their own identity, and withdraw candidacy when they receive a smaller ID; when a process receives its own ID back, it declares itself leader.

Abstract the communication as asynchronous message delivery on the ring (messages may be in transit and can be buffered per recipient). Do not fix message ordering beyond per-recipient buffering. Model process state only in terms of whether it is currently a candidate, has lost, or has won, and the messages pending for each process. Allow all processes to run fairly.

Require the following correctness properties:
- Safety: If a process declares itself leader, it must have been an initiator and must hold the globally smallest identity among initiators; all other processes must have the “lost” status at that point.
- Uniqueness: At most one process may ever declare itself leader.
- Termination/Liveness: If at least one initiator exists, eventually some process declares itself leader.
- Progress fairness: Under weak fairness for each process step, election completes (no livelock).

Parameterize the model by N and the sequence of unique process identities. Provide invariants and temporal properties expressing the above safety, uniqueness, and liveness requirements, but do not prescribe a particular implementation encoding.
