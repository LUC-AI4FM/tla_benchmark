Specify a formal TLA+ model of a small distributed clock synchronization subsystem that runs on three distinct processes. Model each process as maintaining a local integer clock and periodically exchanging timing information with the other processes. The system must track the pairwise drift between clocks (difference in reported clock values) and allow updates to clocks based on the information received so each process can correct its local time. The model should permit arbitrary but finite nondeterministic message delays and asynchronous independent clock advances, while preventing unbounded message loss—messages that are sent are eventually received. The specification must express safety properties: (1) pairwise clock drifts remain bounded by a fixed known constant at all times, and (2) updates preserve monotonicity of each local clock (a clock never decreases). It must also express liveness: given repeated exchange and correction steps, the maximum drift across all pairs eventually falls below any larger threshold than the bound. Include assumptions about initial clock values and environment behavior (message delivery fairness and bounded local step size). Do not prescribe a particular correction algorithm; instead allow nondeterministic choices for update amounts constrained by physical limits (maximum adjustment per step).
