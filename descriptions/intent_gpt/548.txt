Specify a formal model of an N-process mutual exclusion algorithm based on hierarchical entry levels and a "last-to-enter" pointer for each level (an extension of Peterson-style algorithms to N processes). The model should abstract the system as N identical concurrent processes that repeatedly cycle through: noncritical section, a multilevel entry protocol that grants progressively stricter priority at levels 0..N-2, a critical section, and exit which relinquishes entry claims. At each entry level a process announces its level, records itself as the last to enter that level, and checks other processes: it must wait for conflicting processes at the same or higher level unless either those processes have left (no level) or the last-to-enter for the level has changed. The entry protocol must ensure only one process may be in the critical section at a time (mutual exclusion), and every process that attempts to enter a finite waiting period (no starvation / eventual progress) provided other processes do not continuously reenter indefinitely (weak fairness / progress assumption). Model should allow arbitrary N>0 and treat processes symmetrically. Key properties to verify: safety (mutual exclusion), absence of deadlock (system cannot reach a state where no progress is possible while some process wants to enter), and a liveness property such as weak fairness or bounded waiting ensuring a requesting process eventually enters when other processes make only finite intervening attempts. Provide states for announced levels and last-to-enter pointers, and model nondeterministic interleaving of steps.
