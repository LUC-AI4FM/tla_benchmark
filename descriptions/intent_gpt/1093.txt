Specify a formal model of a simple staged reaction system that manipulates three nonnegative integer quantities representing available substrate, available catalyst, and bound complex. The system evolves in discrete steps; each step performs exactly one of a small set of atomic stage types: (1) a heating stage that leaves counts unchanged except possibly satisfying pre/postconditions of the stage; (2) a cooling stage that similarly may change state according to its intended semantics; (3) an annealing stage that transfers some nonnegative amount from both substrate and catalyst into the bound complex simultaneously; (4) an extension stage that models growth of the bound complex; or (5) a no-op stage that leaves all three counts unchanged. The annealing stage must choose a nonnegative integer amount k not exceeding the smaller of the current substrate and catalyst counts, then decrease both substrate and catalyst by k and increase the bound complex by k. All counts must remain in the natural numbers at all times.

Model initial conditions allowing arbitrary natural-number values for the three quantities subject to any declared constant bounds. Specify a safety invariant ensuring that every reachable state satisfies the natural-number typing constraint for all three quantities. Also specify progress/step constraints that each action is executable only when its preconditions hold and that state updates preserve the invariant. Do not encode implementation details; state only the allowed stage kinds, their required effects, and the invariant properties the model must guarantee.
