Specify a model of a simple two-counter concurrent system that evolves in discrete steps. The system maintains two integer-valued counters and, at each step, performs one of two independent update operations: one operation increments or decrements the first counter by an arbitrary integer while leaving the second unchanged; the other operation increments or decrements the second counter by an arbitrary integer while leaving the first unchanged. Also record which operation was executed at each step (as an observable label).

The specification must capture initial conditions (both counters start at zero and the last operation label indicates initialization) and the nondeterministic choices of integer deltas for each operation. It must allow unbounded execution sequences.

State the safety requirement that the sum of the two counters always remains strictly less than a fixed bound (10). Additionally, express and check simple local nonnegativity properties for views of the state: both counters should be nonnegative in certain observable views (for example, at the start and for at least one subsequent state), and the operation label should be observable as part of the trace.

Provide trace properties that combine initial-state constraints and eventual safety: allow traces that exhibit a specific two-step scenario (for instance, reaching a particular counter value in one step) or otherwise guarantee that the final state of any finite trace satisfies the sum-bound invariant. Offer variants of such trace invariants that require the nonnegativity of counters across the first two (or first three) states unless the final-state sum-bound holds.

Do not prescribe implementation details such as how nondeterminism is encoded or the exact syntactic structure; focus on the states, allowed transitions, observables, and the safety/trace properties to be verified.
