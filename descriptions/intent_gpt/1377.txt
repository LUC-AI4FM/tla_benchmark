Specify a concurrent system that models a client repeatedly performing an operation that may either complete successfully or be rejected. The system must capture an unbounded sequence of trials where each trial ends in exactly one of two outcomes: success or rejection. Between trials, the client may immediately attempt another trial or stop. The model should allow nondeterminism in outcome and in the decision to continue or stop, and should permit an arbitrary finite number of trials.

Require that once a given trial has produced an outcome, that outcome is final for that trial (no rollback or retroactive change). Ensure the system does not deadlock: from any reachable state where the client has not chosen to stop, it must be possible to progress to a next trial outcome or to stop. Capture liveness properties that guarantee that if the client continually chooses to attempt trials, then infinitely many trial outcomes will eventually occur (no starvation).

Specify safety properties: (1) mutual exclusivity of outcomes for each trial (success and rejection cannot both hold for the same trial); (2) no undefined intermediate outcome states persist as a terminal state. Provide an invariant asserting the structure of the sequence of trials and a temporal property expressing the guaranteed eventual occurrence of outcomes under continued attempts.
