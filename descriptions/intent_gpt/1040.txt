Specify a formal TLA+ model of Dijkstra’s original mutual‑exclusion algorithm for an arbitrary finite set of concurrent processes. The model should describe processes that repeatedly attempt to enter a critical section, execute it, and then return to a non‑critical remainder. Each process may read and write shared Boolean flags and a shared selector variable used to suggest which process should next be favored.

The specification must capture the intended behavior (not an implementation): processes may loop trying to enter the critical section, can examine other processes’ published state in arbitrary order, and may update the shared selector to influence contention. Processes must be allowed to make nondeterministic choices about the order in which they check others. The model should not assume fairness beyond what is explicitly required for particular liveness properties.

Require and prove these correctness properties:

- Mutual exclusion: no two distinct processes are ever simultaneously in their critical sections.
- Deadlock freedom (weak form): whenever some process is trying to enter the critical section, eventually some process (not necessarily the same one) will enter a critical section, under the usual fairness assumption that a continuously enabled process action is not perpetually ignored.
- Deadlock freedom (strong form): whenever any process is in the trying region (i.e., not in the final non‑critical state), eventually some process will enter the critical section, taking into account that processes may legitimately remain forever in the non‑critical remainder.
- Starvation characterization: specify the property of starvation freedom (each trying process eventually enters), and show whether the algorithm satisfies or violates it.

Also specify initial conditions (all flags in the initial state set to indicate non‑interest) and allow parameterization by the set of processes. Include invariants and temporal properties suitable for model checking with TLC and for proving with TLA+.
