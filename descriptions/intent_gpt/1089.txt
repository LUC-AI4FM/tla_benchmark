Specify a formal model of a staged biochemical protocol that repeatedly processes DNA templates with primers across discrete cycles. The model should capture a population of template molecules and a population of primer molecules, structured into distinct molecular states representing whether a template is free, bound by a primer (hybridized), or converted into an amplified product. Each cycle must perform the sequence of logical stages: primer binding to available templates, extension of bound primers into new DNA strands, and reset or cleanup steps that prepare for the next cycle. The system should allow nondeterministic choices for which specific templates or primers interact, subject to conservation of molecules (no spontaneous creation or loss except by explicit extension actions) and limited resource constraints (primers can be consumed or recycled according to the protocol rules). Specify the initial conditions for templates and primers and the control of discrete stage progression within a cycle.

State the key correctness properties to verify: safety (a primer binds only to a compatible template and a template is not concurrently bound by more than one primer), conservation (total counts reflect only allowed transformations), progress (every started cycle eventually completes all stages), and liveness of amplification (if primers and templates remain available across cycles, then new amplified products are eventually produced). Do not prescribe implementation details of stage sequencing—only the required effects and invariants.
