Specify a formal model of a connection-establishment handshake similar to TCP's three-way handshake between an initiator and a responder. The model should describe two principals that exchange messages to transition from initial idle/listening states to an established connection. Capture that messages carry at least these fields: a sequence number, an acknowledgment flag, a SYN flag, and an acknowledgment number. Sequence and acknowledgment numbers are nonnegative integers and may be chosen nondeterministically when sending. The model must allow messages to be created and placed into an abstract network (a bag or set) and later observed by the other side; message delivery may be reordered and duplicated but not altered in-transit.

Specify the allowed state transitions for each principal at a high level: the initiator may send an initial SYN with a chosen sequence number and advance to a SYN-sent state; the responder, upon seeing a SYN, may send a SYN+ACK that carries its own sequence and an acknowledgment for the received SYN and move to SYN-received; the initiator, upon seeing a SYN+ACK, may send a final ACK and move to ESTABLISHED; the responder, upon seeing that ACK, may move to ESTABLISHED. Require that once the responder is established, the initiator must also be established (safety property). Also require a liveness-style guarantee that termination to both-established is reachable when messages are delivered (i.e., there exists a run where a handshake completes). Do not prescribe implementation details such as specific variable names or the mechanism for nondeterministic choice.
