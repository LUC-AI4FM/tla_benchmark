Specify a formal model of the transitive closure of a binary relation and prove key equivalences between different characterizations. The model should treat a relation as a set of ordered pairs over some carrier and must be able to express the following notions and guarantees without prescribing a particular implementation:

- Define the support of a relation (the set of elements that appear in some pair) and treat the transitive closure as a relation on that support.
- Provide multiple independent characterizations of transitive closure and state their intended equivalence:
  - A path-based characterization: two elements are related iff there exists a finite nontrivial path of edges connecting them.
  - An iterative bounded-path characterization suitable for finite supports: existence of a connecting path whose length is bounded by the size of the support.
  - An algebraic closure via repeated relation composition and union: the union of R, R∘R, R∘R∘R, ... up to the support size.
  - A recursive doubling/closure method that repeatedly composes the relation with itself until saturation.
  - A node-elimination (Warshall-like) characterization that incrementally permits intermediate vertices.
- Require correctness properties:
  - Each characterization must yield a relation that is transitive and contains the original relation.
  - For finite relations, all characterizations must be provably equal to one another and equal to the minimal transitive superset (the standard transitive closure).
  - The support of the closure must equal the support of the original relation.
- Allow an alternate representation where relations are Boolean-valued predicates on pairs; provide a corresponding iterative construction and prove it matches the set-based definitions on finite supports.

Do not prescribe algorithmic steps; specify only the mathematical properties, invariants, and equivalence theorems that the model must establish.
