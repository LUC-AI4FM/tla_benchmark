Specify a formal model of an asynchronous point-to-point communication channel used between a single sender and a single receiver. The channel should hold at most one message at a time and allow the sender to present a datum and the receiver to consume it, with explicit handshake signals for readiness and acknowledgment. The model should capture the following behavior and requirements:

- The channel stores either no datum or exactly one datum from a fixed finite set of payload values.
- The sender can place a datum into the channel only when it indicates it is ready to send; placing a datum changes the channel state to contain that datum and updates a send-ready signal appropriately.
- The receiver can take a datum from the channel only when the channel contains a datum and the receiver indicates readiness to accept it; taking the datum clears the channel and updates an acknowledgment signal.
- The handshake signals must correctly reflect transitions: the sender can detect whether the receiver has acknowledged the last transmitted datum, and the receiver can detect whether a new datum is present and whether it is fresh.
- Allow interleaving of sender and receiver actions, including repeating sends and receives, and arbitrary finite delays between actions.
- Safety properties: the channel must never contain two different data items simultaneously; the receiver must not consume a datum that was never sent; and acknowledgments must correspond to actual deliveries.
- Liveness properties: any datum offered by the sender while the receiver is continuously ready should eventually be consumed; if the receiver is continuously attempting to receive, it should eventually either obtain a datum or observe that none is present.

Do not commit to a specific implementation protocol; leave the model free to represent handshake signals and state abstractly so multiple sender/receiver scheduling behaviors can be explored.
