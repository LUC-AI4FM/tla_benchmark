Specify a formal model of a mapping between regions of a source specification language and regions of generated code. The model should represent a linear sequence of annotated elements that mark (a) delimiters that open and close nested source-language syntactic units, (b) tokens that identify specific source-language regions considered "important", and (c) explicit separators that indicate non-adjacent successive units. The system must enforce that delimiters are properly nested and balanced, that token regions appear in strictly increasing source order, and that the matching open/close delimiters enclose tokens and other delimiters consistently with nesting depth. When two top-level sibling syntactic units are intended to be successive in the generated code, they must be considered adjacent unless an explicit separator appears between their enclosing delimiters; such separators carry a numeric nesting-level that indicates how many surrounding syntactic levels are involved in the non-adjacency. The model must relate nesting depth to where separators are allowed and ensure separators only occur between a closing and a following opening delimiter. Key correctness properties to guarantee: well-formedness (non-empty sequence, starts and ends with delimiters, no negative nesting depth, final depth zero), tokens strictly ordered by source location, correct matching of delimiters (each open matches a unique later close and enclosing locations are non-decreasing), and that separators only appear at syntactically valid positions with an appropriate depth annotation. Additionally, state invariants expressing that within any matched pair, all immediate top-level child pairs are ordered and, if not adjacent, separated by a separator whose depth reflects their enclosing context.
