Specify a formal model of a blockchain light client protocol that decides whether to accept and trust headers (light blocks) from a set of validators while some validators may be Byzantine. The model should capture multiple validators, a notion of validator fault tolerance (less than one-third faulty is assumed), and repeated attempts by the client to fetch and verify chain headers up to a target height starting from a trusted height. The client maintains a notion of a trusted header, may fetch headers at increasing heights, and may probe validators to detect equivocation or signing faults. The system must model clock drift and a trusting period after which previously trusted headers expire; the client must not accept headers whose trusting period has elapsed according to its local clock (which may drift relative to a reference clock). The model must allow for a primary validator that may be faulty and for other validators used to build quorum proofs. Correctness properties to guarantee: safety (the client never accepts a header that contradicts what a correct quorum of validators signed at a given height), liveness under the assumed fault ratio (if sufficiently many non-faulty validators and network responses, the client eventually verifies a header up to the target height before trust expiry), and that clock-drift assumptions prevent accepting expired headers or falsely rejecting valid ones. Also specify invariants that detect equivocation and ensure the client only advances its trusted header when valid trust proofs are present. Include the ability to model and check scenarios where the primary is faulty and where network/fetch attempts may fail.
