Specify a distributed termination-detection protocol for N sequentially numbered processes arranged in a logical ring. The modeled system must represent that each process can be either active or inactive and can be in one of two local colors (white or black). A single circulating token travels around the ring and carries a color (white or black). Processes may asynchronously send activation messages to other processes (possibly in either direction); sending a message in the direction opposite to the token’s travel must mark the sender black. Active processes may become inactive at any time. The token is passed only in one fixed direction around the ring; when a process holds the token it may forward it to its predecessor under conditions that reflect whether the process is active, black, or the token is black, and forwarding may change the token’s color and the process’s color to white.

The specification should require safety and liveness properties capturing correct termination detection:
- Safety: If the token returns to the designated initiator with color white and that initiator is passive and white, then all processes must be passive (i.e., detection implies global termination).
- Liveness: If all processes eventually become passive, then eventually the initiator will receive a white token and detect termination.

Also include reasonable typing invariants (process set, color domain, token position/color). Allow the environment to nondeterministically deliver activation messages and deactivations. Do not constrain implementation details such as specific state variables or procedural steps; describe only the system behavior and the correctness properties above.
