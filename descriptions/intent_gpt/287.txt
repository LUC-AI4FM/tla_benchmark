Model an integer-valued counter system that can be incremented, decremented, or left unchanged on each step. The model should start from a single initial state where the counter equals zero. At each transition, one of three atomic actions may occur: increase the counter by one, decrease the counter by one, or leave it unchanged. Specify the reachable-state space and allow arbitrary interleavings of these actions over infinite executions.

State the safety and liveness requirements to be verified. Safety: the system must maintain an invariant property expressing that a particular designated predicate about the counter (for example, equality to zero) either always holds or, alternatively, demonstrate the precise invariant that must never be violated. Liveness: from any reachable state, it must be possible to eventually reach states with positive and negative counter values (i.e., both signs are reachable infinitely often), unless forbidden by the chosen safety invariant; alternatively, require that some action type (increment or decrement) is always eventually enabled or occurs infinitely often. Also specify that stuttering steps (no change) are allowed and should not violate liveness checks.

Provide explicit properties to check: initial condition, transition relation correctness, the chosen invariant as a safety property, and one or more temporal liveness properties (e.g., eventual reachability of positive and negative values, or fairness constraints ensuring both increment and decrement occur infinitely often).
