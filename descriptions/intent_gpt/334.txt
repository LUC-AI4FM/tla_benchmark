Specify a system that validates whether a person meets a minimum age requirement based on their birth year, where the birth year is represented as an offset (two-digit or other compact form) and the current year is known. The model should accept configurable parameters: the numeric representation of a person’s birth (e.g., last two digits), the minimum age required for a license or privilege, and the current full year. The system must compute the person’s actual age unambiguously from the compact birth representation and the current year, taking into account century rollover (e.g., when the compact representation could refer to either the previous or current century). It must then determine whether the person is at least the required age.

Specify clear correctness properties: (1) Safety — the system never grants the license to someone whose computed actual age is less than the required minimum; (2) Liveness/Completeness — if a person’s actual age is at least the required minimum, the system eventually grants the license; (3) Determinism — for given inputs the decision is unique (no nondeterministic conflicting outcomes); and (4) Robustness against ambiguous birth encodings — the method for disambiguating century must be sound and documented, and the model should allow exploring alternative disambiguation policies. Include parameters to vary current year and requirement to check edge cases around birthday and century boundaries.
