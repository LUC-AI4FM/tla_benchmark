Model a concurrent mutual-exclusion protocol for N processes that coordinate access to a single critical section using a shared token and local queues. The specification should express process behaviors abstractly: processes alternate between noncritical and critical phases, request access, wait without busy-waiting unless necessary, and enter the critical section only when granted. The model must capture the handshake and transfer of a single permission token among processes so at most one process is in the critical section at a time.

Specify initialization, process steps for requesting, granting, passing, and releasing the token, and the asynchronous interleaving of steps. Include fairness assumptions so that ready requests are eventually served (no starvation): any process that repeatedly attempts to enter the critical section must eventually succeed. Require progress (liveness) properties such as eventual token transfer when appropriate and eventual entry for requesting processes under weak fairness of their request actions.

State safety properties: mutual exclusion (at most one in critical), absence of deadlock (if some process holds or requests the token then some step can progress), and preservation of the single-token invariant (exactly one token exists at all times). State liveness properties: eventual entry for requesting processes and eventual availability of the token to others when released. Avoid prescribing a particular data structure or algorithmic encoding; focus on the behavioral guarantees and invariants the model must enforce.
