Model the classic "prisoners and the light bulb" coordination problem: a finite set of indistinguishable prisoners are each taken, at arbitrary times and possibly repeatedly, into a common room containing a two-state indicator (a bulb or switch) that the prisoners can observe and optionally change. The schedule that chooses which prisoner visits the room is nondeterministic and may revisit prisoners many times; visits are sequential (one prisoner at a time) and eventually every prisoner visits infinitely often is not assumed unless required by the chosen liveness model. The system should allow prisoners to base actions only on their local memory and the single shared indicator; no other communication exists.

Specify a protocol choice parameterized by (a) which prisoner (if any) acts as the dedicated counter, and (b) the role rules for ordinary prisoners. The specification must allow arbitrary initial indicator state and arbitrary fair or unfair scheduling choices (document the fairness assumption used).

Formally state safety and progress properties:
- Safety: If any prisoner announces that all prisoners have visited the room, this announcement must be correct (every prisoner has visited at least once).
- Eventual success (liveness): Under a reasonable fairness assumption (e.g., every prisoner is scheduled infinitely often), the designated protocol must guarantee that some prisoner eventually makes the correct announcement.
- Noninterference: Ordinary prisoners must not use more information than the indicator plus their private memory; they must not rely on global clocks or visit-counts.

Also specify invariants to track counts of effective contributions to the counter, and allow proving termination/time-to-decision bounds as optional properties under stronger scheduling assumptions.
