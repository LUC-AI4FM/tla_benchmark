Specify a concurrent entry protocol for N asynchronous processes that provides mutual exclusion for a single critical section. The model should capture processes that repeatedly: perform noncritical work, announce intent to enter, obtain a globally comparable token value using only read and write of shared registers, and then wait until it is safe to enter the critical section. The token assignment may be non-atomic and may use temporary unreliable reads of other processes’ state; processes must be allowed to pick arbitrary natural numbers when attempting to set their token (to model non-deterministic writes). The system must tolerate arbitrary interleavings and allow processes to toggle an announcement flag used to communicate progress.

The specification must state safety and liveness requirements: safety—at most one process may be in the critical section at any time (mutual exclusion); liveness—any process that begins the entry protocol (i.e., indicates intent) will eventually enter the critical section (starvation-freedom), and the system admits progress from trying to in–critical (deadlock-freedom) under weak fairness for enabled process steps. The model should include the ordering relation used to compare tokens (lexicographic on pair (token, process id)) and express the intended ordering-based precedence conditions that guarantee safety. Do not prescribe a particular implementation; allow nondeterministic choices for reads, writes, and token picks so the proof obligations must be stated in terms of invariants and temporal properties only.
