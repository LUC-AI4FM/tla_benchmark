Model the classic "100 prisoners and a light bulb" coordination protocol and the property that all prisoners eventually deduce they have all visited the central room at least once. The system should consist of a fixed number of agents (prisoners) who repeatedly enter a shared room according to a nondeterministic scheduler. Each visit a prisoner may observe and (if allowed) modify a single shared binary token (a light bulb with on/off). Each prisoner has local state that may include memory of past observations and actions. Initially no prisoner has declared that all have visited, and the shared token starts in a defined initial state.

Specify the allowed behaviors of prisoners: when a prisoner visits, they may read the token and optionally change it according to some protocol-dependent strategy; they may also declare (announce) that every prisoner has visited the room, which ends the protocol. The scheduler may choose the visiting sequence arbitrarily, including repeatedly selecting the same prisoner, and visits continue until an announcement is made.

Require the following correctness properties:
- Safety: any announcement that "all prisoners have visited" must be true (i.e., every prisoner has indeed visited the room at least once).
- Liveness: the protocol must guarantee that eventually some prisoner will announce that all have visited (for all fair infinite schedules).
- Invariance: define and enforce a type invariant for all variables (domains and value ranges).
- Termination condition: once a correct announcement is made, the system reaches and remains in a quiescent accepting state.

Do not prescribe a particular protocol; allow modeling and verification of different strategies (e.g., a counter protocol or symmetric strategies) against the same environment and properties.
