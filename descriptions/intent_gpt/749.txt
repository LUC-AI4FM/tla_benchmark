Specify a formal model of the classic Cigarette Smokers problem. The system has one supplier (dealer) and three smoker processes; each smoker holds an infinite supply of exactly one distinct ingredient needed to make a cigarette (paper, tobacco, or matches). The dealer repeatedly places on the table a pair of distinct ingredients chosen from the three; the smoker who holds the complementary third ingredient should detect when the two other ingredients are available, take those two, make and smoke a cigarette, then signal completion and allow the next deal. The dealer must never place ingredients while another smoker is in the middle of taking ingredients or smoking; only one smoker may take the items from the table for each deal. The model should allow non-deterministic scheduling and arbitrary fair interleavings of dealer and smokers.

Specify the safety properties: at most one smoker may be smoking at any time, a smoker may only smoke after the dealer has placed the exact complementary pair on the table, and the dealer does not lose or duplicate ingredients (the table’s content reflects deals faithfully). Specify liveness: every deal by the dealer is eventually consumed by the correct smoker (no deadlock/starvation), and the dealer will eventually be able to place another pair (progress). Also state invariants about the table contents and the exclusivity of access to those contents.
