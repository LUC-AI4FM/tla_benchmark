Specify a TLA+ model of a round-based Byzantine fault-tolerant consensus protocol for a fixed set of processes that combines proposer-based proposals, local clocks, and bounded message delays. The model should describe how, in each round, a designated proposer may broadcast a timestamped proposal (value plus proposer's local clock reading), how processes receive proposals subject to clock precision, how they prevote and precommit on proposals (or on nil) based on quorum thresholds, and how decisions are formed when a process observes a sufficient number of matching precommits for a particular timestamped value. The system must tolerate up to T Byzantine processes (with N > 3T) and ensure that correct processes exchange messages asynchronously with bounded delay and that local clocks differ only within a given precision and accuracy margin relative to real time.

Require safety properties: (1) Agreement — no two correct processes decide different values; (2) Timestamp agreement — when two correct processes decide the same round, they must decide the same timestamp for that round; (3) Decisions’ timestamps must reflect the proposer's or reception time within specified accuracy and precision bounds; (4) If the initial proposer is correct, any decided timestamp must lie within the range of initial correct local clocks. Require liveness assumptions: timely delivery and progress when proposers are correct and sufficient real time has passed (bounded delay and a real-time proposer behavior assumption), ensuring eventual decision under those conditions. Do not prescribe internal variables or exact message formats—only behaviors, thresholds, time constraints, and the invariants above.
