Specify a system that models a collection of independently invoked operations and a second kind of operation that composes two of the first kind. The system must allow a finite or unbounded set of operation identifiers and support nondeterministic triggering of individual operations. Each operation invocation must be treated as an atomic logical operation that may succeed or fail, and the system must record the occurrence and outcome of each invocation.

The second kind of operation must accept two arguments that identify two previously invoked (or concurrently invoked) first-kind operations and produce a composed result. Composition must be defined so that it depends only on the arguments’ identities and their recorded outcomes at the time of composition; composition must be prevented or handled deterministically if either argument has not been invoked or its outcome is unknown.

Require safety properties: no composition can produce a result that contradicts the recorded outcomes of its component operations; no two distinct composed results may claim to be composed from the same pair of component outcomes but produce different observable effects. Require liveness properties: every invoked operation should eventually have its outcome recorded (unless the system is allowed to model permanent failures explicitly), and every valid composition request should eventually be either performed or rejected with a clear status.

Specify invariants expressing consistency of recorded outcomes, and specify temporal properties guaranteeing eventual recording and resolution of composition requests. Allow modeling of concurrent invocations and compositions, and require that the specification be usable to check for races, lost updates, and inconsistent compositions.
