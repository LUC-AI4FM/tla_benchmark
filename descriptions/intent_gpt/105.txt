Specify a concurrent system that launches and coordinates multiple worker tasks to process independent jobs from a finite pool and report completion to a single coordinator. The system must allow workers to be dynamically created up to a fixed maximum and to terminate when idle or when all jobs are finished. Each job should be processed exactly once by some worker. The coordinator must distribute jobs fairly (no job is indefinitely postponed) and must detect when all jobs are completed so it can shut down the system.

Model the job pool, worker lifecycle (idle, processing, terminated), assignment of jobs to workers, and the communication of completion acknowledgements. Allow nondeterministic scheduling and failures limited to a worker crashing while processing a job; if a crash occurs, the job must be returned to the pool for reprocessing. Ensure that no two workers can process the same job simultaneously. The system must guarantee eventual termination when all jobs are completed and no noncrashed worker is active, and must preserve a safety property that counts of completed jobs never exceed the initial number of jobs. Specify invariants expressing uniqueness of job processing, absence of lost completions, and eventual completion under fairness assumptions for scheduling and worker restarts.
