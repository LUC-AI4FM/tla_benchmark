Specify a formal model of a mutually defined parity predicate system that determines whether a natural number is even or odd. The model must treat "even" and "odd" as separate predicates that may be defined in terms of each other, possibly via external or parameterized definitions, and it must allow those definitions to be supplied or instantiated later. The system should accept any natural number input and produce a boolean classification into even or odd according to the standard parity semantics: 0 is even and not odd, and for any n>0, even(n) holds iff odd(n-1) holds, while odd(n) holds iff even(n-1) holds. The specification must ensure consistency and totality of the predicates over the naturals, rule out contradictory instantiations, and guarantee that for every n exactly one of even(n) or odd(n) holds. It should permit treating one predicate as a constant or externally provided function and verify that any supplied definition satisfies the mutual-recursive parity laws. Include safety properties (no number is both even and odd) and liveness/coverage properties (every number is classified by at least one predicate). Do not commit to a concrete implementation strategy for the mutual recursion.
