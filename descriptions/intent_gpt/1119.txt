Model the classic Tower of Hanoi puzzle as a state-machine over three pegs holding a finite set of distinct disks of different sizes. The system must start from an arbitrary initial distribution of the disks across the three pegs (with each peg’s contents given as an ordered stack, smallest represented by the smallest identifier), and allow only legally valid single-disk moves. A legal move picks the top disk from one peg and places it on the top of a different peg, with the constraint that a disk may be placed only on an empty peg or on a larger disk. Only one disk may move at a time.

Specify the state space, an initial state predicate capturing arbitrary initial stacks, and a next-state relation capturing all and only the above legal single-disk moves. Require invariants ensuring: the set of disks present on all pegs never changes (no new or lost disks), the total number of disks is constant, and the representation of each peg is always a well-formed ordered stack of disk sizes. Specify a liveness or reachability goal asserting that from the initial state there exists a sequence of legal moves that achieves a target configuration in which all disks reside on a designated peg in strictly increasing size order (smallest on top). Optionally allow specifying which peg is the destination and require termination when the goal is reached.
