Specify a formal model of finite sequences (ordered lists) over an arbitrary element type and the operations and properties needed to manipulate them safely and predictably.

Model what a sequence is (a finite ordered collection with a well-defined length and indexed positions starting at 1) and provide these abstract operations: constructing the empty sequence and singleton sequences; obtaining the length; accessing the first element and the rest; concatenating two sequences; extracting a contiguous subsequence by start and end positions; appending an element; and selecting a subsequence by a predicate (filtering while preserving order).

Require the model to enforce standard invariants: lengths compose correctly under concatenation and appending; indices are valid only in the appropriate domain; head and tail are undefined for the empty sequence; subsequence extraction preserves element order and has length equal to n−m+1 for valid m≤n; concatenation and subsequence operations are associative and interact predictably (for example, extracting a contiguous range from a concatenation yields the appropriate pieces from each operand). For the filtering operation, require it to produce exactly those elements that satisfy the predicate in original order, and to be compositional (filtering with a conjunction equals successive filters). Specify boundary conditions and error conditions (e.g., invalid indices) as explicit side conditions. Include correctness properties that can be model-checked: preservation of element multiset under concatenation/append, idempotence where applicable, and invariants about domains and lengths.
