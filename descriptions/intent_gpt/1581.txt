Specify a model of the classic Dining Philosophers concurrency problem with N philosophers seated around a circular table and one fork between each pair. Each philosopher alternates between thinking and attempting to eat; to eat a philosopher must hold both adjacent forks. The model should capture asynchronous nondeterministic interleaving of philosophers' actions and the mutual exclusion on forks (a fork can be held by at most one philosopher at a time).

Require the following correctness properties:
- Safety (no two adjacent philosophers may be eating simultaneously).
- Mutual exclusion on forks (a given fork cannot be held by more than one philosopher at once).
- Liveness: every philosopher who attempts to eat must eventually be able to eat (no philosopher is permanently blocked while others repeatedly eat). Specify a strong starvation-freedom requirement: for each philosopher, it is always the case that they will infinitely often enter the eating state (or, at minimum, that any attempt to acquire forks eventually succeeds).
- Deadlock freedom: the system must guarantee there is no reachable global state in which every philosopher is stuck waiting for forks such that no progress is possible.

Parameterize the model by N (a positive integer ≥ 2). Do not presuppose a particular fork-acquisition ordering; allow the specification to permit different protocols but state that the implementation under consideration should be proved to satisfy the above safety and liveness properties for all valid N. Include fairness constraints as needed to express progress assumptions about scheduling or about enabling conditions for fork acquisition.
