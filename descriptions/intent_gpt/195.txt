Specify a formal model of the "prisoners and two switches" coordination puzzle solved by a designated counter and cooperating prisoners. The model should represent a shared room containing two binary switches and a population of N > 1 prisoners. The environment nondeterministically (but fairly) chooses a prisoner to enter the room repeatedly, with the guarantee that each prisoner is selected infinitely often. Each visit the chosen prisoner must change exactly one switch (flip or set to a particular position) according to a pre-agreed protocol.

The model must capture the intended strategy in abstract terms: one prisoner is designated the counter whose role is to detect, by observing and manipulating switch states over many visits, whether every other prisoner has visited the room at least once. The other prisoners may perform limited signalling actions (at most a bounded number of times per prisoner) to communicate their first visitation to the counter by manipulating one of the switches, otherwise they must perform a default action. All actions must obey the rule of exactly one switch change per visit.

Specify the safety requirement that whenever the counter concludes (i.e., reaches the decision condition) and declares that all prisoners have visited, then indeed every other prisoner has visited at least once. Specify the liveness requirement that, under the fairness assumption (each prisoner visited infinitely often), the counter eventually reaches the declaration condition and thus the protocol terminates with success. Also specify an invariant relating the counter's internal count to the cumulative signalling performed by the non-counter prisoners and the current observable switch state, which supports proving the safety and liveness properties.
