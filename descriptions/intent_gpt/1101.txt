Specify a formal TLA+ model of Peterson’s two-process mutual exclusion algorithm and verify its key correctness properties. The model should represent two concurrent processes that repeatedly try to enter a critical section by announcing intention to enter and by setting a shared turn variable to give the other process priority. The system must allow each process to loop forever: indicate noncritical work, perform the entry protocol (announce intent, set the turn, wait for the guard), execute the critical section, and perform the exit protocol (revoke intent).

Requirements:
- Capture the shared state (each process’s intent flag and the turn indicator) and each process’s control flow through the entry, wait, critical, and exit stages.
- Model nondeterministic interleaving of the two processes and stuttering steps.
- Ensure safety: mutual exclusion must hold at all times (no two processes may be in their critical sections simultaneously).
- Ensure consistency between intent flags and control locations: a process’s flag should reflect when it has progressed past the relevant point in the entry protocol.
- Prove that a suitable inductive invariant holds that implies mutual exclusion and is preserved by all steps.
- Show a refinement mapping from this concrete protocol to an abstract two-process lock specification (where a single lock variable indicates which process, if any, holds the lock), and prove the concrete specification implements the abstract one.

Do not model implementation-level scheduling primitives beyond nondeterministic process selection; focus on the algorithm, its state, invariants, safety, and refinement to an abstract lock.
