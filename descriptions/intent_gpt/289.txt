Specify a model of a system of distributed processes that each maintain a local integer clock. The model should represent a fixed set of processes and allow time to advance in discrete positive steps. When time advances, every process’s clock is increased by the same positive integer delta. After such an advance, the system must update a derived relation that records the pairwise differences (drifts) between clocks.

The specification must allow two styles of time advancement (for comparison): (1) a single, atomic update that increases all clocks simultaneously and recomputes all pairwise drifts; and (2) a more explicit stepwise update that conceptually visits each process and updates its clock, then visits each ordered pair of processes and updates their drift entry. The behavior of the two styles should be observationally comparable.

Require the following correctness properties:
- Invariants: clocks are always nonnegative integers; the drift relation always equals the difference of the current clocks for every ordered pair of processes.
- Safety: after any time-advance step (either style), the recomputed drifts must match the actual differences between the updated clocks.
- Progress: the system must permit arbitrarily large cumulative advancement of time (no deadlock preventing further positive deltas).

Do not commit to a particular implementation technique for the stepwise update; instead specify the abstract effect and ensure both update styles preserve the invariants and agree on drift values.
