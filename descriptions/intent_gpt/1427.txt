Model the classic two-jug water-pouring puzzle as a state-transition system: two containers with fixed integer capacities (a smaller jug of capacity 3 and a larger jug of capacity 5) hold nonnegative integer amounts of water. The system should allow the following kinds of atomic operations at any state: fill either jug to its capacity, empty either jug to zero, and pour water from one jug to the other until the source is empty or the destination is full. The model must enforce that jug quantities are always integers within their capacity bounds and that transitions preserve total water except when a fill or empty occurs.

Specify an initial state with both jugs empty. Specify the reachable-state relation that permits any of the allowed operations nondeterministically at each step. Formulate correctness properties to check: (1) safety — both jug amounts always remain within their allowed ranges; (2) reachability — whether a state exists in which one of the jugs contains exactly a target volume (for example, 4 units) and, if requested, whether such a state is reachable from the initial state; and (3) liveness/termination properties — if desired, characterize whether repeated nondeterministic operations can indefinitely avoid reaching the target (fairness or eventuality requirements). Also provide the ability to check invariants and to search for shortest sequences of operations achieving the target.
