Model a simple biochemical protocol that manipulates four nonnegative integer resources representing counts of primer, template, hybrid (paired primer-template), and DNA product. The system repeatedly performs one of a small set of atomic operations that conserve or reassign these counts under natural-number semantics. Specify the initial state allowing arbitrary natural-number values for the four resources. The allowed atomic operations are: (a) a heating step that transfers all hybrid into separate primer and template counts and clears hybrid, possibly producing DNA; (b) an annealing step that pairs up to the minimum available of primer and template, moving that many units into hybrid while decreasing primer and template; (c) an extension step that converts all current hybrid into DNA product and clears hybrid; and (d) a no-op that leaves state unchanged. The model must ensure all values remain natural numbers throughout execution and that annealing only pairs a positive number up to the available minimum. Express the main correctness properties to prove: (1) a type-safety invariant that primer, template, hybrid, and DNA stay in Nat; (2) a positivity invariant ensuring primer count never becomes negative; and (3) a conservation invariant that the quantity template + primer + 2*(DNA + hybrid) is preserved by every step. Do not commit to a particular implementation; allow nondeterministic choice among the operations.
