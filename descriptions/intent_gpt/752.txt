Specify a formal model of the classic “coffee can” bean-removal process. The system starts with a multiset of beans of two colors (black and white) in a container. Repeatedly, two beans are removed uniformly at random from the container; depending on their colors, zero, one, or two beans may be returned to the container according to the following rule: if the two beans are the same color, remove them and put back a black bean; if they are different colors, remove them and put back a white bean. Continue until at most one bean remains.

Model the non-deterministic evolution of the multiset of beans under these rules and allow any valid initial counts of black and white beans (including zero) up to a fixed maximum. The model should capture termination when the container has 0 or 1 beans left, and should allow reasoning about all possible removal orders.

Specify and verify these key correctness properties:
- Termination: from any allowed initial state, the process reaches a state with ≤1 beans in a finite number of steps.
- Invariant parity: the parity (odd/even) of the count of white beans is preserved across every step (equivalently, the parity of the number of white beans in the initial state determines whether the final remaining bean, if any, is white).
- Final-state characterization: when the process terminates with exactly one bean, that bean’s color is white iff the initial white-bean count was odd; otherwise, the final bean is black (or no bean remains if counts allow).
Include assertions expressing these invariants and the termination property for model checking.
