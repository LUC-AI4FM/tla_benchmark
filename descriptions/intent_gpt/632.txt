Specify a TLA+ model of a light-client attack-detection subsystem that monitors a blockchain tip and produces evidence when peers provide conflicting or otherwise invalid block proofs. The model should represent a set of validators with a bounded fraction that may be faulty, an external reference chain that advances over time, and one or more remote peers that supply light-block headers, commits, and validator-set information. The detector must periodically fetch and verify light-blocks at various heights, compare sets of signed commits from different peers, and detect equivocations or invalid commits according to the protocol’s voting-power rules. It must also consider clock drift, trusting periods, and thresholds for trusting an initial trusted height; detection decisions may depend on local and reference clocks and on whether peers behave as primary or secondary evidence sources.

The specification should capture allowed faulty behaviors (including equivocation and omission), model network asynchrony for fetches, and allow the reference chain to advance and rotate validator sets. Key correctness properties to assert: (1) Safety — the detector never accepts conflicting blocks as both valid without producing evidence; (2) Liveness of detection — if a quorum of validators equivocate on a block, the detector eventually produces evidence for that equivocation provided the evidence is within trusting periods and reachable via fetches; (3) Evidence validity — any produced evidence contains sufficient signed votes to prove a fault under the protocol’s faulty-ratio threshold; and (4) Clock-consistency constraints — detection does not rely on unrealistic clock assumptions and respects allowed clock drift and trusting-period bounds.
