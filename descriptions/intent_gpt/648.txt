Specify a TLA+ model of a blockchain light client that verifies headers (light blocks) from a set of validators and advances its trusted chain tip over time. The model should represent a client that periodically fetches candidate headers at increasing heights and decides whether to accept and adopt them as the new trusted tip based on validator voting predicates, time bounds, and clock drift. The system must allow a subset of validators to be Byzantine up to a configurable fault ratio, and it must allow arbitrary network reordering and omission of messages between client and validators.

The specification must capture the client’s trust and verification rules: what constitutes sufficient validator agreement to trust a header, how conflicting headers are handled, when the client considers a header expired or outside a trusting period, and how local clock inaccuracies affect verification deadlines. The model must also represent a primary validator that may behave correctly or maliciously, and the client’s probing/retry behavior when encountering unverifiable or missing data.

Key correctness properties to guarantee:
- Safety: the client must never adopt a header that is not supported by a supermajority of non-faulty validators or that violates the trusting/time constraints.
- Liveness (under assumed bounds): if a sufficient number of honest validators continually produce headers and the client’s clock drift is within modeled bounds, the client eventually makes progress to reach a specified target height.
- Fault tolerance: the client tolerates up to the configured fraction of faulty validators without violating safety.
- Monotonicity: the sequence of adopted trusted heights is strictly increasing and only moves to headers that are properly verified.

Include parameters for validator set, trusting period, target height, clock drift assumptions, and the fraction of faulty validators so the model can explore both correct and adversarial scenarios.
