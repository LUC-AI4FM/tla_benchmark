Specify a timed distributed protocol executed by seven processes that maintain local integer clocks and measure pairwise drift. Model a system in which each process repeatedly updates its own clock and communicates with others to bound clock differences, while occasional protocol steps may be skipped by a designated exception process. The model must capture independent integer-valued clock variables per process and a function capturing measured drift between any ordered pair of processes. The protocol should ensure that when processes perform updates and measurements under normal operation, the absolute difference between any two correct processes' clocks remains within a fixed bounded range. It must also tolerate the effect of one process being excluded from certain synchronization steps: the exclusion may temporarily increase drift measurements involving that process, but must not allow the drift between any two non-excluded processes to exceed the bound. Specify fairness conditions ensuring that no correct process is permanently prevented from taking synchronization steps, and allow arbitrary finite non-deterministic scheduling of actions subject to those fairness constraints. Require invariants expressing (1) clocks advance by non‑negative integer amounts, (2) measured drift values correctly reflect differences between the latest known clock values up to the allowed measurement error, and (3) the maximum absolute drift between any two non-excluded processes is always ≤ B for some constant B. Include liveness goals that every non-excluded process eventually performs synchronization infinitely often.
