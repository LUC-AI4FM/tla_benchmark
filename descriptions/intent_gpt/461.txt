Specify a formal model of a small static-logic checker that evaluates and reports on predicate-logic expressions over finite sets and Cartesian products. The model should describe a system that takes as given a finite universe of integers and a collection of finite sets, and then evaluates a suite of Boolean queries that use universal and existential quantification, emptiness, membership, and choice over those finite sets. The system must compute truth values of expressions such as: "there exists an element of a given set satisfying a predicate", "all elements of a multiset or repeated-element collection satisfy a predicate", the behaviour of quantification over the empty set, and simple arithmetic or equality comparisons inside quantified expressions. It must also model deterministic selection when a unique element satisfies a predicate and identify when a selection is undefined or ambiguous. The specification must state the expected outcome (true/false) for each query and require the checker to flag mismatches as violations. Key correctness properties: (1) Quantifiers over nonempty finite sets obey standard logic: existential is true iff at least one element satisfies the predicate; universal is true iff every element satisfies it. (2) Quantifiers over empty sets follow vacuous truth rules (universal true, existential false). (3) Repetition of elements in a collection does not change quantifier outcomes. (4) Cartesian-product quantification ranges over ordered tuples and composes predicates correctly. (5) A deterministic-choice operator yields the unique satisfying element when one exists, and the system detects and reports nonexistence or non-uniqueness. The model should provide explicit assertions for each query and a traceable report of passed/failed checks.
