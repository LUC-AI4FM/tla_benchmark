Specify a formal model of a synchronous atomic commit protocol for a fixed set of distributed participants (cohorts) that must reach a consistent global decision: either all participants that decide commit must stay committed forever, and all participants that decide abort must stay aborted forever. The system should model asynchronous steps of participants communicating with a coordinator (or running a coordinatorless consensus) and permit retries, message reordering, and failures of participants and the coordinator, subject to eventual reliable recovery or timeouts. The model should allow nondeterministic choices for initial participant states and for message delivery ordering, but must capture safety constraints that no participant can switch from commit to abort or from abort to commit once a final decision has been reached for that participant. It should express liveness expectations under reasonable fairness: if a sufficient set of non-faulty participants and the coordinator remain responsive, then a unanimous decision (commit or abort) is eventually reached by all non-faulty participants. Include invariants capturing validity (no participant can commit unless a prepare/accept condition was satisfied), agreement (no two non-faulty participants decide differently), and decision permanence (decisions are stable thereafter). Do not prescribe a particular algorithmic structure; focus on the protocol goals, fault model, permitted actions, and the safety and liveness properties to be verified.
