Specify a formal model of the classic Die Hard water‑jug puzzle: two jugs with fixed integer capacities (a larger jug of capacity 5 units and a smaller jug of capacity 3 units) and an unbounded water source and sink. The model should represent states by the current volume of water in each jug (nonnegative integers bounded by their capacities) and should allow the following kinds of state transitions: fill either jug to capacity from the source, empty either jug completely to the sink, pour from one jug to the other until the source jug is empty or the destination jug is full (whichever occurs first), and a stuttering (no‑op) step. Do not prescribe an implementation strategy or encoding; describe only the allowed state space and transition relation.

Require that the model ensure type and bound safety invariants: jug volumes always remain integers within their respective capacity ranges and the total water amount is a nonnegative integer. Specify the initial condition (both jugs empty) and allow arbitrary sequences of enabled transitions. State the key correctness properties to verify: (1) safety — volumes always stay within bounds; (2) reachability — there exists a sequence of transitions that reaches a state where one jug contains exactly 4 units; and (3) liveness/termination variants — optionally assert that from the initial state a sequence of transitions reaches the 4‑unit goal within a finite number of non‑stuttering steps.
