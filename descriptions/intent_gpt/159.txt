Specify a formal model of a two-phase commit protocol between a single coordinator (transaction manager) and a fixed set of resource managers. The model should describe asynchronous message-passing between the coordinator and the resource managers, where messages may be delayed, duplicated, or reordered but not corrupted or lost. The system must capture the following behaviors and goals:

- Resource managers decide to either prepare or abort locally; preparing means they are willing to commit but still await the coordinator’s decision. Aborting is final for that resource manager.
- The coordinator collects prepare votes and, once it has prepared votes from all resource managers, issues a global commit; if any resource manager aborts, the coordinator issues a global abort.
- Resource managers follow the coordinator’s final decision (commit or abort) when they receive it, and they must not commit unless they have previously prepared.
- The model must allow concurrent and independent decisions by resource managers, and it must allow the coordinator to make decisions based only on messages received so far.

State the safety properties to be guaranteed:
- Agreement: No two resource managers may end up in conflicting final states (one committed and another aborted).
- Validity: If every resource manager prepared, then the final decision must be commit; if any resource manager aborted, the final decision must be abort.
- Consistency of local constraints: No resource manager may commit without having prepared first.

Also state liveness requirements to be checked (under fair execution assumptions):
- Eventual decision: If messages are eventually delivered and processes are fair, the coordinator will eventually reach and broadcast a final decision, and every non-faulty resource manager will eventually learn and adopt that decision.

Do not prescribe a specific implementation; leave freedom to model processes, messages, and faults so proofs can verify the stated safety and liveness properties.
