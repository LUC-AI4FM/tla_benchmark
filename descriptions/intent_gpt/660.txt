Specify a concurrent system that manages a fixed multiset of identical resources and a set of clients that repeatedly request and release batches of those resources. The system should allow each client to atomically acquire a specified number of resource units (possibly zero) and later release exactly the units it previously held. Resources are fungible and the global pool has a finite capacity; no allocation may cause the total allocated units to exceed capacity. Clients may contend for resources, their requests may be satisfied immediately or be delayed until sufficient units are available, and the system must permit progress when possible.

Model the clients as independent processes that repeatedly: decide a desired batch size, request that many units, hold them for some time, then return them. The environment may nondeterministically choose the batch sizes and the scheduling of clients. Specify the safety properties: allocated units never exceed capacity, no client holds a negative amount or more than it requested, and the total held equals the sum of per-client holdings. Specify liveness/weak-fairness properties: if a client repeatedly requests a batch that is currently satisfiable by the total free units, then eventually the request is granted; and if a client holds units, it will eventually release them. Also require that requests are granted atomically (no partial allocations) and that the system cannot deadlock when outstanding requests could collectively be satisfied by some ordering.
