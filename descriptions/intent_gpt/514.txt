Specify a formal model of the Alternating Bit protocol for reliable, ordered delivery of items drawn from an abstract data set across an unreliable channel that may reorder, duplicate, or drop transmissions but does not corrupt their payloads. The model should treat the sender and receiver as concurrent components communicating via message values that include a single data payload and a one‑bit sequence value. The sender repeatedly transmits data items, attaching the alternating bit; the receiver accepts and delivers each new data item only when its bit differs from the last delivered bit, and otherwise discards duplicates. The sender must not advance to the next data item until it is certain the receiver has accepted the current item (modeled by observing acknowledgement via the alternating bit). Model the channel as possibly lossy and duplicating: messages in transit can be duplicated, lost, or delayed arbitrarily, but delivered messages preserve payload and bit.

Specify the initial conditions, allowed atomic transitions, and fairness assumptions needed so progress is possible. State and prove these correctness properties:
- Safety (no spurious deliveries): every item delivered was actually sent and is delivered exactly once in the intended order.
- Receiver delivery ordering: the sequence of delivered data items equals the sequence of items produced by the sender, without reordering.
- Liveness (eventual delivery): assuming the sender repeatedly retransmits the current message and the channel delivers some retransmission, every sent message is eventually delivered.
- Bit alternation invariant: successive accepted messages at the receiver have alternating bit values.

Do not constrain implementation details such as internal variables or specific message encodings beyond the one‑bit sequence and data payload.
