Specify a concurrent two-process mutual-exclusion lock and its intended refinement relation to Peterson’s algorithm. The model should describe two processes that repeatedly execute a three-step protocol to enter a critical section: an entry protocol composed of two ordered precritical steps plus an assignment that sets which process has the “turn”, followed by the critical section and an exit step. The system must allow stuttering steps that represent internal micro-steps of the entry protocol so that each high-level transition corresponds to a sequence of lower-level steps. Include an auxiliary history component that records the value of the turn variable at the moment it is assigned during the entry protocol, and include a stuttering control component that encodes the extra internal steps.

Require safety properties:
- Mutual exclusion: the two processes can never be in their critical sections simultaneously.
- Invariant relating history and program counters: when a process is in the critical section and an internal stutter is active, the stuttering context matches that process and the recorded history turn value implies the proper ownership condition (the recorded turn equals the other process whenever the invariant demands it).
- Type and domain invariants for process states, the turn history, and the stuttering controller.

Require liveness/behavioral refinement goals:
- Show that every behavior of this lock with history and stuttering refines to a behavior of the standard Peterson algorithm by mapping stuttering sequences and the recorded history to Peterson’s single-step transitions.
- Preserve enabledness of external actions: the observable sequence of entry, critical, and exit actions corresponds exactly to Peterson’s observable actions once stuttering is removed and the history is used to recover the turn value.

Do not prescribe implementation details; specify only the processes’ abstract steps, the auxiliary history and stuttering artifacts, the required invariants, and the refinement relation to Peterson.
