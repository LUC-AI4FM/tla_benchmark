Specify a finite-state supervisor that coordinates two phases of a mission: initialization, repeated verification of a primary target, and secondary evidence detection. The system should begin in an initialization phase that either proceeds to the monitoring loop or fails to initialize. While active, it repeatedly attempts to verify a primary target; each verification attempt may either continue the loop, transition into a detection phase for possible attacks/evidence, or give up because peers are exhausted. In the detection phase the supervisor either finds evidence of an attack, reports no evidence and returns to the verification loop (producing an observable output when returning), or gives up because secondary peers are exhausted. Certain terminal states stop all progress (failed initialization, exhausted primary peers, exhausted secondary peers, or evidence found). Model the externally visible outputs produced when the supervisor concludes a detection round with no evidence. Specify safety properties that forbid reaching exhausted-peers states unless peers are legitimately consumed, and that terminal states do not change further. Specify liveness properties requiring that the system makes progress from initialization toward the loop or to failure, and that a detection round that reports no evidence eventually produces the observable output and returns to the verification loop. Also specify invariants asserting that while the system remains active it is never in an exhausted-peers terminal state, and that outputs are only produced at the designated transition.
