Model a distributed replicated state machine using the Raft consensus algorithm. The model should describe a fixed set of servers that communicate via unreliable asynchronous RPCs (vote requests/responses and append-entries requests/responses) and may experience message delay, duplication, loss, and server restarts. The system must support leader election, log replication of client-submitted commands, and commit semantics that ensure a single sequence of commands is eventually chosen and applied consistently.

Specify the system’s allowed behaviors (timeouts that trigger elections, sending and handling of RequestVote and AppendEntries RPCs, leader transition on winning a majority, appending entries on leaders, followers accepting or rejecting based on log matching, retry/backoff for failed replication) without prescribing how internal mechanisms are implemented. Capture persistent state that survives restarts (term numbers, voted-for, and local log) and volatile state that may be lost. Model quorums as any set with majority overlap.

State the key safety properties to prove:
- At most one leader per term (no two different leaders elected in the same term).
- Log safety: once a log entry is committed (decided by a quorum), no other leader can commit a different value at that log index; committed entries are never overwritten.
- Leader append-only invariant: a leader only extends logs with entries from its term in a way that preserves previously committed prefixes.
- Election-commit monotonicity: committed indices do not decrease.

Also state liveness desiderata informally: under eventual message delivery and a stable leader, client commands are eventually replicated and committed.
