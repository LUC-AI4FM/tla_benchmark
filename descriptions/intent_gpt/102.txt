Specify a formal model of an ERC‑20-like token contract and its interaction with external transaction submitters. The model should represent accounts with integer balances and per-pair allowances, and it should model three kinds of externally submitted operations: direct token transfers, approvals that set allowances for a spender, and delegated transfers that move tokens from an approved owner by a spender. The system must process a possibly concurrent stream of transactions that may fail (i.e., be rejected by the contract) or succeed, and it must update balances and allowances accordingly.

Require the model to capture the following correctness properties:
- Total supply invariance: the sum of all account balances remains constant except for explicitly modeled mint/burn operations (if included).
- Safety of balances: no account ever has a negative balance; transfers must only succeed when the sender has sufficient balance.
- Allowance discipline: delegated transfers cannot exceed the allowance granted to the spender; allowances decrease appropriately when used.
- Atomicity and consistency: each accepted transaction must be applied atomically producing a predictable state transition; failed transactions must leave state unchanged.
- Liveness/processing: every submitted transaction is eventually either accepted or rejected (no transaction is permanently ignored), and transaction identifiers progress monotonically.
- No unauthorized transfers: only the owner of an address may initiate direct transfers or approvals, and only the approved spender may initiate a delegated transfer on behalf of an owner.

Also specify reasonable assumptions about the environment: a finite set of addresses, integer ranges for balances and allowances, and that transaction submitters provide a unique identifier for each transaction. Include invariants or temporal properties to assert the absence of double-spending, preservation of approvals semantics, and eventual handling of pending transactions.
