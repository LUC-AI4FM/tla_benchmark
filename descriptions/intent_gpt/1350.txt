Specify a simple state machine that models a single integer-valued counter that starts at 1 and repeatedly increases until it reaches 5, after which it remains fixed. The model should capture initialization, allowed state transitions, and liveness assumptions without prescribing any particular coding style.

Requirements:
- The system must initialize the counter to 1.
- From any state less than 5, the system must be able to take a step that increments the counter by exactly 1.
- When the counter equals 5, it must remain at 5 forever (no further changes).
- The model should allow fairness assumptions ensuring that enabled increments are not permanently ignored: if the increment action remains continuously enabled, it must eventually occur.
- Specify both safety and liveness properties:
  - Safety: Every step either increments the counter by one (when below 5) or leaves it unchanged (when at 5); the counter never decreases and never jumps by more than one.
  - Liveness: If the system starts at 1, it must eventually reach 5 and stay there forever (eventual stabilization at 5).
- State any auxiliary invariants needed to prove these properties, such as bounds on the counter (it always stays between 1 and 5 inclusive) and monotonicity (nondecreasing).

Do not commit to a concrete implementation style; express the behavior and properties declaratively so they can be encoded in TLA+.
