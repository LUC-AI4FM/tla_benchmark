Specify a small module system and name-resolution mechanism for a language that supports module definitions, module importing, and module instantiation. Model modules as independently defined collections of named definitions (functions, values, type annotations) that can be referenced by other modules either by direct extension (static import), by explicit instantiation (creating a local bound instance of another module), or by referencing an instantiated module's exported names. The model should allow re-exporting: a module that imports or extends another may make the other's definitions available under its own namespace. It must also support multiple instantiations of the same module and nested instantiations (modules that instantiate modules that themselves import or instantiate others).

Require that references to a definition resolve deterministically to a single definition according to the import/instantiate relationships and the scoping rules, and that name collisions are detected and handled (either forbidden or deterministically disambiguated). Ensure that type- or signature-like annotations attached to definitions are preserved and correctly associated with the resolved definition across imports and instantiations. Specify correctness properties: absence of ambiguous references, preservation of annotations through import/instantiation chains, and stability under repeated instantiation (i.e., instantiating a module multiple times does not change semantics of previously resolved references). Also require that cyclic import/instantiation graphs are either rejected or given a well-defined semantics.
