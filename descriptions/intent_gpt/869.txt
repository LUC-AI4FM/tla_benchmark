Model a single-lane bridge traffic-control system where vehicles approach the bridge from two directions (left and right) and must cross without collisions or deadlock. The system should represent multiple distinct vehicles on each side, a finite sequence of positions representing the road and the bridge, and a subset of those positions designated as the bridge segment. Vehicles may move one position at a time forward toward and across the bridge; they cannot occupy the same position simultaneously. The protocol must allow vehicles from both sides to alternate access but must enforce mutual exclusion on the bridge so that no two vehicles moving in opposite directions are ever simultaneously on the bridge segment.

Specify fairness assumptions so that vehicles that continuously attempt to cross eventually make progress (no starvation). The model must prevent livelock and deadlock: if there are vehicles waiting on either side and the bridge becomes available, some waiting vehicle eventually enters and progresses across. Provide invariants ensuring (1) safety: no position is occupied by more than one vehicle and no opposing-direction vehicles coexist on the bridge; (2) boundedness: positions remain within the defined road domain; and (3) progress: any vehicle that is not permanently blocked will eventually reach the bridge and then leave it. The specification should allow reasoning about different numbers of vehicles on each side and different bridge lengths.
