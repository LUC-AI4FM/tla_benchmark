Specify a formal model of the Alternating Bit Protocol (ABP) for reliable message delivery over an unreliable channel. The model should represent a sender and a receiver that communicate by sending data messages and acknowledgments; messages may be lost but not corrupted or reordered. The sender transmits a stream of data values tagged with a one-bit sequence number; the receiver accepts each data item exactly once, delivers it in order, and returns an acknowledgment containing the corresponding bit. The sender retransmits the current message until it receives a matching acknowledgment, then advances to the next data item and toggles the bit. The receiver ignores duplicate data (same bit) but acknowledges it so the sender can stop retransmitting.

Require the specification to express:

- The state of sender and receiver (including current sequence bit and pending data) and the channel contents as sequences of messages and acknowledgments.
- A non-deterministic unreliable channel where send actions append messages and loss actions may remove an arbitrary message or acknowledgment.
- Safety properties: the channel only carries well-formed messages (bit paired with data), sent and received bits are within {0,1}, and type invariants for sequences are preserved by every transition.
- Liveness and correctness properties: every delivered data item is one of the sent data items in order, each data item is eventually delivered provided the sender stops sending new items and losses are fair, and no data item is delivered more than once.

Do not constrain timing or fairness beyond allowing nondeterministic loss; expose fairness assumptions needed to prove eventual delivery.
