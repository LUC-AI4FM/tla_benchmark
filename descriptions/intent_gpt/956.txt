Specify a formal model of arithmetic on natural numbers and integer division suitable for proving basic algebraic properties and reasoning about ranges. The model should represent a discrete numeric domain (natural numbers and integers) with the usual operations: addition, subtraction, multiplication, exponentiation, ordering (≤, <, ≥, >), integer ranges, integer division (quotient) and remainder. It must ensure that addition, multiplication and exponentiation are closed and associative/commutative where appropriate, and that ordering is a total order compatible with arithmetic. Define integer ranges as finite sets of consecutive integers given endpoints.

Crucially, specify integer division and remainder so that for any nonnegative divisor d > 0 and any dividend a, there exists a unique quotient q and remainder r such that a = d*q + r and 0 ≤ r < d. The quotient must be an integer and the remainder nonnegative and strictly smaller than the divisor. Provide properties linking division and modulo to ordering and arithmetic (e.g., a = d*(a div d) + (a % d), and relationships when a < d or when a is a multiple of d).

State invariants and theorems to verify uniqueness of quotient, correctness of remainder bounds, closure properties, and that ranges defined by endpoints contain exactly the intended integers. Also require proofs (or the ability to check proofs) of these properties.
