Specify a formal model of a two-phase commit (2PC) protocol with a single coordinator and a fixed set of participants. The model should capture asynchronous message delivery (messages may be delayed or lost), participant failures and recovery semantics, and non-Byzantine faults (processes may crash and later recover, but do not act maliciously). The system must support a prepare phase where the coordinator requests each participant’s vote (yes/no/undecided) and a commit/abort phase where the coordinator broadcasts the global decision (commit/abort) to all participants.

Require that the protocol guarantees: (1) Agreement — no two non-faulty participants decide differently (no split brain); (2) Atomicity — if any participant decides commit, then every participant that eventually decides must decide commit; (3) Validity — if all non-faulty participants vote yes and the coordinator does not crash permanently, the final decision is commit; conversely, if any non-faulty participant votes no, the decision is abort; (4) Termination under weak liveness assumptions — if messages are eventually delivered and crashed processes eventually recover, then all non-faulty participants eventually reach a decision; and (5) Coordinator consistency — the coordinator’s decision is consistent with the votes it received.

Model message channels abstractly (per-recipient queues or unreliable broadcast), allow explicit modeling of “vote sent/received” and “decision received/applied,” and expose participant and coordinator crash/recovery events. Specify invariants capturing safety properties and liveness properties expressed under fairness assumptions.
