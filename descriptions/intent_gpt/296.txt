Model a distributed mutual-exclusion algorithm for N identical processes that uses only shared readable/writable registers and numerical "ticket" values to decide access to a single critical section. The model should capture each process repeatedly executing a protocol to (1) indicate intent to enter, (2) pick a ticket number strictly greater than the current maximum seen, (3) wait until it is the process with the smallest (ticket, id) pair among contenders, enter the critical section, and then relinquish its ticket. The environment is asynchronous: processes interleave arbitrarily, and reads/writes of shared registers are atomic.

Specify the state space (process-local program counters and local temporaries plus the shared arrays/registers), initial conditions, and the allowed atomic transitions for each process (intention, number selection, waits, entry, exit). The specification must state and verify these correctness properties:

- Safety (Mutual Exclusion): no two distinct processes may be in the critical section at the same time.
- Type and domain invariants: all registers, tickets, and bookkeeping sets remain within their declared types/ranges.
- Liveness: if a process begins trying to enter its critical section, it eventually succeeds (starvation freedom). Also specify weak liveness: if some process is trying then some process eventually enters (deadlock freedom).
- Progress fairness: assume weak fairness of each process so that enabled steps are not permanently ignored.

Do not prescribe implementation-level optimizations; focus on the algorithmic requirements, the intended ordering rule using lexicographic comparison of (ticket, process id), and the invariants needed to prove safety and liveness.
