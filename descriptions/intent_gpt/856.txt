Specify a formal model of the classic "prisoners and a light" coordination problem. Model N prisoners who are in isolated cells and occasionally, one at a time, are brought into a shared interrogation room that contains a single light switch (or bulb) whose state may be initially unknown. The system runs indefinitely until some prisoner correctly announces that every prisoner has visited the room at least once. The model must allow arbitrary nondeterministic scheduling of which prisoner visits next, subject only to eventual fairness (each prisoner may visit infinitely often). Each visit gives the visiting prisoner the ability to observe and modify only the shared light state and to update their private memory; no other communication channel exists. The goal is to capture a protocol that, when followed by the prisoners, guarantees that eventually some prisoner will make a correct global announcement (i.e., the announcement is made only after all prisoners have visited at least once), and that incorrect announcements never occur.

Specify the state space (shared room state and per-prisoner private state), the allowed transitions for a visit, and an initial condition that leaves the light possibly unknown. Formalize the safety property: any announcement implies every prisoner has visited. Formalize the liveness property: under the fairness assumption of visits, an announcement will eventually occur. Include the ability to model standard leader-based or counter-based protocols and state invariants that ensure no announcement is made prematurely and that the counter logic cannot be tricked by repeated visits.
