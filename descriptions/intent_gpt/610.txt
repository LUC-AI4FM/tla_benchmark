Model a Tendermint-style Byzantine-fault-tolerant consensus protocol for a fixed set of processes with partially synchronous clocks and proposer-based timestamps. The model should describe rounds of proposal, prevote, and precommit phases driven by proposers per round, where at most T processes can be Byzantine and the rest are correct. The system must allow proposers to attach local-clock timestamps to proposals and must model asynchronous message delivery with a bounded delay and possible clock drift/accuracy/precision bounds between local clocks and a real time reference.

Specify the system goals and safety properties (not the algorithm steps): (1) Agreement on value — no two correct processes may decide different valid values. (2) Agreement on time — if two correct processes decide in the same round, their decided timestamps must be equal. (3) Validity of decided timestamps — any timestamp attached to a decision must be consistent with the modeled clock bounds (within precision/accuracy/delay relations relative to initial clock bounds and proposal receipt times). (4) If the initial proposer is correct and a decision occurs, the decided timestamp must lie between the minimum and maximum initial local clocks. (5) Real-time consistency — decided timestamps must be close to the proposer’s real-time proposal instant and to the first correct receipt instant, within Accuracy/Precision/Delay bounds. (6) Bounded-delay fairness — if a proposal was sent sufficiently earlier than real time, all correct processes will have inspected it. Finally specify a liveness property: once a correct proposer’s timely proposal is delivered and time has advanced beyond Delay, every correct process at or behind that round will eventually receive it and progress toward decision. The model should be parametric in N, T, sets of correct/faulty processes, clock parameters, message delay, and sets of valid/invalid values.
