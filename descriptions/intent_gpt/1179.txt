Specify a concurrent state machine that models a simple two-component system that evolves in discrete steps. The system must maintain two integer-valued state quantities: a small control/state counter that advances through a finite sequence of steps, and a data component that may change according to rules tied to the control counter. The model must allow nondeterministic branching of transitions and explicit initialization to a known starting state.

Requirements:
- Initial state: the control counter is at its start value and the data component is at a specified initial value.
- Transition relation: must include guarded transitions that advance the control counter deterministically along a short progression, while the data component either stays the same, flips between two values, is assigned from a finite domain, or — in an alternative branch — may be computed in a way that could be undefined or error-prone (to exercise evaluation error detection).
- Some transitions should be intentionally partial or leave the data component unspecified to test detection of missing assignments.
- The model should permit transitions that yield a very large or infinite branching factor when the data component is drawn from a large domain (to exercise state-space explosion detection).
- Specify simple safety properties: invariants that must hold for all reachable states (e.g., data always within a given finite range or never equal to a particular forbidden value), and liveness properties such as eventual reaching of a terminal control value.
- Include a property that deliberately may fail or be ill-defined to validate error reporting for invalid expressions or invariant violations.

Do not specify internal encoding choices; focus on the abstract control/data states, permissible transitions, and the correctness properties and failure cases the specification should expose.
