Specify a model of a simple state-transition system that captures nondeterministic choices of variable updates and the potential for reordering of independent assignments. The system should initialize two integer state components to a given value and allow transitions that attempt to assign new values to those components using a mix of direct assignments, existentially quantified choices over a finite set, and simple numeric constraints. Model the semantics so that some constraints may be considered "use-case" or dependent constraints that require certain values to exist before they can be evaluated, while others are pure assertions about chosen values. The model must allow the assignment solver to nondeterministically choose which assignment constraints to treat as binding assignments and in what order to apply them, including the possibility of different consistent orderings when dependencies do not force an order. Define two kinds of transition templates: one in which a particular ordering of assignment and dependent constraints can lead to a successful next-state, and another in which dependency structure forbids reordering and therefore must always fail. Specify correctness properties to check: reachability of postconditions under all nondeterministic solver choices, absence of illegal reorderings that violate dependency constraints, and identification of transitions that can non-deterministically succeed versus those that must always fail. Include invariants that the initial values are respected and that all existential choices are drawn from the finite set.
