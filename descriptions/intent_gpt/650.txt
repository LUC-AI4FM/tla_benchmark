Model a light-client protocol that fetches and verifies compact summaries of a blockchain (light blocks) from a set of validators, some of which may be Byzantine. The model should describe how the client discovers, requests, and verifies light blocks at increasing target heights while operating with an imperfect local clock and within a configured trusting period. The system must permit a configurable fraction of faulty validators and allow clock drift between the client’s local clock and a reference clock.

Specify the client’s allowed behaviors: initiating probes to validators, receiving responses (possibly conflicting or malicious), applying verification rules to accept or reject a candidate light block, and updating its internal trusted state (latest verified height and time). Include the possibility of transient network delays and lost or delayed responses. The model must capture time progression relative to the reference clock and enforce that verification decisions respect the trusting period and clock-drift assumptions.

State correctness properties to be checked: safety (the client never accepts a light block that contradicts an earlier accepted block or violates cryptographic or quorum verification requirements given the assumed faulty ratio), liveness (if a correct validator publishes a valid chain and network delivery eventually succeeds, the client eventually advances to the target height within the trusting period), and freshness (the client’s accepted block timestamps are consistent with the reference clock within allowed drift bounds). Also require that the client detects and reports equivocation or insufficient quorum evidence and does not advance on insufficient or stale evidence.
