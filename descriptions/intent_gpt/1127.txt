Specify a state-machine model of a distributed two-phase commit protocol for a set of participants (resource managers) under asynchronous, nondeterministic execution. The model should describe participant-local decisions (prepare, decide to commit or abort) without prescribing a particular message-passing implementation. It must capture these behaviors:
- Initially every participant is in a neutral pre-commit state.
- A participant may move to a prepared state when it has finished local checks and is willing to commit.
- A participant that is prepared may decide to commit only when it is safe to do so globally; otherwise a participant may decide to abort.
- Participants that have not prepared may also decide to abort under appropriate conditions.

Specify the global conditions that determine when committing is allowed versus when aborting must occur, and ensure all allowed steps are nondeterministic interleavings of individual participant actions.

Require the following correctness properties to be enforced as invariants:
- Type safety: every participant state is one of the four intended states (neutral/working, prepared, committed, aborted).
- Agreement (consistency): no two participants can end up with conflicting final decisions (i.e., one committed and another aborted).
- Liveness-style safety condition: once all participants are prepared, it is possible for participants to reach commit decisions (no global deadlock preventing commit decisions when everyone is prepared).

Do not commit to specific variable naming or concrete action encodings; specify only the states, legal state transitions for a participant, the global enabling conditions, and the invariants to prove.
