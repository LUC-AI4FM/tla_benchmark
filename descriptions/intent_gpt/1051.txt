Specify a formal model of binary relations on a finite universe and the standard relation-theoretic queries and closures that must be guaranteed. The model should treat a relation as a Boolean predicate over ordered pairs drawn from a given finite set. It must provide predicates to determine whether a relation is reflexive, irreflexive, symmetric, asymmetric, and transitive with respect to a designated finite domain.

Additionally, specify operators that compute the transitive closure of a relation over the domain and the reflexive-transitive closure. The transitive-closure operator must be defined so that it yields the least relation containing the original one and closed under composition (i.e., whenever (x,y) and (y,z) are in the closure, (x,z) is in the closure), and it must be computable in a finite number of steps for finite domains. The reflexive-transitive closure must additionally include all identity pairs.

Finally, specify a predicate that tests whether the relation induces a single strongly connected component on the domain (every element can reach every other element through zero or more steps along the relation). State the intended semantics and correctness properties (closure operators are monotone and idempotent and satisfy the usual characterizations) without prescribing a particular implementation strategy.
