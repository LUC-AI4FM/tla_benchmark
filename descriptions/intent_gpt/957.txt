Model the natural numbers as an abstract Peano structure and specify the essential properties and operations needed for reasoning about arithmetic and induction. The model should treat a set of numbers, a distinguished zero element, and a successor function as primitive and must enforce the Peano axioms: zero is a member of the set; the successor is a total injective function from the set to itself; every nonzero element has a unique predecessor (i.e., an element is a successor iff it is not zero); and the set is the smallest set containing zero and closed under successor (induction principle). 

Specify the usual derived notions: the set of natural numbers, equality and inequality, the notion of predecessor where defined, and finite iteration of successor to represent natural-number addition by repeated successor. Define addition and multiplication as total operations on the natural numbers using these primitives (addition as iterated successor; multiplication as repeated addition). Require that addition and multiplication satisfy expected algebraic laws: associativity and commutativity of addition, identity zero for addition, associativity and commutativity of multiplication, distributivity of multiplication over addition, and zero annihilates multiplication.

Specify and prove the induction principle and basic correctness properties: closure of operations within the naturals, injectivity of successor, no predecessor for zero, and that every natural number is reachable from zero by a finite number of successor steps.
