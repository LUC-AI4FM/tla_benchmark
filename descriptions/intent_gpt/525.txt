Specify a formal model of a communication protocol that reliably delivers successive proposed values from a sender to a receiver over an unreliable channel that may drop, duplicate, or reorder messages. The system handles a stream of proposals produced by an environment; each proposal carries a value and a single-bit sequence tag used to detect duplicates and order (an alternating bit). The sender may accept new proposals at any time and must transmit messages to convey the latest accepted proposal and its tag. The receiver must eventually adopt the most recent proposal that was successfully delivered, but must not mistakenly accept an old or duplicate message as a newer update.

Model the externally generated proposals as a bounded queue of values. The protocol must ensure safety properties: the receiver never adopts a different value while the sender and receiver agree on the tag (i.e., when tags are equal, values must be equal), and delivered values must always come from the set of proposed values. Specify liveness properties: every proposal that is accepted by the sender must eventually either be adopted by the receiver or be superseded by a later accepted proposal whose adoption eventually occurs; the receiver should infinitely often become equal to the sender when new proposals stop arriving. Include invariants limiting queue size and type constraints on messages and state. Do not prescribe implementation details such as specific message formats or step interleavings.
