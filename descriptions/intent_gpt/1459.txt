Specify a simple state machine that models a non-decreasing counter with a saturation point and describe its required behaviors and correctness properties.

Model a single integer-valued state that initially equals zero and may increase by one in discrete steps. The system may repeatedly perform an increment step as long as the state is strictly less than a fixed upper threshold of three; once the threshold is reached or exceeded, the state must remain unchanged forever. The model should permit stuttering steps (where the state does not change) at any time, but must prevent any decrement or any increment that would take the state above the threshold.

Formally state the initial condition, the allowed transition relation, and the temporal behavior of executions. Specify liveness and safety properties: safety properties should assert that the state is always a natural number, never decreases, never exceeds the saturation value, and that no illegal jumps occur. A liveness requirement should assert that from the initial state the counter will eventually reach the saturation value (i.e., three) after a finite number of increments. Also provide invariants that prove the safety properties and a proof obligation or model-checkable properties to validate the liveness claim.
