Specify a formal TLA+ model of the Pearson chi-square goodness-of-fit test for discrete categorical data. The model should take as inputs a nonempty sequence of strictly positive expected counts, a sequence of nonnegative observed counts of the same length, and a significance level alpha in (0,1). It must compute the chi-square test statistic as the sum, over categories, of (observed - expected)^2 / expected, and determine the test decision by comparing that statistic to the appropriate critical value from the chi-square distribution with degrees of freedom equal to (number of categories − 1). The specification should include preconditions ensuring expected counts are positive and that total observed count is nonnegative and well defined. It must precisely state the decision rule: reject the null hypothesis that observations follow the expected frequencies if and only if the statistic exceeds the critical value at significance level alpha. The model should also capture properties useful for verification: monotonicity of the statistic with respect to deviations from expectation in a single category; sensitivity to scaling of all expected and observed counts by the same positive factor; and invariance of the decision under permuting categories. Finally, specify correctness properties asserting that the test never rejects when observed equals expected, and that for sufficiently large symmetric deviations the test will eventually reject.
