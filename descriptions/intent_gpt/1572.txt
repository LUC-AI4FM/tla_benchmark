Specify a model of a simple concurrent choice between two alternative sequences of operations that operate on two separate integer counters. The system starts with both counters at zero. At a first decision point, exactly one of two mutually exclusive branches is taken: the left branch performs two successive updates to the first counter (an initial assignment to set it to one, then an increment), while the right branch performs two successive updates to the second counter (initial assignment to one, then an increment). After the chosen branch completes, the system performs a final check that the sum of the two counters equals two; the execution should terminate successfully only when that check holds.

Model should capture non-deterministic branching and sequential steps within a branch, allow interleaving only insofar as the two branches are exclusive (no mixing of the branches' steps), and allow termination. Specify initial states, the allowed step transitions, and fairness or liveness constraints needed to ensure progress to termination when steps remain enabled.

Key correctness properties to guarantee:
- Safety: in every reachable state the counters are nonnegative integers and, at the final check point, the assertion that the sum of counters equals two holds (i.e., executions that reach the final check must only reach it when the invariant holds).
- Liveness: from the initial state the system eventually reaches termination (the final state) under weak fairness for enabled actions.
- Mutual exclusion of branches: no execution may perform steps from both branches; exactly one branch's sequence executes in any run.

Also specify that the model should allow stuttering at termination to avoid deadlock in temporal reasoning.
