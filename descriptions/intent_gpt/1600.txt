Specify a concurrent mutual-exclusion protocol for N identical processes that provides a fast-path attempt and a slower contention-resolution path, and formally verify its safety and liveness goals.

Model N processes repeatedly attempting to enter a single critical section. Each process may first take a fast attempt that can succeed immediately when there is no contention, or it may detect contention and take a slower, deterministic waiting path that inspects other processes’ advertised intent before reattempting. The protocol uses shared flags and a shared “owner” indicator to coordinate access, and processes must be allowed to back off and retry if they detect contention. Processes repeatedly cycle through: express intent, try a fast acquisition, possibly withdraw and wait for a window where others are not indicating interest, and then enter the critical section if successful; on exit they must clear their intent and release any ownership indicator.

Require the following correctness properties to be specified and checked:
- Mutual exclusion (safety): at most one non-aborted process may be inside the critical section at any time.
- No permanent starvation (liveness): from any reachable state, it must always be the case that eventually some process will successfully enter the critical section (global progress).
- Progress under contention detection: if a process observes that it failed a fast attempt, it must be able to complete the slower protocol and either enter the critical section or retry, guaranteeing the system does not deadlock.
- Proper cleanup: on exit, processes must reset their advertised intent and any ownership markers so other processes can make progress.

Also specify reasonable fairness assumptions for individual processes to avoid trivial starvation, and allow verifying the model for parameters N > 1.
