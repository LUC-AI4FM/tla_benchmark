Specify an abstract model of an asynchronous, non‑blocking atomic commitment protocol for N processes that may crash and may be suspected by a failure detector with the following guarantees: the failure detector permanently suspects every crashed process and never suspects a correct one. The model should represent processes that initially propose either YES or NO and then communicate by broadcasting their vote to all others; messages may be lost only due to crashes (i.e., faulty processes may fail to deliver their broadcast to some recipients before crashing). Crashed processes stop taking steps and may have contributed at most a bounded number of undelivered messages of each value.

The model must capture these behaviors and allow nondeterministic interleaving of sends, receives, crashes, and local decisions, while supporting weak fairness for actions of live processes so liveness can be reasoned about. It must track for each process whether it has decided COMMIT, ABORT, or remains active, and count sent and received YES/NO broadcasts in a way that permits reasoning about what a process knows from the messages it has received.

Specify correctness properties to verify:
- Agreement: no two non‑faulty processes decide differently (no one commits while another aborts).
- Abort validity: if any initial proposal is NO, then no process may commit.
- Commit validity: if all processes initially propose YES and no one is suspected, processes do not abort or crash spuriously.
- Termination (liveness): if no process crashes and no one is suspected, every correct process eventually decides (commit or abort); also support checking eventual all‑commit when all votes are YES.

Do not constrain the internal algorithmic steps; model only the external send/receive/crash/suspect/decision behavior and the counts needed to express the above properties.
