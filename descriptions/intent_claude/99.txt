# Specification Request: Unbounded Growth Counter with Forbidden Value

## System Overview

I need a formal specification for a simple counter system that starts with some initial value and grows over time at varying rates depending on its current magnitude.

## Behavioral Requirements

The system should model a counter with the following characteristics:

1. **Initialization**: The counter begins at some positive integer value within a small initial range (roughly 1 to 1000).

2. **Growth dynamics**: The counter can increase (or stay the same) at each step, but the rate of potential growth depends on the current value:
   - When the counter is relatively small, it can grow by a moderate amount per step
   - Once the counter reaches a larger threshold, it can grow by much larger increments per step

3. **Non-deterministic increments**: At each step, the counter may increase by any amount from zero up to some maximum increment (determined by its current size), allowing for exploration of many possible execution paths.

## Correctness Property

The key property to verify is whether the counter can ever reach a specific "forbidden" value during its execution. Specifically:

- **Invariant**: The counter must never equal a particular large value (around 450,000).

The goal is to determine whether this forbidden value is actually reachable given the growth rules, or whether the non-deterministic jumps might always skip over it or never reach it at all.

## Purpose

This specification appears designed to test whether certain values are inevitably reached or can be avoided given discrete, non-deterministic growth with variable step sizes based on thresholds.
