Specify a TLA+ model of the one-step Byzantine consensus algorithm (Algorithm 2 from Dobre and Suri, "One-step consensus with zero-degradation," DSN 2006). The system consists of N processes, of which at most F may be Byzantine (with F ≤ T and N > 3T). Each correct process starts with an initial value of 0 or 1, broadcasts that value, and after receiving at least N−T messages decides: it decides 0 if it received at least N−T "0" messages, decides 1 if it received at least N−T "1" messages, or falls back to its own proposal if neither threshold is met. Byzantine processes may send arbitrary messages and may be activated at any point up to the fault bound F. Message counts for correct and faulty processes are tracked separately. The specification must satisfy weak fairness so that correct processes eventually propose and decide. The key correctness properties are: (1) if all correct processes initially propose 0, no correct process ever decides 1 or enters an undecided state; (2) if all correct processes initially propose 1, they all eventually decide 1 and never enter an undecided state; (3) under all-ones initialization, every process eventually decides 1. Type safety over all counters and process states must also be maintained as an invariant.
