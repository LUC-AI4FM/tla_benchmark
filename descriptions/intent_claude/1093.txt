# Specification Request: DNA Amplification Process (PCR Stages)

## System Overview

I need a formal specification of a simplified model of the Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) process used in molecular biology to amplify DNA. The system should model the key stages of PCR and track the quantities of molecular components as they change through thermal cycling.

## System Description

The model should represent a PCR thermal cycler that processes DNA through distinct temperature-controlled stages:

1. **Heating (Denaturation)**: High temperature causes double-stranded DNA hybrids to separate into single strands, increasing the available template count.

2. **Cooling**: Temperature reduction prepares the system for the next stage (primer binding).

3. **Annealing**: At moderate temperature, primer molecules bind to complementary template strands, forming primer-template hybrids. This consumes both primers and templates while producing hybrids.

4. **Extension**: DNA polymerase extends the primers along templates, synthesizing new DNA strands.

The system should track the quantities of:
- Free primer molecules
- Single-stranded template molecules  
- Primer-template hybrid complexes

## Key Requirements

- The initial quantities of DNA and primer molecules should be configurable as natural number parameters.
- All molecular counts must remain non-negative natural numbers throughout execution.
- During annealing, the number of hybrids formed is limited by the availability of both primers and templates (cannot use more of either than currently exists).
- The system should allow these stages to occur in any sequence, modeling the flexibility of the thermal cycling process.

## Correctness Properties

The primary correctness property to verify is **type safety**: all molecular quantity counters must always remain natural numbers (non-negative integers). This ensures the model never reaches physically impossible states with negative molecule counts.

## Deliverables

1. A base specification defining the state variables, initial conditions, and transition rules for each PCR stage.
2. A formal proof that the type safety invariant holds for all reachable states of the system.
