# Specification Request: Year 2000 Bug in Age Verification System

## System Overview

I need a formal specification of a simplified registration office system that demonstrates the Y2K (Year 2000) bug. The system tracks a person's birth year and determines when they become eligible for a driver's license based on reaching a minimum age.

## System Description

The system should model:

1. **Time progression**: Years advance one at a time from a starting birth year
2. **License issuance**: A driver's license can be issued once a person reaches the required minimum age (e.g., 18 years old)

The critical constraint is that the system stores years using only two digits (00-99), simulating legacy software that was designed before engineers anticipated the year 2000 rollover. When the year reaches 99, it wraps around to 00.

## Parameters

- A birth year (represented as a two-digit number, 0-99)
- A minimum licensing age (between 1 and 99 years)

## Expected Behavior and Bug

The system calculates age by subtracting the birth year from the current year. Under normal circumstances, a license should only be issued to someone who has actually reached the minimum age.

However, due to the two-digit year representation, when the year counter wraps from 99 to 00, the calculated age becomes negative or incorrect. This can lead to situations where someone who legitimately received a license suddenly appears to be underage according to the system's calculations.

## Correctness Properties

The specification should demonstrate:

1. **Type correctness**: The year always remains a valid two-digit number, and the license status is always a boolean value

2. **The flawed safety property**: The "obvious" expectation that anyone holding a license must be at least the minimum age—this property should be *violated* by the specification, illustrating the Y2K bug

The goal is to use this specification to show how the year wraparound causes the safety property to fail, demonstrating the real-world consequences of the Y2K design flaw.
