Specify a mutual exclusion algorithm for N processes (where N is a constant natural number) using shared variables. The algorithm is a fast mutex protocol in the style of Lamport's fast mutual exclusion algorithm. There are N processes competing to enter a critical section, coordinated through two shared integer registers (initially zero) and a shared boolean flag array of size N (initially all false). Each process repeatedly cycles through a non-critical section and then attempts to acquire the lock before entering its critical section.

The acquisition protocol involves each process announcing its interest via its flag, writing its identity into the shared registers in sequence, checking for contention, and potentially waiting or retrying based on the current values of those registers and the flags of other processes. After leaving the critical section, a process clears the shared state it set and resets its flag.

The key safety property is mutual exclusion: no two distinct processes may be in the critical section simultaneously. The liveness property is conditional: if some process is continuously attempting to enter (i.e., not stuck in the non-critical section), then some process must enter the critical section infinitely often. Liveness should be verified under weak fairness of every protocol step for every process.
