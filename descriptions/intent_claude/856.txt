# Specification Request: Prisoner Light Switch Puzzle

## System Overview

I need a formal specification of the classic "100 Prisoners and a Light Bulb" puzzle (or a generalized version with configurable prisoner count). This is a coordination problem where prisoners must collectively determine when all of them have visited an interrogation room, using only a single light bulb as their communication mechanism.

## Problem Description

A group of prisoners are held in separate cells with no way to communicate directly. They are brought one at a time, in some arbitrary order, to an interrogation room that contains a single light bulb with an on/off switch. When a prisoner enters the room, they can observe the current state of the light and may choose to toggle it or leave it unchanged. At any point, any prisoner may announce that they believe all prisoners have visited the room at least once.

The prisoners can agree on a strategy beforehand, but once isolated, the light bulb is their only means of communication.

## Key Requirements

1. **Light Bulb State**: The light can be on or off. There should be a configurable option for whether the initial state of the light is known to the prisoners or unknown.

2. **Prisoner Visits**: Prisoners are selected to visit the room in a non-deterministic order. The same prisoner may visit multiple times before others have visited at all.

3. **Counting Strategy**: The specification should model a counting-based protocol where one designated prisoner (the "counter") tracks visits by other prisoners through the light bulb signal.

4. **Announcement**: Any prisoner may make a final announcement claiming all prisoners have visited. Once announced, the protocol terminates.

## Correctness Properties

1. **Safety**: If a prisoner announces that everyone has visited, then indeed every prisoner must have visited the room at least once. No false announcements should be possible.

2. **Liveness** (optional/desired): Eventually, an announcement will be made—the protocol should guarantee termination under fair scheduling assumptions.

## Configuration

- The set of prisoners should be configurable (e.g., for testing with a small group like three prisoners)
- The initial light state knowledge should be configurable (known vs. unknown)
