# Specification Request: Allocator Refinement Verification

## System Overview

I need a formal specification that demonstrates a refinement relationship between two resource allocator designs. The goal is to prove that a more sophisticated scheduling allocator correctly implements the behavior of a simpler, more abstract allocator specification.

## Context

There are two allocator specifications involved:

1. **Simple Allocator**: A basic, abstract specification of resource allocation that captures the essential safety and liveness properties without concern for implementation details like scheduling order.

2. **Scheduling Allocator**: A more detailed allocator that includes explicit scheduling mechanisms to determine the order in which resource requests are granted.

## Objective

Prove that the scheduling allocator is a valid refinement of the simple allocator. This means every behavior exhibited by the scheduling allocator must be a valid behavior of the simple allocator when viewed at the appropriate level of abstraction.

## Requirements

1. **Refinement Mapping**: Establish that the scheduling allocator's state can be mapped to corresponding states in the simple allocator specification.

2. **Behavioral Inclusion**: Every trace of the scheduling allocator, when projected through the refinement mapping, must satisfy the simple allocator specification.

3. **Property Preservation**: All safety and liveness properties guaranteed by the simple allocator must hold for the scheduling allocator.

## Key Correctness Property

The central theorem to verify is that the scheduling allocator specification logically implies the simple allocator specification. This refinement relationship ensures that:

- The scheduling allocator doesn't introduce any behaviors that would violate the simple allocator's constraints
- The additional scheduling complexity is purely an implementation detail that doesn't compromise correctness
- Clients can reason about the system using the simpler specification while the actual implementation uses the more sophisticated scheduling approach
