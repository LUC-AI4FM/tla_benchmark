# Specification Request: Prisoner Switch Puzzle

## System Description

Model a coordination protocol for the classic "100 Prisoners and a Light Bulb" puzzle (also known as the Two Switches variant).

**Scenario:** A group of prisoners must collectively determine when all of them have visited a special room at least once. The room contains two switches that can each be in one of two positions. A warden repeatedly selects prisoners at random to enter the room, where each prisoner must flip exactly one switch. The prisoners can agree on a strategy beforehand but cannot communicate once the process begins. They cannot observe the room or know who else has visited. At any point, a prisoner may declare that everyone has visited; if correct, all go free—if wrong, all are executed.

**Key constraint:** The initial positions of both switches are unknown to the prisoners.

## Requirements

1. **Room mechanics:** Two binary switches exist in the room. Each visit requires flipping exactly one switch.

2. **Scheduling fairness:** The warden's selection process must guarantee that every prisoner visits the room infinitely often (no prisoner is permanently excluded).

3. **No communication:** Prisoners cannot share information except through the switch positions they observe and modify during their visits.

4. **Unknown initial state:** The protocol must work regardless of the initial switch configuration.

5. **Designated counter:** One prisoner is pre-designated to be responsible for tracking and making the final declaration.

6. **Signal mechanism:** Non-counter prisoners must be able to signal their presence through switch manipulation, with each prisoner allowed to send a limited number of signals (specifically, up to two signals to handle the unknown initial state).

## Correctness Properties

**Safety:** When a prisoner declares that all prisoners have visited the room, this declaration must be true—every prisoner must have actually entered the room at least once.

**Liveness:** Under fair scheduling (every prisoner visits infinitely often), the protocol must eventually reach a state where a prisoner can correctly declare completion, guaranteeing the prisoners' eventual release.
