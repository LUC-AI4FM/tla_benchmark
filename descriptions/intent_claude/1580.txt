# Specification Request: Dining Philosophers with Asymmetric Fork Ordering

## System to Model

Model the classic **Dining Philosophers problem** with N philosophers seated around a circular table, where each philosopher alternates between thinking and eating. Each philosopher needs two forks (shared with their neighbors) to eat.

## Problem Description

N philosophers sit around a table with N forks, one placed between each adjacent pair. To eat, a philosopher must acquire both the fork to their left and the fork to their right. After eating, they release both forks and return to thinking. This cycle repeats indefinitely.

## Solution Strategy

Implement an **asymmetric solution** to prevent deadlock:
- Most philosophers (all except one designated "leader") should acquire their right fork first, then their left fork
- The leader philosopher should acquire forks in the **opposite order**: left fork first, then right fork

This asymmetry breaks the circular wait condition that would otherwise cause deadlock when all philosophers simultaneously grab their right forks.

## Key Requirements

1. **Mutual Exclusion on Forks**: Each fork can only be held by one philosopher at a time (modeled as binary semaphores)

2. **Exclusive Eating**: Two adjacent philosophers cannot eat simultaneously (since they share a fork)

3. **Deadlock Freedom**: The system must never reach a state where all philosophers are blocked waiting for forks

4. **Starvation Freedom**: Every philosopher must eventually get to eat, repeatedly and forever—no philosopher should be indefinitely denied access to their forks

## Correctness Properties

The specification should verify:

1. **Safety (Invariant)**: At no point are two neighboring philosophers eating at the same time

2. **Liveness (Starvation Freedom)**: Every philosopher will eat infinitely often—formally, for each philosopher, it is always the case that they will eventually be eating

## Parameters

- N: The number of philosophers (and forks), where N is a natural number
