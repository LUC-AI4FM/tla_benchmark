# Specification Request: Lamport's Bakery Algorithm

## System Overview

I need a formal specification of Lamport's Bakery Algorithm, a classic solution to the mutual exclusion problem for N concurrent processes. The algorithm is inspired by the ticket-number system used in bakeries: each process that wants to enter its critical section takes a number, and processes enter in order of their numbers (with process ID as a tiebreaker).

## System Requirements

The system consists of N processes, each repeatedly cycling through:
1. A non-critical section (where the process may remain indefinitely)
2. A doorway/entry protocol to acquire a ticket number
3. A waiting phase until it's the process's turn
4. A critical section
5. An exit protocol to release the ticket

### Entry Protocol
When a process wants to enter its critical section, it must:
- Signal that it is in the process of choosing a number
- Observe all other processes' current numbers and select a number strictly greater than any it observed
- Clear the "choosing" signal
- Wait for all other processes, ensuring that:
  - No other process is currently choosing a number
  - Either the other process has no number (not competing), or this process has priority based on lexicographic ordering of (number, process ID)

### Exit Protocol
When a process leaves its critical section, it resets its number to zero.

### Atomicity Constraints
The specification should model a version where individual reads and writes are atomic, but the protocol steps themselves are not—meaning that the act of choosing a number (reading others' values and writing one's own) is not a single atomic operation. The algorithm must remain correct despite this interleaving.

## Correctness Properties

### Safety: Mutual Exclusion
At no point may two distinct processes be in their critical sections simultaneously.

### Liveness: Deadlock Freedom
If at least one process is trying to enter its critical section, then eventually some process will succeed in entering.

### Liveness: Starvation Freedom
Every process that begins trying to enter its critical section will eventually succeed in entering.

## Additional Considerations

The specification should support an arbitrary number of processes N (any natural number). The model should account for fair scheduling—processes that are trying to make progress should eventually be allowed to do so.
