# Specification Request: Bitwise Operations Library

## System to be Modeled

A library of bitwise arithmetic operations for natural numbers, providing standard bit manipulation functions that operate on the binary representation of non-negative integers.

## Required Operations

The library must provide the following bitwise operations:

1. **Bitwise AND**: Computes the logical AND of corresponding bits in two numbers
2. **Bitwise OR**: Computes the logical OR of corresponding bits in two numbers
3. **Bitwise XOR (exclusive or)**: Computes the logical XOR of corresponding bits in two numbers
4. **Bitwise NOT**: Inverts the bits of a number (within the bounds of its binary representation)
5. **Right shift**: Shifts bits to the right by a specified number of positions

## Correctness Properties

The implementation must satisfy the following mathematical properties:

### AND Operation
- AND with zero yields zero
- AND of a number with itself yields the original number
- AND with 1 extracts the least significant bit (parity)

### OR Operation
- Must match the standard bitwise OR semantics for all input pairs

### XOR Operation
- Identity: XOR with zero yields the original number
- Self-inverse: XOR of a number with itself yields zero
- Commutativity: order of operands does not affect the result
- Associativity: grouping of operations does not affect the result
- XOR of consecutive numbers (odd n with n-1) yields 1
- XOR of complementary values within a power-of-two range yields that range's maximum (e.g., n XOR (15-n) = 15 for n in 0..15)

### NOT Operation
- Must flip all bits within the number's binary width
- NOT of any number of the form 2^k - 1 (all ones) yields zero
- NOT of 2^k yields 2^k - 1

### Right Shift
- Right shift by 1 is equivalent to integer division by 2

## Verification Approach

The operations should be validated against pure mathematical reference implementations that compute results bit-by-bit using standard arithmetic (division, modulo, and powers of 2), ensuring the optimized implementations match the mathematically defined behavior across a reasonable range of inputs.
