Specify a TLA+ model of the Coffee Can problem (attributed to Dijkstra and Scholten, described in Gries's *The Science of Programming*). A coffee can begins with some number of black and white beans, up to a configurable maximum total. Repeatedly, two beans are drawn at random: if both are black, both are discarded and one black bean is returned; if both are white, both are discarded and one black bean is added; if they differ in color, the white bean is returned and the black bean is discarded. Each round reduces the total bean count by exactly one, and the process terminates when only one bean remains.

The specification must verify four properties. First, every step strictly decreases the total number of beans. Second, the parity of the white bean count is preserved across every step (the key loop invariant). Third, the process eventually terminates with exactly one bean remaining. Fourth, the terminal color is fully determined by the initial white bean count: if that count is even the final bean is black, and if odd the final bean is white. The model should assume weak fairness so the process cannot stutter indefinitely, and should be model-checked for a concrete maximum bean count such as 100.
