# Specification Request: Dijkstra's Mutual Exclusion Algorithm

## System to Model

I need a formal specification of Dijkstra's original mutual exclusion algorithm from his 1965 paper "Solution of a Problem in Concurrent Programming Control." This was the first published algorithm solving the mutual exclusion problem for N concurrent processes.

## System Description

The system consists of an arbitrary set of concurrent processes, each repeatedly cycling through:
1. Attempting to enter a critical section
2. Executing in the critical section
3. Executing in a non-critical section (where a process may remain indefinitely)

The algorithm uses a contention-based protocol where processes coordinate using shared boolean flags and a single shared "turn" indicator. Processes go through an entry protocol involving checking and updating these shared variables, potentially backing off and retrying when they detect contention from other processes. The order in which a process checks other processes during the entry protocol should be non-deterministic.

## Correctness Properties Required

### Safety Property
- **Mutual Exclusion**: At most one process may be in its critical section at any given time.

### Liveness Properties

- **Deadlock Freedom**: If any process is attempting to enter its critical section, then eventually *some* process (not necessarily the same one) will succeed in entering its critical section. This must hold under the assumption that processes do not halt while competing for entry (though they may remain in their non-critical section forever).

- **Note on Starvation**: The algorithm is known to *not* guarantee starvation freedom—individual processes may wait indefinitely while others repeatedly enter the critical section. The specification should be able to demonstrate this via counterexample.

## Fairness Assumptions

The specification should support checking liveness under weak fairness, but with the important caveat that a process in its non-critical section is not required to ever leave. Fairness should only apply to processes actively trying to enter or executing within the critical section.

## Goal

Produce a formal model that can verify mutual exclusion as an invariant and check the liveness properties described above, demonstrating both what the algorithm guarantees (deadlock freedom) and what it does not (starvation freedom).
