Please write a formal specification for a **one-bit mutual exclusion algorithm** designed to coordinate access to a critical section among multiple concurrent processes.

## System Description

The system consists of a fixed number of processes (for verification purposes, consider 3 processes numbered 0 through 2) that repeatedly attempt to enter a critical section. Each process follows a protocol to request entry, and the algorithm should use minimal shared state—specifically, a one-bit flag per process.

## Requirements

The specification should model:

1. **Multiple concurrent processes** that cycle through phases: wanting to enter the critical section, executing the critical section, and exiting
2. **A synchronization protocol** where processes coordinate using shared one-bit variables to determine who may proceed

## Correctness Properties

The specification must guarantee:

1. **Mutual Exclusion**: At most one process may be in the critical section at any given time. No two distinct processes can simultaneously occupy the critical section.

2. **Deadlock and Livelock Freedom**: Whenever any process is trying to enter the critical section, eventually some process will succeed in entering. The system must make progress and cannot get stuck.

3. **Starvation Freedom** (to be investigated): Examine whether individual processes are guaranteed to eventually enter the critical section after requesting entry. Note that some one-bit algorithms may satisfy mutual exclusion and deadlock freedom but may *not* guarantee starvation freedom for all processes—this is an important property to verify and understand the algorithm's limitations.

## Verification Scope

The model should be configured for a small number of processes to enable exhaustive model checking of these properties, allowing us to discover whether the algorithm provides fairness guarantees or if certain processes can be starved indefinitely.
