Specify a voucher lifecycle system based on RFC 3506, modeling how a fixed set of vouchers transitions through well-defined states from creation to final disposition. Each voucher begins in a pre-issued "phantom" state and, once issued, becomes valid. A valid voucher may be transferred (a no-op that leaves its state unchanged), redeemed, or cancelled. Redemption and cancellation are terminal: once a voucher reaches either of those states it cannot change further. The transition from phantom to valid is strictly one-way and irreversible. The system must guarantee as a safety invariant that voucher states and lifecycle machine states remain consistent with one another at all times: a phantom voucher corresponds to an unstarted lifecycle, a valid voucher corresponds to an in-progress lifecycle, and a redeemed or cancelled voucher corresponds to a completed lifecycle. No other combinations are permitted. The specification need not guarantee that every voucher eventually gets issued or finalized; deadlock freedom is explicitly not required.
