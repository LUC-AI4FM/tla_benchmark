Specify a partially synchronous distributed system implementing an **eventually perfect failure detector** (◇P), as defined by Chandra and Toueg (1996). The system consists of N processes, up to T of which may crash. Each correct process maintains a local clock, a per-peer timeout interval (delta), a suspicion set, and a counter tracking how long since it last heard from each peer.

Correct processes operate in a global-tick model: at every environmental tick at least one correct process must act. Each process cycles through three operations governed by its local clock — periodically broadcasting "alive" heartbeats (at SendPoint-aligned ticks), periodically updating its suspicion set (at PredictPoint-aligned ticks), and otherwise receiving messages — but never performing two of these simultaneously. Crashed processes are removed permanently and their in-transit messages are eventually flushed.

The communication system transports messages with explicit ages. Partial synchrony imposes two constraints: a relative-speed bound (PHI) requiring every correct process to act at least once in any PHI consecutive real-time steps, and a message-delay bound (DELTA) requiring any message addressed to a correct process to be delivered within DELTA steps of any qualifying receive. Local clocks are kept finite by resetting modularly.

The specification must satisfy two liveness properties: **Strong Completeness** — every crashed process is eventually and permanently suspected by every correct process — and **Eventually Strong Accuracy** — there exists a time after which no correct process is ever suspected by any correct process.
