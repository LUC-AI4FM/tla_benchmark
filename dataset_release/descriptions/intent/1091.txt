Write a TLA+ specification modeling the Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) process as a simplified abstract system. The model tracks quantities of double-stranded DNA, single-stranded templates, primers, and template-primer hybrids as they evolve through a temperature-driven cycle. The system begins at a moderate temperature with an initial stock of double-stranded DNA and primers, no templates, and no hybrids. The cycle proceeds through three temperature states: at high heat, double-stranded DNA and hybrids denature into single-stranded templates and free primers are recovered; cooling returns the system to a state ready for annealing; at warm temperature, some nondeterministic number of primers anneal to templates forming hybrids; at medium heat, polymerase extends all hybrids into new double-stranded DNA. The cycle then repeats. The key correctness property is a conservation invariant: the total count of nucleic acid strands, weighted appropriately, must remain constant throughout all transitions — specifically, primers plus templates plus twice the count of double-stranded DNA plus twice the count of hybrids equals the initial primer stock plus twice the initial DNA stock. This preservation property must hold as both an invariant and a stuttering-tolerant temporal property.
