Specify a detailed TLA+ model of the Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) process that tracks the full molecular inventory across repeated thermal cycles. The system begins with a fixed quantity of double-stranded DNA and a supply of primers. Each cycle proceeds through four temperature-driven stages: denaturation (at extreme heat, all double-stranded molecules and hybrids separate into single-stranded templates and free primers are recovered), cooling (preparing for annealing), annealing (at warm temperature, primers bind nondeterministically to available single-stranded templates forming hybrids), and extension (at moderate heat, hybrids are converted into longer double-stranded molecules). The model must distinguish three generations of double-stranded molecule by length — full-length, intermediate, and short — reflecting that true PCR product (the precise short double strand) only accumulates starting from the third cycle onward. The system must satisfy a type-correctness invariant over all molecular counts and stage/cycle bookkeeping. The key safety property is that no exact-length product exists before three cycles have completed. Liveness requires that the system fairly progresses through all stages and that primers are eventually depleted. The model must also refine a simpler abstract specification that tracks only aggregate template, hybrid, and total double-stranded counts through the same stage sequence.
