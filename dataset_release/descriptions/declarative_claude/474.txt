The module specifies the asynchronous Byzantine consensus protocol of Bracha and Toueg (1985), Figure 3, using a counting abstraction over N processes, at most F of which may be Byzantine (with the resilience condition N > 3T ≥ 3F ≥ 0).

Each process has a program counter drawn from {"V0", "V1", "EC", "RD", "AC", "BYZ"}, representing its initial proposal (0 or 1), the ECHO and READY phases, the accepted state, and the Byzantine state. Rather than modeling individual messages, the spec tracks aggregate counts: the total number of ECHO and READY messages sent (nSntE, nSntR), each process's received counts (nRcvdE, nRcvdR), and the current number of Byzantine processes (nByz). A process can become Byzantine (up to F total), receive messages one at a time (bounded by sent plus Byzantine counts), send an ECHO message when its proposal or received-message thresholds are met, send a READY message after echoing and receiving sufficient messages, or decide (move to "AC") after receiving at least 2T+1 READY messages. Three initialization predicates cover mixed proposals, all-zero proposals, and all-one proposals.

The temporal specification Spec (and Spec0 for the all-zero case) pairs the Init condition and the Next-state relation with weak fairness over all non-Byzantine process actions. Three LTL properties are stated: Unforg\_Ltl (if all processes propose 0, no process ever decides), Corr\_Ltl (if all propose 1, some process eventually decides), and Agreement\_Ltl (if any process decides, eventually all non-Byzantine processes decide).
