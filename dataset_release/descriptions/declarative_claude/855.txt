The module specifies a solution to the classic "Prisoners and Switches" puzzle. A warden repeatedly selects prisoners at random to enter a room with two switches (A and B), each of which must be flipped exactly once per visit. One designated prisoner, `Counter`, uses switch A to tally visits; every other prisoner flips switch A up at most twice if it is down, and otherwise toggles switch B. The counter flips switch A down whenever it is up and increments an internal count; otherwise the counter toggles switch B. When `count` reaches `2 * (Cardinality(Prisoner) - 1)`, the counter declares that all prisoners have visited, and the group is freed (`Done`).

The four variables are `switchAUp`, `switchBUp` (boolean switch states), `timesSwitched` (a function from non-counter prisoners to how many times each has raised switch A, bounded at 2), and `count` (the counter's tally, bounded at `2 * Cardinality(Prisoner) - 1`). The initial state allows the switches to start in any position. Fairness requires every prisoner's action to be weakly fair, encoding the warden's guarantee that each prisoner visits infinitely often. The safety property asserts that `Done` can only become true when every non-counter prisoner has flipped switch A at least once (guaranteeing correctness of the declaration), and the liveness property asserts that `Done` is eventually reached. An auxiliary invariant, `CountInvariant`, relates `count` to the total number of upward flips and the current state of switch A, and is used to establish the safety argument. The model is checked with four prisoners for `TypeOK`, `CountInvariant`, `Safety`, and `Liveness`.
