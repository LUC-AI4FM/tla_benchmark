The module specifies a Single Producer, Multi-Consumer (SPMC) Disruptor, a high-performance ring-buffer-based event-passing pattern. One writer thread publishes events into a fixed-size ring buffer while multiple reader threads independently consume every published event in a multicast fashion—each consumer reads every event, not just one.

The state consists of the ring buffer itself (managed by an instantiated `RingBuffer` module), a single `published` write cursor (an integer initialized to −1), per-consumer `read` cursors, per-thread program counters (`Access` or `Advance`), and a history variable `consumed` that records the sequence of values each reader has observed. Production is split into `BeginWrite`/`EndWrite` steps: a writer may only claim the next slot once all consumers are at least one full buffer cycle behind, preventing overwrite of unread data. Consumption is likewise split into `BeginRead`/`EndRead` steps, gated on the published cursor having advanced past the reader's next sequence number. The model is bounded by the `StateConstraint` `published < MaxPublished` (set to 10 in the config), with one writer and three readers over a ring of size 4.

Weak fairness is imposed on both consumer actions to ensure readers make progress. The invariants `TypeOk` and `NoDataRaces` assert well-typed state and absence of concurrent read/write conflicts on any slot. The liveness property `Liveliness` asserts that every consumer eventually and permanently has read every published value in order, with `consumed[r][i+1] = i` confirming that the sequence number itself was stored as the slot's value.
