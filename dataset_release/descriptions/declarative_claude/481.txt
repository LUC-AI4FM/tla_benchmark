The Paxos module specifies the classic Paxos consensus algorithm in a message-passing style, without explicit leader or learner processes. It is instantiated with concrete constants: three acceptors (`{1, 2, 3}`), three majority quorums, two possible values (`{1, 2}`), and ballots drawn from `1..3`.

The four variables are `maxBal`, `maxVBal`, and `maxVal` (per-acceptor state tracking the highest promised ballot, highest voted ballot, and corresponding value) and `msgs` (the monotonically growing set of all messages ever sent). Messages are of four types: 1a (ballot initiation), 1b (promise from acceptor), 2a (value proposal), and 2b (vote by acceptor).

The five key actions implement the two-phase protocol. `Phase1a(b)` broadcasts a phase-1a message for ballot `b`. `Phase1b(a)` has acceptor `a` respond to a 1a message with a higher ballot by promising and reporting its last vote. `Phase2a(b, v)` allows a leader to propose value `v` in ballot `b`, provided no 2a for that ballot exists yet and a quorum of 1b messages demonstrates that `v` is safe (via the quorum-based safe-value condition). `Phase2b(a)` has an acceptor vote for the proposed value if the ballot is at least its current `maxBal`. The overall specification is `Init /\ [][Next]_vars` with no fairness constraints, so only safety is directly enforced.

The module defines a refinement mapping to a `Voting` module through the derived `votes` function, along with auxiliary predicates `VotedFor`, `DidNotVoteAt`, and `ShowsSafeAt`. The inductive invariant `Inv` asserts type correctness, consistency between per-acceptor state and the votes record, and that 1b and 2a messages faithfully reflect protocol history, including uniqueness of proposed values per ballot.
